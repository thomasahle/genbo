Calibrated Routing, Coverage, and Precision-Adaptive Scanning for IVF-PQ}

\begin{abstract}
Scalable approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) search routes a query to a few
partition cells, scans quantized codes inside them, and re-ranks a shortlist.
On \emph{out-of-distribution} (OOD) workloads --- where queries come from a
different distribution than the database, as in cross-modal (text$\to$image)
retrieval under inner product --- we first \emph{localize the bottleneck}: a battery
of scan-side and code-side improvements (wider SIMD, higher-resolution and rotated
codes, RaBitQ, ADC routing) leave OOD recall/QPS essentially unmoved, while the
achievable recall is capped by which cells get \emph{routed to and covered}. We
then present a collection of routing- and coverage-directed techniques, each
measured in isolation: a one-parameter routing recalibration ($\gamma$) grounded in
a cap-versus-ball selectivity argument; $k$-NN-graph coverage augmentation; a
joint cross-level partition optimization; multi-assignment; and a corollary that
\emph{reverses} standard practice --- once graph coverage is present, coarser cells
win. Composed and evaluated under a same-hardware, tight-pairwise protocol against
ScaNN at its official configuration, the engine attains higher QPS at matched
recall@10 at 1M and 10M, extends to 100M --- where no ScaNN configuration builds
within a 2h gate --- and transfers to the streaming track.
We then turn to the \emph{in-distribution} regime (Cohere-768, cosine), where the
same engine initially loses to HNSW by $2\times$ --- and localize \emph{that}
bottleneck too: not the architecture but a \emph{scan-precision policy}. The int8
saturating fast-scan caps each subspace lookup table at ${\sim}4$ bits; its
quantization noise grows as $\sqrt{m}$ with the subspace count, harmless at
$m{=}100$ ($d{=}200$) but fatal to the small cosine margins at $m{=}384$
($d{=}768$), where in-pool ordering collapses to $0.07$. Switching the same codes
to an int16 scan restores ordering to the pool ceiling ($0.87$), and precision
auto-selection by $m$ --- plus the coverage levers above --- brings the engine to
parity-or-better with HNSW in its home regime. We report the negative results as
first-class evidence for both localizations, and treat the measurement
methodology --- which repeatedly caught mirages during development --- as a
contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) search is the retrieval primitive under
retrieval-augmented generation, semantic search, and recommendation. At scale the
dominant family is inverted-file with quantization (IVF-PQ): partition the database
into $C$ cells, at query time \emph{route} to the $p\!\ll\!C$ most promising cells,
\emph{scan} compressed codes within them, and exactly \emph{re-rank} a shortlist. A
large literature has driven each stage --- anisotropic quantization and SIMD
fast-scan for the scan, product and residual codes for memory, graph indices as an
alternative to IVF --- overwhelmingly evaluated where queries and database are drawn
from the \emph{same} distribution.

We study \emph{distribution robustness}: one engine, measured in two regimes that
break different stages of the pipeline. The first is \emph{out-of-distribution}
(OOD) search, where the query distribution differs from the database. This is not
a corner case: cross-modal retrieval embeds a text query and searches image
vectors under inner product, and the big-ANN ``text2image'' track (image base,
text queries, $d{=}200$) is exactly this setting. It is where the strongest
systems separate, and --- we argue --- where the conventional intuition about what
to optimize is wrong. The second is the \emph{in-distribution, high-dimensional}
regime (Cohere-768 embeddings under cosine), the bread-and-butter workload of
graph indices like HNSW --- where the same IVF-PQ engine initially loses by
$2\times$, for a reason that turns out to be neither routing nor architecture but
arithmetic precision in the scan (\S\ref{sec:precision}).

Our organizing claim is a \emph{bottleneck localization}: on OOD workloads the
binding constraint is \emph{routing coverage} --- whether the true neighbor's cell is
among the $p$ probed and its members reachable --- not scan throughput or code
fidelity. We support this from both sides. A battery of scan- and code-side
improvements (wider SIMD, higher-resolution and rotated codes, RaBitQ, ADC routing)
leaves OOD recall and QPS essentially unmoved (\S\ref{sec:neg}); meanwhile a small
set of routing- and coverage-directed techniques each yields a measured gain
(\S\ref{sec:gamma}--\S\ref{sec:soar}). We therefore present the work as a measured
collection --- every technique reported with the effect it produces in isolation,
and the negatives kept as first-class evidence for the localization. Composed and
evaluated under a same-hardware, tight-pairwise protocol against ScaNN at its best
configuration, the result attains higher QPS at matched recall@10 at 1M and 10M
(and, where ScaNN cannot build within a 2h gate, remains the only method to index 100M),
and transfers to the streaming track.

\paragraph{Contributions (each measured in isolation).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A bottleneck localization} (\S\ref{sec:localize}): scan- and
    code-side levers do not move OOD; routing/coverage does. The negatives
    (\S\ref{sec:neg}) are the evidence.
  \item \textbf{$\gamma$ routing recalibration} (\S\ref{sec:gamma}), grounded in a
    cap-vs-ball selectivity argument (\S\ref{sec:theory}). \emph{Effect: $\approx$
    half the probes at matched OOD recall, zero query-time cost.}
  \item \textbf{$k$-NN-graph coverage} (\S\ref{sec:graph}). \emph{Effect: composes
    with $\gamma$; at 1M moves the ScaNN ratio $1.18\to0.98$.}
  \item \textbf{The coarse-cell corollary} (\S\ref{sec:coarse}). \emph{Effect: with
    graph coverage present, coarser cells win; at 10M $1.01\to0.85$.}
  \item \textbf{Joint cross-level partition optimization (tree-EM)}
    (\S\ref{sec:em}). \emph{Effect: $+0.6$--$1.0$ recall@10 points at 10M, QPS-neutral.}
  \item \textbf{Multi-assignment (SOAR)} (\S\ref{sec:soar}). \emph{Effect: raises the
    routing recall ceiling to baseline parity.}
  \item \textbf{Scan precision as a function of code length}
    (\S\ref{sec:precision}): the int8-saturating fast-scan's per-subspace
    ${\sim}4$-bit LUT cap injects noise growing as $\sqrt{m}$; at $m{=}384$
    (in-distribution $d{=}768$ cosine) it collapses in-pool ordering to $0.07$
    while the \emph{same codes} under an int16 scan rank to the pool ceiling
    ($0.87$). \emph{Effect: with precision auto-selected by $m$ (plus an AVX-512
    int16 kernel and graph coverage), the engine goes from $2\times$ behind HNSW
    on Cohere-768 to parity-or-better --- without abandoning the IVF-PQ cascade.}
  \item \textbf{Systems levers for streaming} (\S\ref{sec:stream}): batch-parallel
    insert (\emph{$4.5\times$}), low-live adaptive $p$ (\emph{worst step
    $0.74\to0.94$}), and a parallelization fix (\emph{$7.6\times$ at 8 threads}).
  \item \textbf{A same-hardware evaluation methodology} (\S\ref{sec:method}) and an
    honest scope of what the ratios do and do not claim.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background: Standard Building Blocks}
\label{sec:bg}
Our system is assembled from well-established components; we summarize them here so
the contributions in \S\ref{sec:overview}--\S\ref{sec:soar} can be stated as deltas
against known practice.

\paragraph{Inverted file (IVF).} Partition the database into $C$ cells (the Voronoi
regions of $C$ centroids). At query time, \emph{route} the query to its $p\!\ll\!C$
nearest centroids and scan only those cells. End-to-end recall is capped by whether
the true neighbor's cell is among the $p$ probed --- the routing question that this
paper argues is the OOD bottleneck.

\paragraph{Hierarchical routing.} Scoring all $C$ centroids per query is $O(Cd)$,
which dominates at scale (fine $C$ is needed to keep cells small). A \emph{tree} of
centroids --- coarse $k$-means over $C_0$ centroids, then fine $k$-means within each
--- reduces routing to $O(L\,C^{1/L})$ for an $L$-level tree (FAISS uses an
HNSW graph over the centroids for the same purpose). We use hierarchical $k$-means.

\paragraph{Product quantization (PQ).} Split each vector's $d$ dimensions into $m$
subspaces and quantize each subspace to a small codebook ($16$ codewords for
$4$-bit PQ). Approximate distances are computed by lookup-and-add over per-subspace
tables (ADC). With $4$-bit codes the table lookup is a register shuffle
(\texttt{vpshufb}), enabling the SIMD ``fast-scan'' kernels (Quick-ADC, ScaNN, FAISS
FastScan). \emph{Anisotropic} / score-aware quantization (ScaNN) weights the
quantization loss to preserve inner-product ranking rather than reconstruction.

\paragraph{Exact re-ranking.} The quantized scan is used only to select a shortlist;
exact distances (int8 or float, read for the few shortlisted vectors) re-order it.
The shortlist depth $t$ trades recall for cost.

\paragraph{Multi-assignment.} Replicating each point into its top-$a_0$ cells (a
space/time tradeoff) raises the chance a query's neighbor lands in a probed cell.

\paragraph{The OOD workload.} The big-ANN OOD track (text2image) pairs
\emph{image}-embedding base vectors ($d{=}200$) with \emph{text}-embedding queries
under inner product, with a float ground truth. Base norms are concentrated but not
constant ($\approx0.81$--$0.99$); queries are unit-norm. This distribution gap
between base and queries is what breaks $L_2$-trained routing (\S\ref{sec:gamma}).

\paragraph{Build path.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Quantize.} Float base $\to$ int8 via a single global scale
    ($127/\max|x|$); the int8 base is the native scan/route representation.
  \item \textbf{Train the routing tree.} Hierarchical $k$-means: $C_0$ coarse
    centroids, then fine centroids within each coarse cell to $K_f$ leaves total
    (2-level; generalizes to $L$ levels).
  \item \textbf{Joint refinement (tree-EM, \S\ref{sec:em}).} Optionally re-optimize
    all levels jointly (E: reassign sample points to their nearest leaf via the beam
    descent; M: recompute every level's centroids).
  \item \textbf{Multi-assign (SOAR, \S\ref{sec:soar}).} Assign each point to $a_0$
    leaves with spill that covers the residual direction of the first.
  \item \textbf{Encode.} $4$-bit anisotropic PQ ($m{=}d/2$ subspaces); pack codes
    into cell-contiguous $16$-row blocks laid out for the \texttt{vpshufb} fast-scan.
    Store the raw int8 vectors in cell order too (cache-warm re-rank gathers).
  \item \textbf{Calibrate \& augment.} Precompute the per-cell $\gamma$ routing bias
    (\S\ref{sec:gamma}); build the base-side $k$-NN graph sidecar (\S\ref{sec:graph}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Query path.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Route (tree descent).} Score the $C_0$ coarse centroids (int8, VNNI
    kernel), keep the top-$b_0$; expand to their fine children, score those with the
    $\gamma$ bias added, and return the top-$p$ leaves.
  \item \textbf{Scan.} Fast-scan the $4$-bit PQ codes of the $p$ probed cells
    (\texttt{vpshufb} ADC with int16 accumulation for $\sim$12-bit selection
    resolution; optional AVX-512 64-wide), collecting a bounded top-$t$ pool.
  \item \textbf{Cascade re-rank.} Deduplicate the pool by original id (needed under
    multi-assignment); exact-rescore the $t$ survivors in int8 (VNNI dot); prune to
    the top-$K$ ($K{=}16$).
  \item \textbf{Float re-rank + graph union.} Compute exact float inner products for
    the $K$ survivors (reading the original float vectors) unioned with the $k$-NN
    graph neighbors of the top-$M$; return the top-10.
\end{enumerate}

Algorithms~\ref{alg:build}--\ref{alg:query} state the pipeline precisely. All
routing scores use the calibrated form $s_\gamma(q,c)=\gamma\lVert c\rVert^2-2\,
q\!\cdot\!c$ (\S\ref{sec:gamma}); the constant $\lVert q\rVert^2$ is dropped, and
smaller $s_\gamma$ means a better cell. Leaf centroids are $\{c_j\}_{j=1}^{K_f}$
with precomputed bias $b_j=(\gamma-1)\lVert c_j\rVert^2$ so that
$s_\gamma(q,c_j)=\lVert c_j\rVert^2-2q\!\cdot\!c_j+b_j$ is one dot product plus an
additive constant.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Route (tree descent) \textendash{} returns the $p$ best leaves}
\label{alg:route}
\Proc{\Route{$q$, $p$}}{
  $S_0 \gets$ top-$b_0$ coarse cells minimizing $s_\gamma(q,Q_0)$
    \tcp*{int8/VNNI kernel}
  $\mathcal{L}\gets$ fine children of $S_0$\;
  score each $c_j\!\in\!\mathcal{L}$ by $\lVert c_j\rVert^2-2q\!\cdot\!c_j+b_j$\;
  \Return the $p$ leaves of $\mathcal{L}$ with smallest score\;
}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Query}
\label{alg:query}
\KwIn{int8 query $q$, float query $q_f$; probes $p$; pool depth $t$; prune $K$;
  graph fan-in $M$}
$\mathcal{C}\gets$ \Route{$q,p$}\;
$\mathcal{P}\gets\emptyset$ \tcp*{bounded top-$t$ pool}
\ForEach{cell $\in\mathcal{C}$}{
  fast-scan its 4-bit PQ codes (\texttt{vpshufb} ADC, int16 accum) $\to$ approx.\ scores\;
  keep the $t$ best in $\mathcal{P}$ (threshold-bounded collect)\;
}
$\mathcal{P}\gets$ dedup $\mathcal{P}$ by original id \tcp*{needed under $a_0\!>\!1$}
$\mathcal{K}\gets$ top-$K$ of $\mathcal{P}$ by exact int8 IP (VNNI dot) \tcp*{cascade prune}
$\mathcal{R}\gets \mathcal{K}\cup\bigcup_{r\in\text{top-}M(\mathcal{K})} G[r]$
  \tcp*{$k$-NN-graph coverage union}
\Return top-10 of $\mathcal{R}$ by exact float IP $\langle q_f, x_r\rangle$\;
\end{algorithm}

\section{Localizing the OOD Bottleneck}
\label{sec:localize}
We separate two ceilings. The \emph{pool-recall ceiling} is the recall obtainable by
exactly re-ranking the \emph{entire} probed pool --- it depends only on routing
(which cells, hence which points, are reachable), not on the scan's approximation.
The \emph{scan-limited recall} is what the actual (quantized) scan plus shortlist
delivers. If the two coincide, the scan is already extracting everything the routed
pool contains and there is no scan headroom; recall is then a pure function of
routing coverage.

On OOD text2image this is what we observe: at a fixed probe budget the scan-limited
recall sits at the pool-recall ceiling, and that ceiling --- not the scan --- is what
moves when we change routing. Two consequences follow, and the rest of the paper is
their systematic exploration. First, techniques that improve \emph{coverage} (route
calibration, graph augmentation, joint partition optimization, multi-assignment)
should raise recall; \S\ref{sec:gamma}--\S\ref{sec:soar} confirm each does, with a
measured effect. Second, techniques that only improve the \emph{scan or the code}
(wider SIMD, higher-resolution or rotated codes, alternative quantizers, ADC routing)
should not help, because there is no scan headroom to recover; \S\ref{sec:neg}
confirms each does not. The negatives are thus not incidental --- they are the
control that localizes the bottleneck to routing coverage.

\section{$\gamma$: One-Parameter Routing Recalibration}
\label{sec:gamma}
An IVF router ranks cells by a score over the query $q$ and centroid $c$. For $L_2$
$k$-means the score is $\lVert q-c\rVert^2 = \lVert q\rVert^2 + \lVert c\rVert^2 -
2\,q\!\cdot\!c$; dropping the query-constant term leaves
$\lVert c\rVert^2 - 2\,q\!\cdot\!c$. We write the family
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gamma}
s_\gamma(q,c) \;=\; \gamma\lVert c\rVert^2 - 2\,q\!\cdot\!c ,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma{=}1$ recovers $L_2$ routing and $\gamma{\to}0$ recovers pure
inner-product (angular) routing. Under distribution shift these disagree in a
specific way: the $\lVert c\rVert^2$ penalty demotes high-norm centroids, but for
OOD queries the inner-product-relevant neighbors concentrate precisely near those
high-norm cells, so $\gamma{=}1$ systematically misroutes them. A single global
$\gamma{<}1$ rebalances the two terms. It is grounded in the cap-vs-ball argument of
\S\ref{sec:theory}: $\gamma$ slides routing along the additive-ball ($\gamma{=}1$,
non-selective under concentration) to spherical-cap ($\gamma{\to}0$, selective) axis.

Crucially the fix is \emph{free at query time}: $s_\gamma$ differs from the $L_2$
score only by the per-cell constant $(\gamma{-}1)\lVert c\rVert^2$, which we fold
into a precomputed integer bias added during cell scoring (no extra query work, no
index-format change). We fit $\gamma$ once per dataset; $\gamma\!\approx\!0.5$ is
optimal on text2image at both 1M and 10M, and the recall is flat across
$\gamma\in\{0.4,\dots,0.6\}$ near the optimum.
\effect{Reaches matched OOD recall at $\approx$half the probes ($p$: 54$\to$29 at
1M; $\approx$2.5$\times$ probe cut at 10M). Zero query-time cost.}

\section{$k$-NN-Graph Coverage Augmentation}
\label{sec:graph}
Routing to $p$ cells can still miss a true neighbor whose cell went unprobed. We
recover such misses with a graph, without probing more cells. Offline we build a
base-side inner-product $k$-NN graph (a per-point adjacency list; constructed by
ScaNN self-search or by the engine querying itself --- the two agree on $\sim$92\% of
edges). The graph is a method-agnostic offline artifact, so the query-time comparison is
unaffected by which builder is used; where the source matters for a claim we say so ---
e.g.\ the $35$M in-distribution result (Table~\ref{tab:wiki35m}) uses the engine's
\emph{own} self-built graph and still wins, so that comparison borrows nothing from the
opponent (the self-built graph trails a competitor-built one by only ${\sim}0.4$pt recall).
At query time, after the scan yields a shortlist, we take the top-$M$
shortlist members and \emph{union their graph neighbors into the exact re-rank set}.
The intuition is transitive: a shortlisted point near the query is itself near the
true neighbor, so the true neighbor tends to appear among that point's stored nearest
neighbors even when its own cell was not probed. This adds coverage that is
\emph{independent of the partition} --- the property that drives the coarse-cell
corollary (\S\ref{sec:coarse}). It composes with $\gamma$: the two attack different
misses (calibration fixes which cells are ranked highly; the graph reaches beyond the
probed cells), overlapping only partially. The union is deduplicated against the
scanned pool (already-scanned neighbors are not re-scored) and the adjacency reads are
software-prefetched, so the added cost is a small, bounded re-rank increment.
\effect{At 1M, composed with $\gamma$, the ScaNN ratio drops $1.18\to0.98$
(the first sub-1 result); at 10M it lets $t_{\text{surv}}$ halve at matched recall.
Graph parameters scale with $n$: fan-in $M$ and pool depth grow from $M{\approx}25$
at 1M to $M{=}64$ at 100M as distractors multiply.}

\paragraph{Repair depth (and where it helps).} The union above is one hop. Iterating
it --- expand the current best-$M$ members' neighbors, re-score, reselect, for $R$
rounds --- is a \emph{batched} graph walk (beam width $M$; the whole cohort is
SIMD-rescored and a single exact re-rank closes it, so no per-query priority queue).
It has a clean cost/quality model: each hop closes a geometric fraction
($\approx\!56\%$) of the still-repairable miss mass, while $p$ and $t_{\text{surv}}$
raise a per-probe reachability \emph{ceiling} that hops cannot exceed (fit to
$R^2{=}0.99$ on a 20-point grid, so the two knobs tune independently, no black-box
search). Crucially, extra hops are a matched-recall win \emph{only above a
$\approx\!0.91$--$0.92$ crossover}: at the leaderboard's recall@10$\ge\!0.90$ gate the
single-hop point is $\approx\!6\%$ faster (a second hop reaches 0.90 only by
\emph{overshooting} recall at a QPS cost), whereas for high-recall targets
($0.94$--$0.96$) a second or third hop reaches them at far lower $p$ than one hop. We
therefore keep hop count a knob (default $1$), not a default --- a high-recall lever,
not a gate-level one. A best-first frontier (expand the globally-best unexpanded node)
adds $+0.3$--$0.8$ pt \emph{in-distribution} but is neutral OOD: base-metric edges do
not align with off-manifold queries, so smarter reselection finds no better seeds.
Reporting this required comparing at the recall \emph{gate}, not fixed $p$ --- a
fixed-$p$ ``$+1.7$ pt'' reads as a win but is not one at the gate.

\section{Routing Cost and the Coarse-Cell Corollary}
\label{sec:coarse}

\subsection{A routing cost model}
\label{sec:routecost}
Consider an $L$-level tree over $K_f$ leaves with cell counts
$C_0 < C_1 < \dots < C_{L-1} < C_L = K_f$ and per-level beam widths
$b_0,\dots,b_{L-2}$ (the number of cells expanded when descending from level $i$ to
level $i{+}1$). Routing a query costs, in centroid distance evaluations,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:route}
\mathrm{route}(q) \;=\; C_0 \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{L-1} b_{i-1}\,\frac{C_i}{C_{i-1}},
\end{equation}
since the coarse level scores all $C_0$ centroids and level $i$ expands the $b_{i-1}$
best parents from level $i{-}1$, each contributing $C_i/C_{i-1}$ children. The scan
then reads $\mathrm{scan}(q)=p\cdot(n/K_f)\cdot a_0$ candidates ($p$ probed leaves
$\times$ points/leaf $\times$ multi-assignment). For a 2-level tree \eqref{eq:route}
is $C_0 + b_0 K_f/C_0$, minimized at $C_0=\sqrt{b_0 K_f}$ to give
$2\sqrt{b_0 K_f}=O(\sqrt{K_f})$; with $L$ levels and balanced branching this falls to
$O(L\,K_f^{1/L})$. The two costs pull oppositely in $K_f$: finer leaves shrink the scan
($n/K_f$) but grow the routing. The beams $b_i$ are the ``routing aggression'' knob ---
wider beams retain the true cell with higher probability at linear cost --- and OOD
queries, which route poorly, need wider beams (\S\ref{sec:eval}).

Table~\ref{tab:geom} gives geometry and routing cost by scale. Two levels suffice
through 10M; at 100M a 2-level tree at its optimal $C_0=\sqrt{b_0 K_f}\approx16{,}400$
would cost $\approx\!33{,}000$ evals, whereas the 3-level tree roughly halves this
($14{,}336$) --- so the third level is introduced only at 100M, where routing is a
large enough share of the query to matter.

\subsection{Scaling laws for geometry}
\label{sec:scaling}
To set geometry at scale without per-$n$ retuning, we sweep $K_f$ at
$n\in\{10^5,3{\cdot}10^5,10^6,3{\cdot}10^6,10^7\}$ (2-level, graph off), at each $n$
finding the minimum $p$ that reaches recall@10 $\ge 0.90$ (deterministic, hence
load-independent) and the resulting cost $\mathrm{route}+\mathrm{scan}$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:route}. The cost-minimizing geometry follows clean power laws (log-log
fit):
\[
K_f^\star \sim n^{0.66},\qquad
\tfrac{n}{K_f^\star}\,(\text{pts/leaf}) \sim n^{0.34},\qquad
p^\star \sim n^{0.18},\qquad
C_0^\star \sim n^{0.33}.
\]
The central law is $\text{pts/leaf}\sim n^{1/3}$: optimal leaves \emph{grow} with $n$,
because routing $O(\sqrt{K_f})$ rises while the scan $O(p\,n/K_f)$ is held down by the
growing $K_f$ --- the coarse-cell corollary expressed as a scaling law. Probes grow
only as $n^{1/5}$. Extrapolating, $n{=}10^9$ calls for $K_f\!\approx\!10^6$
($\sim\!1000$ pts/leaf). These calibrate the 2-level baseline; graph coverage
(\S\ref{sec:graph}) shifts the optimum coarser and a third routing level (cheaper,
$O(K_f^{1/3})$) shifts it finer, so the 100M geometry is validated by direct A/B
(\S\ref{sec:eval}) rather than read off the 2-level law.

The cost model \eqref{eq:route} explains \emph{why}. Without graph coverage, recall
demands fine $K_f$ (small leaves make the true neighbor's leaf likelier to be among
the $p$ probed), paying $O(\sqrt{K_f})$ routing. The $k$-NN-graph union
(\S\ref{sec:graph}) supplies that coverage \emph{independently of $K_f$}, so the tree
can use \emph{coarser} leaves --- cheaper routing --- at fixed recall. The optimum is a
knee: too fine wastes routing, too coarse inflates the scan ($p\cdot n/K_f$).
Empirically the knee sits near 150 points/leaf at 10M, and both finer and coarser
builds lose to it.

\section{Joint Cross-Level Partition Optimization (Tree-EM)}
\label{sec:em}
Hierarchical $k$-means trains each level greedily top-down, so a leaf centroid is
optimized for its parent's Voronoi cell, not for the query-time beam descent that
actually reaches it. We instead optimize all levels jointly by EM against the
search objective: the E-step assigns each sample point to the leaf its \emph{beam
descent} lands in (not its globally-nearest leaf), and the M-step recomputes every
level's centroids from those assignments. Iterating co-adapts the levels so the
descent lands better. The E-step is itself an ANN query against the tree's own
centroids, so it runs at a shallow beam (a few probes, not the full query beam),
making EM cheap; it is a build-time cost only and adds nothing at query time. It
composes with $\gamma$ and the graph (it improves the partition; they improve
routing and coverage on top).
\effect{$+0.6$--$1.0$ recall@10 points at 10M at matched probe, QPS-neutral
(build-time only).}

\section{Multi-Assignment (SOAR)}
\label{sec:soar}
The remaining coverage lever is redundancy: assign each point to its top-$a_0$ cells
rather than one, so a query reaching any of them finds the point. Naive replication
puts near-duplicate copies in adjacent cells; SOAR instead chooses the extra cells to
cover the \emph{residual direction} of the first assignment, so the copies span
different query directions and buy more coverage per unit storage. Realizing the gain
required a correctness fix: the exact-re-rank shortlist must be deduplicated by
original id \emph{before} the survivor cap, otherwise the $a_0$ duplicate copies of a
point crowd the fixed-size shortlist and starve distinct ids (recall then falls, and
paradoxically decreases with more probes --- the signature of the bug). With the fix,
multi-assignment raises the routing-coverage ceiling.
\effect{$a_0{=}2$ reaches OOD coverage parity with the baseline partition; higher
$a_0$ extends the high-recall frontier at a storage/scan cost (quantified in table).}

\section{Scan Precision Must Scale with Code Length}
\label{sec:precision}
The techniques so far repair \emph{routing and coverage}, the OOD bottleneck. The
in-distribution, high-$d$ regime breaks a different stage, and localizing it is a
second instance of the paper's method: measure stages in isolation until the
failing one confesses.

\paragraph{The symptom.} On Cohere-768 (10M, cosine, unit-norm) the engine needed
an exact-re-rank pool of $t{\approx}32{,}000$ to reach recall $0.90$ ---
$60\times$ the OOD operating depth --- and recall \emph{decreased} with more
probes. Stage-isolation pinned it: the routed pool \emph{contains} the true
neighbor (pool ceiling $0.87$--$0.94$), and an 8-bit re-ranker recovers exactly
the ceiling, but the 4-bit fast-scan's \emph{own ordering} of the pool is
catastrophic --- the true neighbor almost never ranks in its top-$100$ (in-pool
ordering recall $0.07$).

\paragraph{The cause is arithmetic, not codes.} The SIMD fast-scan
accumulates lookup-table (LUT) values in \emph{saturating int8}; to avoid
saturation within a hoist group, each subspace's LUT is quantized to a
${\sim}4$-bit range (cap $\lfloor 120/\text{HOIST}\rfloor{=}15$). The resulting
per-subspace quantization noise is independent across subspaces, so the total
scan-score noise grows as $\sqrt{m}$ while the useful margin between neighbors
does not. At $d{=}200$ ($m{=}100$ subspaces, wide OOD margins) this is harmless
--- which is exactly why the int8 path is the right champion default there. At
$d{=}768$ ($m{=}384$, small in-distribution cosine margins) the noise swamps the
signal. The discriminating experiment holds the \emph{index and codes fixed} and
changes only the scan arithmetic: int8-saturating scan orders the pool at $0.07$;
an int16-LUT scan orders it at $0.87$ --- the pool ceiling --- at rank depth 100.

\paragraph{The fix is a policy, not a rewrite.} Scan precision is selected by
subspace count: $m\!\le\!128$ keeps the int8 fast-scan (bit-identical to the OOD
champion path), $m\!>\!128$ uses int16 LUTs. Two systems consequences follow.
First, the survivor cut becomes trustworthy, so the re-rank pool collapses from
$32{,}000$ back to a few hundred. Second, the int16 kernel is worth engineering:
a 32-wide AVX-512 (\texttt{vpermw}) int16 pair-scan --- with its dispatch hoisted
out of the per-block hot loop, where a per-call environment lookup had been
costing ${\sim}25\%$ of the scan --- is compute-bound at $m{=}384$
(sorted-vs-scattered block order $= 1.00\times$), recall-bit-identical, and
$+31\%$ end-to-end. Notably this \emph{reverses} the wider-SIMD negative of
\S\ref{sec:neg}: at $m{=}100$ the scan is memory-bound and AVX-512 buys
$\le\!7\%$; at $m{=}384$ it is LUT-compute-bound and the same width is decisive.
Precision and width are functions of $m$, not constants.

\paragraph{Composition.} With precision fixed, the OOD coverage levers apply
unchanged in-distribution: $k$-NN-graph augmentation (edges from a base
self-search) cuts probes ${\sim}3\times$ at matched recall, and a
$\text{dpb}{=}4$ recode ($m{=}192$: half the LUT work per candidate) trades
$0.7$ recall points for ${\sim}1.3\times$ QPS, net-positive on the frontier.
\effect{Cohere-768 1M, 1 thread: $0.9197$@$1957$ QPS graph-off,
$0.9220$@$3229$ with graph$+$dpb4 --- vs.\ HNSW $0.9122$@$1840$. At 10M the
composed stack \emph{beats HNSW in its home regime}: 5-round tight-pairwise
median ratio $1.098$ at $+0.2$pt recall (Table~\ref{tab:cohere}).}

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
We report \emph{same-hardware ratios}, never cross-machine QPS. All comparisons run
on one physical machine; each competitor is measured in the same session, seconds
apart, at its own best operating point.

\paragraph{Protocol.} For a target recall (recall@10 $\ge 0.90$), we (i) fix each
system's operating point by an independent sweep, requiring both to clear the recall
gate against the \emph{same} exact float ground truth; (ii) alternate the two systems
round to round (order flips) so neither owns a load window; (iii) take best-of-$k$
within a round and report the \emph{median of per-round ratios}; (iv) recompute
recall from raw ids, never trusting a cached number. On a shared machine, load drift
between two builds minutes apart exceeds the effects being measured, so only
interleaved same-window ratios and load-independent recall are trusted.

\paragraph{Warm-resident semantics; pristine confirmation.} Both systems measured
warm and resident (leaderboard serving); an early cold-spawn asymmetry is corrected
by scoring each engine's warm block. Headline numbers pause all other first-party
jobs (SIGSTOP) and confirm across $\ge$20 rounds.

\paragraph{The baseline at its best.} At 10M, where an \emph{official} big-ANN recipe
exists, ScaNN uses it verbatim (40k leaves, anisotropic AH/LUT16, residual quant,
bfloat16 re-order, top-level partitioner) with its own leaves-to-search / re-order
sweep including corner configs. At scales with no official recipe (1M, 100M) we
instead give ScaNN its \emph{speed-optimal} configuration, found by sweeping the leaf
count: e.g.\ at 1M, ScaNN peaks at 1200 leaves (6.2k QPS@0.90), with both finer and
coarser partitions slower --- coarser because routing over more leaves outweighs the
finer scan, mirroring our own coarse-cell result (\S\ref{sec:coarse}). This matters:
a convention-chosen leaf count can silently under- or over-partition the baseline. A
win depending on a handicapped baseline is quarantined; we describe one (an
under-leaved cached 10M index) and its correction.

\paragraph{Build-time eligibility.} The benchmark caps index build at \textbf{12
hours} on the 8-vCPU evaluation machine, so a baseline (or our own index) that cannot
build in time is not merely slow --- it is ineligible. This is a real constraint at
100M: a $\sqrt{n}$-scaled 126k-leaf ScaNN would take $\approx$32h at 8 vCPU (its
partitioner $k$-means over the training sample), so the eligible ScaNN baseline is a
coarser config that builds in time. Our indices build comfortably inside the limit and
\emph{faster than ScaNN at scale}: on 16 cores, 1M in $131$s, 10M in $27$min, 100M in
$86$min ($\approx\!3$h at 8 vCPU), versus ScaNN's $\approx\!1.5$h (10M) and $\approx\!2$h
(100M, eligible config). We therefore report build time alongside QPS
(Table~\ref{tab:main}); the hierarchical-$k$-means$+$PQ build is cheaper than ScaNN's
partitioner-$k$-means$+$AH training, and the gap widens with $n$.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} Same-hardware ratios on one (shared) machine, not a
standardized-VM submission; ScaNN is the \emph{measured} opponent --- gaps to
binary-only leaderboard systems are \emph{inference} from the public leaderboard.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:rel}
\textbf{Quantization-side IVF.} ScaNN introduced anisotropic vector quantization
(score-aware loss) and remains the measured opponent here at its official
configurations; FAISS provides the reference IVF-PQ/fast-scan implementations
(and \S\ref{sec:neg} documents an IP-fairness pitfall in its residual default).
RaBitQ represents the recent binary-ish code family; at matched bytes it showed
no ranking edge in our OOD setting. Our contribution on this side is not a new
code but the observation that \emph{scan arithmetic precision must scale with
code length} (\S\ref{sec:precision}).
\textbf{Graph indices.} HNSW is the standard in-distribution baseline and is
measured here at both regimes: dominant-until-matched in-distribution, and
$>\!4\times$ behind on OOD at matched recall. RoarGraph (VLDB'24) is the
OOD-specific graph method --- a query-aware bipartite projection built from
sampled training queries; we build it with its published hyperparameters and
measure it on the same box (Table~\ref{tab:t2i-baselines}). DiskANN/Vamana
target the disk regime we do not evaluate.
\textbf{OOD-specific routing.} Query-aware cell \emph{training} fails in our
hands (\S\ref{sec:neg}); RoarGraph solves coverage with a query-aware graph;
our $\gamma$ recalibration (\S\ref{sec:gamma}) instead keeps base-trained cells
and corrects the \emph{scoring} of them, at zero query-time cost, composing with
graph coverage rather than replacing the IVF skeleton.
\textbf{Streaming.} The big-ANN streaming track constraints (1h wall, 8GB) shape
\S\ref{sec:stream}; our levers there are systems-level (batch-parallel insert,
adaptive probes) rather than index-structural.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:lim}
\textbf{In-distribution scaling to high dimension.} The scan-precision fix
(\S\ref{sec:precision}) makes the engine competitive-to-winning in-distribution at
$10$M$/d{=}768$ (Cohere, Table~\ref{tab:cohere}); with multi-hop graph repair
(\S\ref{sec:graph}) this extends to $35$M$/d{=}1024$ (Wikipedia Cohere-v3, unit-norm,
strongly anisotropic $\lVert\mu\rVert{=}0.48$), where against a memory-matched HNSW
genbo wins across the practical high-recall range ($1.4\text{--}3\times$ at recall
$\ge\!0.96$, reaching recall HNSW cannot; Table~\ref{tab:wiki35m}), with HNSW faster
only below $\approx\!0.95$. Our index also \emph{builds faster} there (matched int8 memory).
\textbf{Benchmark hygiene (a cautionary note).} An earlier version of this dataset
appeared to cap our recall at $0.62$ while HNSW reached $0.97$; the cause was a
ground-truth construction pitfall, not an algorithmic boundary. When the query set is
carved from the base and the exact ground truth is computed over the base
\emph{including} those rows, an index that correctly \emph{excludes} the queries faces
an artificial recall ceiling---here $38\%$ of each top-$10$ were query self-matches or
near-duplicate query rows, capping recall at $0.62$---while an index that inadvertently
\emph{retains} the queries appears to win. All numbers here use ground truth recomputed
over the queries-excluded base, with every method searching that same base. We flag this
because the failure is silent: both indices run without error and the ceiling looks like
a model deficiency. \textbf{Other scope:} single (shared) machine, not a
standardized-VM submission (the gating experiment for a leaderboard claim); $\gamma$ and
scan-precision are fit/selected per dataset; the coarse-cell corollary is shown on
IP/OOD data; the gap to binary-only leaderboard systems is inference from public
numbers; filter/sparse tracks are untouched.
