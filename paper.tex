\begin{abstract}
   For fixed $c>1$, the optimal balanced exponent for data-dependent approximate nearest neighbor search in Euclidean space is
   \[
      \rho_c=\frac{1}{2c^2-1}.
   \]
   Known worst-case constructions achieve $n^{\rho_c+o(1)}$ query time and $n^{1+\rho_c+o(1)}$ space. The goal here is to remove the subpolynomial loss, not merely optimize it. This draft isolates which parts of that program are now self-contained and which part remains a genuine mixed score/geometric stopping problem.

   The unconditional milestone is a restartable slice construction with
   \[
      Q(n,d)=
      d\,n^{\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(
         O_c\!\left((\log n)^{2/3}(\log\log n)^{2/3}\right)
      \right),
   \]
   and
   \[
      \qquad
      S(n,d)=
      dn+n^{1+\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(
         O_c\!\left((\log n)^{2/3}(\log\log n)^{2/3}\right)
      \right).
   \]
   The key self-contained ingredient is a spherical continuation-mass decoder with polylogarithmic loss; it lets the restart architecture improve from the older $7/9$ optimization to the $2/3$ exponent above. For terminal spherical search we also give a constant-depth smooth product filter with constant single-table recall and the same optimal exponent.

   We then record two stronger conditional routes. Almost-total cap splits can be made randomized geometric edges inside the same cumulative score tree: the residual edge is always searched, while the cap edge is followed with exactly the probability that makes the radius potential contract. This gives a clean conditional route to a $1/3$ exponent and identifies one polylogarithmic target as a mixed continuation operator over random cap-discovery frontiers. Separately, a structured posterior route shows that finite explicit channel enumeration is not the right bottleneck: if pure posterior children can be decoded output-sensitively across dyadic mass scales, the continuous likelihood-ball geometry plus negative-moment arithmetic already gives the $n^{\rho_c}\polylog n$ bound.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The $(c,r)$-approximate nearest neighbor problem asks us to preprocess a set $P\subseteq\R^d$ of $n$ points so that, given a query $q\in\R^d$ satisfying
\[
   \min_{p\in P}\|p-q\|_2\le r,
\]
the data structure returns some $p'\in P$ with
\[
   \|p'-q\|_2\le cr.
\]
Throughout the paper, $c>1$ is fixed. The data structure is randomized and data-dependent, and succeeds with a fixed constant probability. Candidate points are verified using their true Euclidean distances, so the structure never reports an incorrect answer.

For Euclidean space, the optimal balanced data-dependent exponent is
\[
   \rho_c=\frac{1}{2c^2-1}.
\]
The landmark construction of Andoni and Razenshteyn obtains query time $d\,n^{\rho_c+o(1)}$ and space $dn+n^{1+\rho_c+o(1)}$ for worst-case data sets~\cite{AR15}. Its geometric component removes dense spherical caps, recenters them as smaller certified balls, and applies a spherical filtering primitive to the residual data. The $o(1)$ term absorbs several losses: shell discretization, geometric slack, spherical list decoding, and the repeated restarting of the filtering process after recentering.

The purpose of the one-tree program is to stop optimizing that restart loss and instead remove it. The central design choice is to keep one cumulative score process across geometric recentering. Recentered children are not fresh spherical instances; they are edges inside the same score tree. This forces two changes:
\begin{itemize}
   \item the spherical decoder must survive with only polylogarithmic loss under continuation-mass pruning;
   \item geometric recursion must be paid by a potential identity rather than by restarting a full score tree in every child.
\end{itemize}
The first point is a one-sided branching-random-walk problem. We prove it first for the spherical block and then for predictable adaptive score schedules. The second point is handled by randomized cap thinning: always search the residual child, search the cap child with probability $z^s\Psi(R_C)/\Psi(R)$, and use the padded radius potential $\Psi(R)=1+(R/r)^4$. This makes the recursive query potential contract directly.

\subsection{Main result}

All logarithms are natural.

\begin{theorem}[Main self-contained theorem]\label{thm:main}
   Fix $c>1$. There is a randomized data-dependent data structure for worst-case $(c,r)$-approximate nearest neighbor search in $\R^d$ with constant success probability, query time
   \[
      Q(n,d)
      =
      d\,n^{\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(
         O_c\!\left((\log n)^{2/3}(\log\log n)^{2/3}\right)
      \right),
   \]
   and space
   \[
      S(n,d)
      =
      dn+n^{1+\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(
         O_c\!\left((\log n)^{2/3}(\log\log n)^{2/3}\right)
      \right),
   \]
   where $\rho_c=1/(2c^2-1)$. The bounds hold in expectation over preprocessing; standard truncation and rebuilding convert them to capped bounds with only constant-factor changes in success probability and asymptotic cost.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{What changes}

The algorithm in this draft differs from a restart construction in the following ways:
\begin{itemize}
   \item score prefixes are kept or discarded by continuation mass, not by a fresh hard threshold at every level;
   \item recentering changes the geometric coordinates but preserves the cumulative score state through an explicit offset;
   \item almost-total cap children are followed by randomized thinning probabilities chosen to contract the radius potential;
   \item cap-free terminal score leaves are scanned and charged to their low-correlation mass;
   \item every geometric child is certified by its actual maximum radius.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where the proof stands}

The expected-work calculation for continuation-mass pruning is exact, and the restartable slice construction built from it proves \cref{thm:main}. The smooth product terminal filter gives constant one-table recall once localization has reduced the instance to a spherical gap. The randomized cap split has an exact one-step potential contraction, and the cap-thinning probabilities telescope along a true near-neighbor lineage.

What is not yet a theorem is the global work bound for all cap-discovery stopping frontiers in the non-restarted one-tree process. A prefix-free frontier of a branching decoder is not a normalized path law: a point or query can occupy many prefixes. Thus the desired mixed operator needs a genuine continuation-mass or martingale proof, not just prefix-freeness plus Hölder. We state the needed operator explicitly in the polylog route below.

We also record posterior formulations because they are useful routes to more general certified child hierarchies. The finite-channel version is a coarse explicit-enumeration special case; the structured version is the sharper target suggested by the negative-moment accounting. The older cohort route still has a route-level cohort-or-leaf coupling condition, while the almost-total cap-thinning route isolates a narrower mixed-operator condition.

\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Correlation parameters}

For unit vectors $x,q\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$,
\[
   \|x-q\|_2^2=2-2\ip{x}{q}.
\]
A spherical filtering instance is specified by two correlations
\[
   -1<b<a<1.
\]
A near pair satisfies $\ip{x}{q}\ge a$, while an ordinary far pair satisfies $\ip{x}{q}\le b$. The balanced Gaussian filtering exponent is
\[
   \rho(a,b)
   =
   \frac{(1-a)(1+b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}.
\]
At the critical Euclidean normalization,
\[
   a_0=1-\frac{1}{c^2},
   \qquad
   b_0=0,
\]
and therefore
\[
   \rho(a_0,b_0)=\frac{1}{2c^2-1}=\rho_c.
\]

We repeatedly use the smoothness of $\rho$ near $(a_0,b_0)$.

\begin{lemma}[Stability of the balanced exponent]\label{lem:rho-stability}
   For every fixed $c>1$, there are constants $C_c>0$ and $\eta_c>0$ such that, whenever $0<\eta\le\eta_c$ and
   \[
      a\ge a_0-C_c\eta,
      \qquad
      b\le C_c\eta,
   \]
   we have
   \[
      \rho(a,b)\le \rho_c+C_c\eta.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The denominator $(1+a)(1-b)$ is bounded away from zero in a fixed neighborhood of $(a_0,0)$. The rational function $\rho(a,b)$ is continuously differentiable there. The claim follows from the mean-value theorem, after enlarging $C_c$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Polylogarithmic slack}

All geometric discretization parameters are chosen as inverse powers of $\log n$ with constants depending only on $c$. We write $\log^{O_c(1)}n$ for any resulting polynomial in these logarithmic factors. The restartable theorem still has the displayed $\exp(O((\log n)^{2/3}(\log\log n)^{2/3}))$ loss; the cumulative one-tree target is to absorb every additional nonconstant loss into $\log^{O_c(1)}n$.

\subsection{Expected cost and truncation}

The filtering constructions naturally give expected numbers of explored nodes and candidate occurrences. We use the following standard conversion. If one copy succeeds with probability at least $p_0>0$ and has expected query work at most $T$, stop it after $K T$ work for a sufficiently large constant $K=K(p_0)$. Markov's inequality shows that the truncation probability is at most $1/K$, so the truncated copy still succeeds with constant probability. A constant number of independent copies then restores any desired fixed success probability below one. The same idea applies to space: rebuild whenever the realized space exceeds a fixed multiple of its expectation. We therefore state expected bounds in intermediate lemmas and capped bounds in the final theorem.

\subsection{Dimension and diameter localization}

The score-and-ball proof below works inside projected cells whose diameter is only polylogarithmically larger than $r$. We record the reduction explicitly.

\begin{lemma}[Projected bounded-diameter localization]\label{lem:localization}
   Fix $c>1$. It is enough to solve $(c',r')$-ANN instances in dimension
   \[
      D=\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   whose point sets lie in balls of radius
   \[
      R_0\le r'\log^{O_c(1)}n,
   \]
   with exponent $\rho_{c'}\le\rho_c+O_c(1/\log n)$. The reduction uses only $\log^{O_c(1)}n$ instances and changes query time and space by polylogarithmic factors, plus the unavoidable $dn$ storage and $d\log^{O_c(1)}n$ projection work.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Choose a large constant $C_0=C_0(c)$ and set
   \[
      \varepsilon=\frac{1}{C_c\log n},
      \qquad
      D=C_0\varepsilon^{-2}\log n,
   \]
   and draw $T=C_c\log n$ independent Gaussian maps
   \[
      \Pi_t:\R^d\to\R^D,
      \qquad
      \Pi_t x=\frac1{\sqrt D}(g_1\cdot x,\ldots,g_D\cdot x),
   \]
   where the $g_i$ are standard Gaussian vectors. For a fixed query $q$, a standard chi-square tail gives
   \[
      \Prb\!\left[
         \left|\|\Pi_t(p-q)\|_2^2-\|p-q\|_2^2\right|
         >\varepsilon\|p-q\|_2^2
      \right]
      \le
      2\exp(-\Omega(\varepsilon^2D))
      \le
      n^{-C_0/10}
   \]
   for every fixed data point $p$, after increasing the absolute constant in $D$. A union bound over $P$ shows that one projection is \emph{good for $q$}, meaning all $n$ squared distances from $q$ are preserved within a factor $1\pm\varepsilon$, with probability at least $1-n^{-C_0/20}$. This statement is only over the data-structure randomness for the fixed query; no uniform guarantee over all possible queries is needed.

   In each projected copy, put a randomly shifted axis-aligned grid of side length
   \[
      h=20\sqrt D\,r',
      \qquad
      r'=(1+\varepsilon)r.
   \]
   If $\|p-q\|_2\le r$ and the projection is good, then
   \[
      \|\Pi_t p-\Pi_t q\|_2\le r'.
   \]
   For a random grid shift, the probability that two projected points at distance at most $r'$ are separated by some grid hyperplane is at most
   \[
      \frac{\|\Pi_t p-\Pi_t q\|_1}{h}
      \le
      \frac{\sqrt D\,r'}{20\sqrt D\,r'}
      \le
      \frac1{20}.
   \]
   Hence, in a good projection, a near neighbor shares the query cell with probability at least $19/20$ over the grid shift. With $T=C_c\log n$ independent projected grids, at least one copy is good and keeps the near pair together with constant probability; increasing $C_c$ gives any fixed success probability below one.

   Each grid cell has projected diameter at most $h\sqrt D=20Dr'$, so it is contained in a ball of radius $R_0=10Dr'=r\log^{O_c(1)}n$. We build one score-and-ball instance for every nonempty projected cell. The total number of stored projected point occurrences over one grid is $n$, and over all grids is $n\log^{O_c(1)}n$.

   A point with original distance greater than $cr$ from $q$ has projected distance greater than $(1-\varepsilon)cr$ in a good projection. Therefore a projected $(c',r')$-ANN answer with
   \[
      c'=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)c}{1+\varepsilon}
   \]
   is guaranteed, after verification in the original metric, to contain a valid $c$-approximate neighbor whenever one exists. Since $c\mapsto 1/(2c^2-1)$ is smooth in a fixed neighborhood of the chosen $c>1$, $\rho_{c'}\le\rho_c+O_c(\varepsilon)$, so $n^{\rho_{c'}}\le e^{O_c(1)}n^{\rho_c}$.

   At query time we project $q$ into all $T$ copies, query the cell containing $\Pi_tq$ in each copy, and verify returned candidates in the original metric. The projection work is $dD T=d\log^{O_c(1)}n$, and the number of localized instances queried is polylogarithmic.

   Bad projected copies do not spoil the expected work. The query implementation caps each localized copy by falling back to a brute-force scan of its projected cell, so a bad copy costs at most $n$ distance checks. Its expected contribution is at most
   \[
      T\cdot n\cdot n^{-C_0/20}=o(1)
   \]
   after increasing $C_0$. Good copies satisfy the promised projected near/far gap and bounded diameter, so the localized bounds apply. This preserves the claimed asymptotic query and space bounds.
\end{proof}

\section{The cumulative-score spherical primitive}

The spherical stage uses one cumulative Gaussian score tree and prunes by continuation mass. This avoids the two-sided tube that causes the $\exp(O(k^{1/3}))$ loss in the earlier analysis.

Choose a constant branching factor $B\ge2$, write $\ell_B=\log B$, and let
\[
   t^2=(1+a)\ell_B.
\]
A path of depth $k$ carries independent Gaussian edge vectors $g_e\sim N(0,I_d)$ and cumulative score
\[
   S_v(x)=\sum_{e\preceq v}\ip{g_e}{x}.
\]
The depth is
\[
   k
   =
   \left\lceil
      \frac{2(1+b)\log m}
      {(1+a)(1-b)\ell_B}
   \right\rceil
   =
   \Theta_{a,b}(\log m),
\]
and a leaf accepts $x$ when $S_v(x)\ge kt$.

For a node $v$ at level $\ell$ with score $s$, put $r=k-\ell$ and define the continuation mass
\[
   H_\ell(s)
   =
   B^r
   \overline\Phi\!\left(\frac{kt-s}{\sqrt r}\right).
\]
This is exactly the expected number of accepting descendant leaves of $v$, conditional on the current score $s$. The decoder retains a prefix only while
\[
   H_\ell(S_v(x))\ge k^{-A}
\]
for a sufficiently large constant $A=A_{a,b}$. Equivalently, up to lower-order tail terms, a retained prefix satisfies
\[
   S_v(x)\ge
   kt-\sqrt{2r(r\log B+A\log k)}.
\]
Early scores may lie well below the final linear trajectory; the barrier tightens only near the leaf. See \cref{fig:barrier}.

\begin{lemma}[Exact expected work]\label{lem:continuation-work}
   For fixed $-1<b<a<1$, continuation-mass pruning gives expected query path work
   \[
      m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m,
   \]
   expected ordinary-far candidate occurrences
   \[
      m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m,
   \]
   and stored point-path occurrences
   \[
      m^{1+\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   At any fixed level, summing $H_\ell(S_v(x))$ over all prefixes $v$ counts the expected number of accepting descendant leaves for $x$. Since every retained prefix has continuation mass at least $k^{-A}$, the expected number of retained prefixes at that level is at most $k^A$ times the expected number of accepting leaves. Summing over $k$ levels gives only a polynomial factor in $k$.

   The expected number of accepting leaves for one point is
   \[
      B^k\overline\Phi(t\sqrt{k})
      =
      m^{\rho(a,b)}k^{O(1)}.
   \]
   For a pair of correlation at most $b$, the Gaussian joint-tail estimate
   \[
      \Prb[X\ge u,\,Y\ge u]
      \le
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{1+b}\right)
   \]
   gives expected common accepting leaves at most
   \[
      m^{-(1-\rho(a,b))}k^{O(1)}.
   \]
   Summing over $m$ ordinary far points proves the far-candidate bound. Summing the one-point bound over stored data points proves the storage bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Tame good near leaf]\label{def:good-near-leaf}
   Fix a near pair $(p,q)$ with $\ip{p}{q}=a$. For a prefix $u$ at level $\ell$, write
   \[
      Y_u(x)=S_u(x)-\ell t
   \]
   and define the centered common coordinate
   \[
      M_u=\frac{Y_u(p)+Y_u(q)}{\sqrt{2(1+a)}}.
   \]
   A leaf $v$ is tame good for $(p,q)$ if
   \[
      S_v(p),S_v(q)\in[kt,kt+1]
   \]
   every prefix $u\preceq v$ satisfies the continuation-mass pruning rule for both $p$ and $q$, and every prefix satisfies the analytical upper barrier
   \[
      M_u\le H_{a,b}
   \]
   for a sufficiently large fixed constant $H_{a,b}$. This upper barrier is used only in the proof; the algorithm does not enforce it.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Tame-good first moment]\label{lem:good-first-moment}
   For every fixed $-1<b<a<1$ and sufficiently large pruning constant $A$, if $Z$ is the number of tame good near leaves for a pair of correlation exactly $a$, then
   \[
      \E Z\ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Work at the critical correlation $a$, which is the least favorable near case. Exponentially tilt the two-dimensional Gaussian increment $(X,Y)$ so that both coordinates have mean $t$. Since
   \[
      \Sigma=
      \begin{pmatrix}
         1&a\\
         a&1
      \end{pmatrix},
      \qquad
      \theta=\frac{t}{1+a}(1,1),
   \]
   the tilted law has mean $\Sigma\theta=(t,t)$ and
   \[
      \frac12\theta^\top\Sigma\theta=\frac{t^2}{1+a}=\ell_B.
   \]
   Let $Q$ denote the tilted measure. On the endpoint event
   \[
      S_v(p),S_v(q)\in[kt,kt+1],
   \]
   the likelihood ratio satisfies
   \[
      B^k\frac{dP}{dQ}=\exp(O_{a,b}(1)).
   \]
   Therefore the expected number of tame good leaves under the original measure is, up to constant factors, the $Q$-probability that one tilted two-dimensional walk has endpoint in the unit square, stays above the two continuation barriers, and stays below the common-coordinate upper barrier.

   Under $Q$, after subtracting the linear drift $\ell t$, each coordinate is a centered Gaussian walk. The endpoint has covariance $k\Sigma$, so the probability that both centered endpoints lie in $[0,1]$ is $\Theta_{a,b}(1/k)$.

   It remains to check that the path barriers do not cost more than a polynomial factor. Write $r=k-\ell$ and let
   \[
      d_r=\sqrt{2r(r\ell_B+A\log k)}-rt.
   \]
   A prefix at level $\ell$ is retained whenever the centered coordinate is at least $-d_r$, up to harmless constant tail corrections. Since $t^2=(1+a)\ell_B<2\ell_B$, there is a constant $c_{a,b}>0$ such that
   \[
      d_r\ge c_{a,b}r
      \qquad\text{for } r\ge C_{a,b}\log k,
   \]
   and, for $1\le r\le C_{a,b}\log k$,
   \[
      d_r\ge c_{a,b}\sqrt{A r\log k}
   \]
   after increasing $A$. Conditioned on endpoints in $[0,1]^2$, each coordinate is a Gaussian bridge whose variance at distance $r$ from the endpoint is $O(r)$. A union bound over $r$ therefore gives failure probability
   \[
      \sum_{r\ge C\log k}\exp(-\Omega_{a,b}(r))
      +
      \sum_{1\le r\le C\log k} k^{-\Omega_{a,b}(A)}
      =
      o(1)
   \]
   for $A$ large enough.

   The common coordinate $M_u$ is a standard one-dimensional centered Gaussian bridge, independent of the difference coordinate. By the reflection principle for a Gaussian bridge, conditioned on its endpoint lying in a fixed bounded interval below $H_{a,b}$,
   \[
      \Prb_Q\!\left[\max_{u\preceq v}M_u\le H_{a,b}\right]
      \ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
   On this upper-barrier event, violation of either continuation lower barrier would force the difference-coordinate bridge to make an excursion of size at least $d_r-O_{a,b}(1)$ at distance $r$ from the endpoint. The same union bound as above shows that this has probability $o(1)$ after increasing $A$. Thus, conditional on the endpoint event, all barriers in the definition of tame good hold with probability at least $k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}$. Hence $\E Z\ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tame-good second moment]\label{lem:tame-second-moment}
   For every fixed $-1<b<a<1$, the tame-good leaf count $Z$ from \cref{lem:good-first-moment} satisfies
   \[
      \E Z^2\le k^{O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\caption{Second-moment decomposition by the lowest common ancestor. Two tame-good leaves $v,w$ share a prefix of length $j$ and then run independently for $r=k-j$ steps. After multiplying the conditional endpoint probability by the $B^{2k-j}$ ordered pairs at split depth $j$, the common-coordinate drift $\mu^2=2\ell_B$ cancels the exponential growth; the upper cap $M_u\le H_{a,b}$ at the split kills prolific high shared prefixes, leaving a summable $\sum_j (j{+}1)^{-1/2}(k{-}j)^{-3/2}k^{-1/2}=O(1)$.}
\label{fig:lca}
\end{figure}

\begin{proof}
   We decompose pairs of leaves by the depth $j$ of their lowest common ancestor. Write $r=k-j$, and first consider $0\le j<k$. There are at most $B^{2k-j}$ ordered pairs with split depth $j$.

   Use the independent common and difference coordinates
   \[
      C_\ell=\frac{S_\ell(p)+S_\ell(q)}{\sqrt{2(1+a)}},
      \qquad
      D_\ell=\frac{S_\ell(p)-S_\ell(q)}{\sqrt{2(1-a)}}.
   \]
   The common coordinate has unit Gaussian increments and endpoint threshold
   \[
      \mu k,
      \qquad
      \mu=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+a}}\,t=\sqrt{2\ell_B}.
   \]
   If both leaves are tame good, then at the split prefix the centered common value $y=C_j-\mu j$ satisfies $y\le H_{a,b}$, and both common-coordinate suffixes end in a fixed unit interval around $\mu k$. Ignoring all other constraints, the common-coordinate probability is at most
   \[
      \int_{-\infty}^{H_{a,b}}
      \frac{1}{\sqrt{j+1}}
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\mu j+y)^2}{2j}\right)
      \left[
         \frac{C}{\sqrt r}
         \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\mu r-y)^2}{2r}\right)
      \right]^2dy,
   \]
   with the evident interpretation when $j=0$. Multiplying by the pair count $B^{2k-j}$ and using $\mu^2=2\ell_B$ cancels the exponential main term and leaves
   \[
      C_{a,b}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j+1}\,r}
      \int_{-\infty}^{H_{a,b}}\exp(\mu y)\,dy
      \le
      \frac{C_{a,b}}{\sqrt{j+1}\,r}.
   \]

   The difference coordinates for the two leaves are centered jointly Gaussian with variances $k$ and covariance $j$. The event that both endpoint differences lie in the fixed interval forced by
   \[
      S_v(p),S_v(q),S_w(p),S_w(q)\in[kt,kt+1]
   \]
   has probability at most the maximum bivariate Gaussian density times a constant area:
   \[
      O_{a,b}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^2-j^2}}\right)
      =
      O_{a,b}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{kr}}\right).
   \]
   Since the common and difference coordinates are independent, the total contribution of split depth $j<k$ to $\E Z^2$ is at most
   \[
      \frac{C_{a,b}}{\sqrt{j+1}\,r^{3/2}\sqrt k}.
   \]
   Summing over $j<k$ gives a bounded quantity:
   \[
      \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
      \frac{1}{\sqrt{j+1}\,(k-j)^{3/2}\sqrt k}
      =
      O(1).
   \]

   The diagonal contribution $v=w$ is at most the first moment of accepting near leaves, hence at most $k^{O_{a,b}(1)}$ by the same endpoint calculation used in \cref{lem:good-first-moment}. Therefore $\E Z^2\le k^{O_{a,b}(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-sided spherical survival]\label{cor:spherical-survival}
   If a query $q$ has a data point $p$ with $\ip{p}{q}\ge a$, then the continuation-mass decoder produces a common accepting leaf for $(p,q)$ with probability at least $\log^{-O_{a,b}(1)}m$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
   By \cref{lem:good-first-moment} and \cref{lem:tame-second-moment}, Paley--Zygmund gives
   \[
      \Prb[Z>0]\ge \frac{(\E Z)^2}{\E Z^2}
      \ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
   Since $k=\Theta_{a,b}(\log m)$, this is the desired inverse-polylogarithmic survival probability. Correlation larger than $a$ can only improve the tilted endpoint exponent; the same constants cover a fixed compact neighborhood of the critical parameters after the usual slack adjustment.
\end{proof}

The spherical primitive therefore has polylogarithmic loss: expected work follows from continuation mass, and near-pair survival follows from the tame-good first and second moments. No algorithmic two-sided tube is imposed.

\subsection{Budgeted score blocks}

The spherical block can be run with a budget parameter larger than the number of resident points. This is useful in the Euclidean recursion because the natural query potential is
\[
   F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2(1-\rho)},
\]
not just $m^\rho$.

\begin{corollary}[Budgeted continuation block]\label{cor:budgeted-block}
   Fix $-1<b<a<1$ and write $\rho=\rho(a,b)$. Let a spherical node contain $m$ points, and let $N\ge m$. Run the continuation-mass decoder exactly as above, except choose the depth
   \[
      k
      =
      \left\lceil
         \frac{2(1+b)\log N}
         {(1+a)(1-b)\ell_B}
      \right\rceil .
   \]
   Then the expected query path work is
   \[
      N^\rho\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}N,
   \]
   the expected ordinary-far candidate occurrences are at most
   \[
      mN^{-(1-\rho)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}N
      \le
      N^\rho\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}N,
   \]
   and the stored point-path occurrences are at most
   \[
      mN^\rho\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}N.
   \]
   If a query has a near point with correlation at least $a$, the success probability is at least $\log^{-O_{a,b}(1)}N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
   The proof of \cref{lem:continuation-work} depends on the depth only through the expected one-point accepting-leaf count and the far joint-tail count. With the displayed depth these become $N^\rho\log^{O(1)}N$ and $N^{-(1-\rho)}\log^{O(1)}N$, respectively. Summing the latter over the $m$ resident points gives the stated far-candidate bound, and $m\le N$ gives the displayed simplification. The storage bound is the one-point accepting count summed over $m$ data points.

   The first- and second-moment near-pair arguments in \cref{lem:good-first-moment}, \cref{lem:tame-second-moment}, and \cref{cor:spherical-survival} use only the score depth $k=\Theta_{a,b}(\log N)$ and the fixed correlations. Replacing $m$ by $N$ in that depth therefore gives success probability $\log^{-O_{a,b}(1)}N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Radius-budgeted score block]\label{cor:radius-budgeted-block}
   Fix $\rho=\rho_c$ and $s=1-\rho$. At a Euclidean node with $m$ points in radius $R\ge r$, set
   \[
      N=m\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s/\rho}.
   \]
   Then one continuation-mass score block has expected query path work and ordinary-far candidate occurrences
   \[
      F(m,R)\log^{O_c(1)}N,
      \qquad
      F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s},
   \]
   stored point-path occurrences
   \[
      mF(m,R)\log^{O_c(1)}N,
   \]
   and inverse-polylogarithmic near-pair survival.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
   Since $R\ge r$, the chosen $N$ is at least $m$. Also
   \[
      N^\rho=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}=F(m,R).
   \]
   Apply \cref{cor:budgeted-block}. In the bounded-diameter instances used below, $\log N=\log^{O_c(1)}n$, so the logarithmic factors are absorbed into $\log^{O_c(1)}n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A constant-recall smooth terminal filter}

The continuation-mass decoder above is designed to survive adaptive score schedules and recentering. For a terminal spherical search, where no further geometric edge has to be coupled to a score prefix, there is a simpler constant-depth smooth product filter with constant one-table recall. This gives a non-channel terminal layer for any localization scheme that can reduce the remaining instance to a spherical near/far gap.

Let
\[
   \phi(\gamma)=\frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma},
   \qquad
   \rho(a,b)=\frac{\phi(a)}{\phi(b)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Smooth product terminal filter]\label{thm:smooth-product-terminal}
   Fix $-1<b<a<1$ and a fixed integer $k\ge 2/(1-a)$. Let $m$ be the spherical instance size. Put
   \[
      t^2=\frac{2\log m}{\phi(b)}+O_{a,b}(\log\log m),
      \qquad
      u=\frac{t}{\sqrt k}.
   \]
   For each block $i\in[k]$, sample
   \[
      g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,B}\sim N(0,I_d),
      \qquad
      B=\Theta_{a,b}\!\left(J_a(u)^{-1}\right),
   \]
   where $J_\gamma(u)=\Pr[X\ge u,Y\ge u]$ for correlated standard Gaussians with correlation $\gamma$. A leaf is a tuple $j=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)\in[B]^k$ with aggregate normal
   \[
      G_j=\frac1{\sqrt k}\sum_{i=1}^k g_{i,j_i},
   \]
   and accepts a point $x$ if $\ip{G_j}{x}\ge t$.

   Then one table has constant success probability for every near pair of correlation at least $a$. Its expected query leaves, update leaves per point, and ordinary far candidates are
   \[
      m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m,
   \]
   and its expected storage is
   \[
      m^{1+\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m.
   \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
   For a near pair $(p,q)$ of correlation at least $a$, one block contains a column satisfying
   \[
      \ip{g_{i,j},p}\ge u,
      \qquad
      \ip{g_{i,j},q}\ge u
   \]
   with probability bounded below by a positive constant, by the choice $B=\Theta(J_a(u)^{-1})$. The blocks are independent and $k$ is fixed, so with constant probability every block contains such a common column. Choosing one such column per block gives a tuple accepted by both $p$ and $q$, since
   \[
      \ip{G_j,p},\ip{G_j,q}\ge\frac{k u}{\sqrt k}=t.
   \]

   The number of implicit leaves is
   \[
      M=B^k
      =
      \exp\!\left(\frac{t^2}{1+a}\right)\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m,
   \]
   using the standard bivariate Gaussian tail
   \[
      J_a(u)=u^{-O_{a,b}(1)}
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{1+a}\right).
   \]
   For each fixed tuple, $G_j\sim N(0,I_d)$. Hence the expected number of leaves entered by one query, and also by one stored point, is
   \[
      M\overline\Phi(t)
      =
      \exp\!\left(\frac{t^2}{1+a}-\frac{t^2}{2}\right)
      \log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m
      =
      m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m,
   \]
   because $t^2=2\log m/\phi(b)+O(\log\log m)$ and $\rho(a,b)=\phi(a)/\phi(b)$. This proves the query-leaf and storage bounds.

   For a far data point $x$ with $\ip{x}{q}\le b$, the pair $(\ip{G_j,x},\ip{G_j,q})$ is bivariate Gaussian with correlation at most $b$. Therefore
   \[
      M J_b(t)
      \le
      \exp\!\left(\frac{t^2}{1+a}-\frac{t^2}{1+b}\right)
      \log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m
      =
      m^{-(1-\rho(a,b))}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m.
   \]
   Summing over the $m$ resident far points gives the far-candidate bound.
\end{proof}

The leaf set $[B]^k$ is implicit. For a query or data point, sort the $B$ scores in each block and enumerate tuples whose block-score sum is at least $t\sqrt k$ by a best-first heap over the $k$ sorted lists. If $L$ tuples are output, this costs $O_k(B\log B+L\log L)$. The condition $k\ge2/(1-a)$ gives $B\le m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m$, so the enumeration overhead is absorbed into the stated polynomial logarithmic factors.

The smooth product filter is not a replacement for \cref{lem:adaptive-continuation}: it has no long prefix process on which to hang many recentering decisions. Its role is terminal. Once a localization method produces a bounded spherical subproblem with the critical near/far angular gap, \cref{thm:smooth-product-terminal} gives constant one-table recall and removes the last inverse-polylogarithmic amplification loss.

\begin{lemma}[Continuation-sampled detector tokens]\label{lem:continuation-detector}
   In any continuation-mass score block, a detector token can be sampled as follows. At a retained prefix $u$, assign every retained child $v$ its one-point continuation mass $H(v)$ for the remaining schedule, choose one child with probability proportional to $H(v)$, and kill the token if the total retained child mass is zero. Then the resulting detector law is normalized: for every prefix-free family $\mathcal U$,
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\Prb[\text{the token reaches }u]\le1.
   \]
   The same construction applies to the adaptive schedule of \cref{def:regular-adaptive-score}, using the exact conditional continuation mass for the remaining predictable schedule.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   A detector token has a single current prefix. At each score step it either dies or moves to exactly one child. Hence the events that it first reaches members of a prefix-free family are disjoint. Summing their probabilities gives at most one. The adaptive case is identical because the continuation masses are predictable once the current prefix is fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Detector path probability and sibling inflation]\label{lem:detector-inflation}
   For a retained score prefix $u$, let
   \[
      D(u)=\sum_{v:\,v\text{ retained child of }u}H(v),
      \qquad
      \Gamma(u)=\frac{D(u)}{H(u)}
   \]
   whenever $H(u)>0$. If $v$ is a retained prefix and $u\prec v$ ranges over the proper prefixes of $v$, then the continuation-sampled detector law satisfies
   \[
      \Prb[\text{one detector token reaches }v]
      =
      \frac{H(v)}{H(\varnothing)}
      \prod_{u\prec v}\Gamma(u)^{-1}.
   \]
   In particular, for a retained accepting leaf $v$,
   \[
      \Prb[\text{one detector token reaches }v]
      =
      \frac{1}{H(\varnothing)}
      \prod_{u\prec v}\Gamma(u)^{-1}.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   At a retained prefix $u$, the detector chooses the next prefix $w$ with probability $H(w)/D(u)$. Multiplying these transition probabilities along the path to $v$ gives
   \[
      \prod_{u\to w\preceq v}\frac{H(w)}{D(u)}
      =
      \prod_{u\to w\preceq v}
      \frac{H(w)}{H(u)}\Gamma(u)^{-1}
      =
      \frac{H(v)}{H(\varnothing)}
      \prod_{u\prec v}\Gamma(u)^{-1}.
   \]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Off-spine reduction for detector inflation]\label{lem:off-spine-inflation}
   Fix a retained path
   \[
      v_0=\varnothing,\ v_1,\ldots,v_h
   \]
   and reveal the on-spine score increments along this path. Put
   \[
      R_i=\frac{H(v_{i+1})}{H(v_i)}
      \qquad (0\le i<h).
   \]
   Conditional on the revealed spine, the off-spine children satisfy
   \[
      \E\!\left[
         \prod_{i=0}^{h-1}\Gamma(v_i)
         \,\middle|\, v_0,\ldots,v_h
      \right]
      \le
      \prod_{i=0}^{h-1}\left(R_i+\frac{B-1}{B}\right).
   \]
   Consequently, if
   \[
      \sum_{i=0}^{h-1} R_i\le C\log K
   \]
   on a path of score depth at most $K$, then with conditional probability at least $1/2$ over the off-spine children,
   \[
      \prod_{i=0}^{h-1}\Gamma(v_i)\le 2K^C .
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Write $O_i$ for the retained continuation mass of the off-spine children of $v_i$:
   \[
      O_i=\sum_{\substack{w:\,w\text{ retained child of }v_i\\w\ne v_{i+1}}}H(w).
   \]
   Then
   \[
      \Gamma(v_i)=R_i+\frac{O_i}{H(v_i)}.
   \]
   Conditional on the spine, the off-spine child subtrees at different levels are independent. Also, by the defining harmonicity of continuation mass before the finite children are realized,
   \[
      \E[O_i\mid v_0,\ldots,v_h]
      \le
      \frac{B-1}{B}H(v_i),
   \]
   where the inequality only uses that discarding non-retained children can decrease the sum. Therefore
   \[
      \E\!\left[
         \prod_i\Gamma(v_i)
         \,\middle|\, v_0,\ldots,v_h
      \right]
      =
      \prod_i
      \E\!\left[
         R_i+\frac{O_i}{H(v_i)}
         \,\middle|\, v_0,\ldots,v_h
      \right]
      \le
      \prod_i\left(R_i+\frac{B-1}{B}\right).
   \]
   Since $(B-1)/B\le1$, the displayed spine-ratio condition gives
   \[
      \prod_i\left(R_i+\frac{B-1}{B}\right)
      \le
      \prod_i(1+R_i)
      \le
      \exp\!\left(\sum_iR_i\right)
      \le
      K^C.
   \]
   Markov's inequality gives the final claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A sufficient detector-shadowing target]\label{cor:detector-shadowing-target}
   Suppose a retained prefix $v$ of depth at most $K$ satisfies
   \[
      \sum_{u\prec v}\frac{H(u^+)}{H(u)}\le C\log K,
   \]
   where $u^+$ is the next prefix of $v$. Then, with probability at least $1/2$ over the off-spine score children,
   \[
      \Prb[\text{one detector token reaches }v]
      \ge
      \frac{H(v)}{2K^C H(\varnothing)}.
   \]
   Thus
   \[
      O\!\left(K^C\frac{H(\varnothing)}{H(v)}\log^{A_c}n\right)
   \]
   continuation-sampled detector tokens hit $v$ with probability at least $1-n^{-A_c}$, conditional on the same off-spine event. For an accepting leaf $H(v)=1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
   Combine \cref{lem:detector-inflation} and \cref{lem:off-spine-inflation}. The final amplification bound is the elementary estimate
   \[
      \left(1-\frac{H(v)}{2K^CH(\varnothing)}\right)^{
         C'K^C(H(\varnothing)/H(v))\log^{A_c}n}
      \le
      n^{-A_c}
   \]
   after increasing the constant $C'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The spine-ratio condition is an early-prefix condition]\label{rem:linear-spine-ratio}
   The logarithmic spine-ratio condition in \cref{cor:detector-shadowing-target} should not be expected for a full accepting score path. In the homogeneous spherical block, consider the deterministic linear spine with score $S_\ell=\ell t$. If $r=k-\ell$, then
   \[
      \frac{H_{\ell+1}((\ell+1)t)}{H_\ell(\ell t)}
      =
      \frac{1}{B}\,
      \frac{\overline\Phi(t\sqrt{r-1})}
           {\overline\Phi(t\sqrt r)}
      =
      B^{-(1-a)/2}\exp(O_{a,b}(1/r))
   \]
   for $r\ge2$, by Mills' ratio and $t^2=(1+a)\log B$. Thus a typical tilted near spine can have
   \[
      \sum_{\ell<k}\frac{H_{\ell+1}(S_{\ell+1})}{H_\ell(S_\ell)}
      =
      \Theta_{a,b}(k),
   \]
   not $O(\log k)$. Consequently \cref{cor:detector-shadowing-target} is useful for early activation prefixes, where the relevant detector cost is $H(\varnothing)/H(v)$, but long near routes need an aggregate detector-mass argument over many possible good prefixes, or else the ordinary continuation path must reach a cap-free scan.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why detector shadowing is still nontrivial]\label{rem:detector-shadowing-obstruction}
   A tempting shortcut would be to claim that the inflation product in \cref{lem:detector-inflation} is always at most one, so every retained accepting leaf $v$ is reached with probability at least $1/H(\varnothing)$. This would be true if the realized child masses satisfied
   \[
      H(u)=\sum_{v:\,v\text{ child of }u}H(v)
   \]
   at every sampled score node. The analytic continuation mass used by the decoder is only harmonic in expectation over the next Gaussian children, however. After the $B$ children of a finite branching node are realized, the sibling sum can be larger than $H(u)$, and a detector token can be diverted toward atypically large siblings. Thus detector tokens are normalized for prefix-free accounting by \cref{lem:continuation-detector}, but they do not automatically shadow an ordinary tame-good near leaf. This is the remaining probabilistic content in the cohort-or-leaf route.
\end{remark}

\section{The certified score-and-ball process}

We now describe the Euclidean part as one branching process. A node of the process carries both a cumulative Gaussian score state and a certified Euclidean ball. Gaussian score edges perform filtering inside the current ball; recentering edges are deterministic branches that move to a smaller certified ball while retaining the score prefix and updating the coordinate offset. Thus the geometric recursion is not a separate wrapper around the score tree.

\subsection{Two elementary geometric facts}

The first fact certifies the radius decrease obtained from a spherical cap.

\begin{lemma}[Cap recentering]\label{lem:cap}
   Let $P$ lie on the sphere of radius $R$ centered at $o$, and suppose
   \[
      \ip{(p-o)/R}{w}\ge\gamma
   \]
   for every $p$ in a cap $C\subseteq P$, where $\|w\|_2=1$. Then
   \[
      C\subseteq
      \Ball\!\left(o+\gamma Rw,\,R\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}\right).
   \]
   In particular, if $\gamma=\Theta_c(\eta)$, the certified child radius satisfies
   \[
      R_C\le R(1-\Theta_c(\eta^2)).
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   For $p\in C$,
   \begin{align*}
      \|p-(o+\gamma Rw)\|_2^2
                                           &=\|p-o\|_2^2+\gamma^2R^2-2\gamma R\ip{p-o}{w}\\
                                           &\le R^2+\gamma^2R^2-2\gamma^2R^2\\
                                           &=R^2(1-\gamma^2).
   \end{align*}
   The final estimate follows from $\sqrt{1-x}=1-\Theta(x)$ for sufficiently small positive $x$.
\end{proof}

The second fact shows that thin radial shells do not degrade the approximation factor.

\begin{lemma}[Angular gap on two shells]\label{lem:shell}
   Let data points have radius $S$ and let the query have radius $T$ around a common center. Put $\Delta=|S-T|$. If a data-query pair has Euclidean distance at most $r$, its squared angular chord distance is at most
   \[
      d_1^2=\frac{r^2-\Delta^2}{ST}.
   \]
   If its Euclidean distance is greater than $cr$, its squared angular chord distance is greater than
   \[
      d_2^2=\frac{c^2r^2-\Delta^2}{ST}.
   \]
   Whenever the near case is feasible,
   \[
      \frac{d_2^2}{d_1^2}\ge c^2.
   \]
   The same statements hold with $1+O_c(\eta)$ multiplicative slack for shells of relative width $O_c(\eta)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   For normalized directions $u=(p-o)/S$ and $v=(q-o)/T$,
   \[
      \|p-q\|_2^2=(S-T)^2+ST\|u-v\|_2^2.
   \]
   Solving for $\|u-v\|_2^2$ gives the first two formulas. Moreover,
   \[
      \frac{c^2r^2-\Delta^2}{r^2-\Delta^2}
      \ge c^2
   \]
   because $(c^2-1)\Delta^2\ge0$. Shell discretization perturbs $S$, $T$, and $\Delta$ by relative $O_c(\eta)$, which yields the final claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Posterior stopping}

Fix a geometric node containing $m$ points in a certified ball $\Ball(o,R)$. The next two lemmas use the continuation-sampled detector law of \cref{lem:continuation-detector}, not the ordinary retained-prefix multiplicity of the continuation decoder. A detector token follows one score channel at a time, or dies, so every prefix-free set has total detector mass at most one.

Let $\mu(u)$ be the probability that one detector token driven by a uniformly random data point in the node reaches score prefix $u$, and let $\nu_q(u)$ be the corresponding one-token detector probability for query $q$. Thus, for every prefix-free family $\mathcal U$,
\[
   \sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\mu(u)\le1,
   \qquad
   \sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\nu_q(u)\le1.
\]
For a deterministic child cluster $C$ of relative size $\alpha=|C|/m$, let $\mu_C(u)$ be the detector prefix law conditioned on the point belonging to $C$. The posterior mass of $C$ at prefix $u$ is
\[
   Z_C(u)=\frac{\alpha\mu_C(u)}{\mu(u)}.
\]
Let $\mathcal A_C$ be the first-hit frontier on which $Z_C(u)\ge\tau$.

Put
\[
   \rho=\rho_c,
   \qquad
   s=1-\rho.
\]
The Bellman detector mass assigned to prefix $u$ is
\[
   w_q(u)=\mu(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Posterior first-hit inequality]\label{lem:first-hit}
   For every prefix-free first-hit frontier $\mathcal A_C$,
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C} w_q(u)
      \le
      \left(\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\right)^s.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   On $\mathcal A_C$, the inequality $Z_C(u)\ge\tau$ gives
   \[
      \mu(u)\le \frac{\alpha}{\tau}\mu_C(u).
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho
      \le
      \left(\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\right)^s
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu_C(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho.
   \]
   Since $s+\rho=1$ and $\mathcal A_C$ is prefix-free, Hölder's inequality gives
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu_C(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho
      \le
      \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu_C(u)\right)^s
      \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\nu_q(u)\right)^\rho
      \le1.
   \]
\end{proof}

This lemma is deliberately stated for normalized detector mass. It would be false if $\mu$ and $\nu_q$ were interpreted as the raw number of retained ordinary prefixes in the branching continuation decoder; a point or query can occupy many such prefixes. Ordinary retained-prefix work is controlled separately by continuation mass in \cref{lem:continuation-work}.

\subsection{Radius Bellman cancellation}

Use the recursive query potential
\[
   F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}.
\]
Suppose child $C$ has size $\alpha m$ and certified radius $R_C=\lambda R$. Activate it at posterior threshold
\[
   \tau=\lambda^2.
\]
Then the fractional cost of entering and recursively searching $C$ is exactly
\[
   \left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda^2}\right)^s
   F(\alpha m,\lambda R)
   =
   \alpha F(m,R).
\]
Thus disjoint activated children consume at most the parent potential, because $\sum_C\alpha_C\le1$. No cap geometry, balance condition, or bound on the number of children appears in this cancellation. Geometry enters only through the certified radii $R_C$. \Cref{fig:bellman} shows the cancellation schematically.

The same idea controls space:
\[
   \sum_C |C|^{1+\rho}
   \le
   m^{1+\rho}
\]
for disjoint children. Polynomially many shells and bounded-diameter roots contribute only $\log^{O_c(1)}n$ factors when shell widths and distortions are inverse-polynomial in $\log n$.

\begin{lemma}[Vote-budgeted activations]\label{lem:vote-budget}
   At a state with parent potential $F(m,R)$, give the query
   \[
      T_u=A\log^{A_c}n\,F(m,R)
   \]
   detector tokens, for a sufficiently large constant $A$. Each token may cast at most one vote, to the first child whose posterior reaches its activation threshold. A child $C$ of size $m_C$ and certified radius $R_C$ is opened only after receiving
   \[
      T_C=\log^{A_c}n\,F(m_C,R_C)
   \]
   votes. Then, for every realization of the score tree and the detector tokens,
   \[
      \sum_{\text{opened }C}F(m_C,R_C)\le A F(m,R).
   \]
   The analogous storage charge satisfies
   \[
      \sum_{\text{stored children }C} m_C F(m_C,R_C)\le mF(m,R)
   \]
   whenever the stored children form a disjoint partition or a disjoint family of prefix-conditioned slices.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Since each detector token votes once, the number of realized votes is at most $T_u$. Every opened child consumes $T_C$ votes, and these vote sets are disjoint. Hence
   \[
      \sum_{\text{opened }C}\log^{A_c}n\,F(m_C,R_C)
      =
      \sum_{\text{opened }C}T_C
      \le
      T_u
      =
      A\log^{A_c}n\,F(m,R),
   \]
   which proves the query-potential bound. For storage, if children are disjoint then
   \[
      m_C F(m_C,R_C)
      =
      m_C^{1+\rho}\left(\frac{R_C}{r}\right)^{2s}
      \le
      m_C^{1+\rho}\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s},
   \]
   and convexity gives $\sum_Cm_C^{1+\rho}\le m^{1+\rho}$. The prefix-conditioned slice case is the storage part of \cref{lem:prefix-slice-accounting}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cost and value surrogates]\label{rem:cost-value-surrogates}
   The threshold $T_C=\log^{A_c}n\,F(m_C,R_C)$ is the conservative all-or-nothing version of the same closed-form surrogate idea used by Andoni and Beaglehole~\cite{AB22}. They replace an intractable recursive success probability by a closed-form lower bound in the local min-max game. Here
   \[
      W(m,R)=F(m,R),
      \qquad
      \sigma(m,R)=W(m,R)^{-1}
   \]
   should be viewed as paired work and value surrogates: $W$ is the budget needed to make a child fully searchable, while $\sigma$ is the closed-form value of one unit of unamplified recursive success inside that child.

   This suggests a cleaner integer-materialization target. Instead of opening $C$ only after it has received enough votes to pay for a full child search, let $b_C$ detector votes buy a $b_C/W(m_C,R_C)$ fraction of the child search. If the recursive continuation procedure is made splittable under independent thinning of its query branches, this uses expected work $b_C$ and gives conditional success value
   \[
      \Omega\!\left(\frac{b_C}{W(m_C,R_C)}\right)\log^{-O_c(1)}n
      =
      \Omega(b_C\,\sigma(m_C,R_C))\log^{-O_c(1)}n
   \]
   when the true near point lies in $C$. The current vote threshold is the special case $b_C\simeq W(m_C,R_C)$.

   The posterior mass of a child can then be used as a local payoff multiplier, just as in the hashing game of~\cite{AB22}, but not as an average-case assumption about the unknown near point. The worst-case target should be stated in value form: along the true near-neighbor lineage, either an ordinary continuation path reaches a residual scan, or detector votes deliver enough value $b_C\sigma(m_C,R_C)$ to every required child to preserve inverse-polylogarithmic total success. This may be the right replacement for demanding full integer activation of every child on the lineage.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Prefix-conditioned slice accounting}

The preceding Bellman cancellation applies to deterministic full children. The full-child version is too coarse for the persistent score process: a rare score prefix can isolate an atypical part of a large child, while the full child would require the budget of all its points. The right recursive object is the slice conditioned on the score prefix itself.

\begin{lemma}[Prefix-conditioned detector slice accounting]\label{lem:prefix-slice-accounting}
   Let a parent node contain $m$ points in radius $R$, and let $\mathcal U$ be a prefix-free family of detector prefixes under normalized laws $\mu,\nu_q$ as above. At each prefix $u$, suppose we have disjoint certified slices $C_{u,j}$ with
   \[
      |C_{u,j}|=z_{u,j}\,\mu(u)m,
      \qquad
      R_{u,j}=\lambda_{u,j}R,
   \]
   and at most $J$ slices for each $u$. Then the total query charge of all prefix-conditioned slices satisfies
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\sum_j
      \nu_q(u)^s F(|C_{u,j}|,R_{u,j})
      \le
      J^{1-\rho}F(m,R).
   \]
   Their storage charge satisfies
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\sum_j
      |C_{u,j}|\,F(|C_{u,j}|,R_{u,j})
      \le
      mF(m,R).
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   For the query charge,
   \[
      F(|C_{u,j}|,R_{u,j})
      =
      z_{u,j}^{\rho}\mu(u)^\rho\lambda_{u,j}^{2s}F(m,R).
   \]
   Since $\lambda_{u,j}\le1$ and the slices at a fixed prefix are disjoint,
   \[
      \sum_j z_{u,j}^{\rho}
      \le
      J^{1-\rho}\left(\sum_j z_{u,j}\right)^\rho
      \le
      J^{1-\rho}.
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
      \sum_{u,j}\nu_q(u)^sF(|C_{u,j}|,R_{u,j})
      \le
      J^{1-\rho}F(m,R)
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\mu(u)^\rho\nu_q(u)^s.
   \]
   Hölder's inequality and prefix-freeness give
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\mu(u)^\rho\nu_q(u)^s
      \le
      \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\mu(u)\right)^\rho
      \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\nu_q(u)\right)^s
      \le1.
   \]

   For storage, put $G(m,R)=mF(m,R)$. Then
   \[
      G(|C_{u,j}|,R_{u,j})
      =
      z_{u,j}^{1+\rho}\mu(u)^{1+\rho}\lambda_{u,j}^{2s}G(m,R).
   \]
   Since $\sum_j z_{u,j}^{1+\rho}\le\sum_j z_{u,j}\le1$ and $\sum_u\mu(u)^{1+\rho}\le\sum_u\mu(u)\le1$, summing over all prefixes and slices gives at most $G(m,R)$.
\end{proof}

Thus prefix-conditioned detector accounting avoids the full-child budget obstruction. If a heavy cap is discovered inside a score bucket, the recursive child charged to detector mass is that bucket slice, not the global cluster containing the bucket. When the number of peeled slices per prefix is polylogarithmic, \cref{lem:prefix-slice-accounting} gives the desired parent-potential bound for detector activation. The separate cohort-or-leaf condition below is exactly the assertion that these normalized detector activations couple to the ordinary continuation paths needed for near-neighbor success.

\subsection{Randomized geometric branching}

There is a simpler mechanism for almost-total cap splits. Instead of normalizing a score bucket into a new hard channel, make the geometric split an edge of the same cumulative branching process. The residual child is always searched. The cap child is searched only with a carefully chosen independent probability. This subsection records the deterministic accounting; the adaptive continuation section later supplies the matching score-process estimates.

For this route use the padded radius potential
\[
   \Psi(R)=1+\left(\frac Rr\right)^4,
   \qquad
   F_4(m,R)=m^\rho\Psi(R),
\]
and keep $s=1-\rho$. On the bounded-diameter localized instances used for ANN, $R/r=\log^{O_c(1)}n$, so $F_4$ differs from the ideal $m^\rho$ scale only by a polylogarithmic factor. The fourth power is not essential; it is a convenient way to make every certified cap radius decrease produce a uniform multiplicative drop of $\Psi$ even when $R$ is close to $r$.

\begin{lemma}[Thinned cap-split contraction]\label{lem:thin-cap-contraction}
   Let a score bucket contain $m$ points in a certified ball of radius $R\ge r$. Suppose it is partitioned into a cap child $C$ and a residual child $C^0$ with
   \[
      |C|=zm,
      \qquad
      z\ge1-\delta,
      \qquad
      C\subseteq\Ball(o_C,R_C),
      \qquad
      R_C\le R\sqrt{1-\eta^2}.
   \]
   Put
   \[
      \theta=\frac{\Psi(R_C)}{\Psi(R)}
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      a_C=z^s\theta .
   \]
   If a query always searches $C^0$ and searches $C$ independently with probability $a_C$, then the expected recursive query charge is
   \[
      F_4((1-z)m,R)+a_CF_4(zm,R_C)
      =
      F_4(m,R)\bigl((1-z)^\rho+z\theta^2\bigr).
   \]
   Moreover, for $0<\eta<1/2$ there is an absolute constant $\kappa>0$ such that, whenever $\delta^\rho\le\kappa\eta^2$,
   \[
      F_4((1-z)m,R)+a_CF_4(zm,R_C)
      \le
      (1-\kappa\eta^2)F_4(m,R).
   \]
   The corresponding storage potential $G_4(m,R)=mF_4(m,R)$ is nonincreasing under the deterministic split:
   \[
      G_4((1-z)m,R)+G_4(zm,R_C)\le G_4(m,R).
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The displayed identity follows by substitution:
   \[
      a_CF_4(zm,R_C)
      =
      z^s\theta\cdot z^\rho m^\rho\Psi(R_C)
      =
      z\theta^2F_4(m,R).
   \]
   Since $z^s\le1$ and $\theta\le1$, $a_C$ is a valid probability.

   Write $u=(R/r)^4\ge1$. The radius certificate gives
   \[
      \theta
      \le
      \frac{1+u(1-\eta^2)^2}{1+u}
      =
      1-\frac{u}{1+u}(2\eta^2-\eta^4)
      \le
      1-\kappa_0\eta^2
   \]
   for an absolute $\kappa_0>0$. Hence $\theta^2\le1-\kappa_1\eta^2$ after decreasing the constant. Also $(1-z)^\rho\le\delta^\rho$, so
   \[
      (1-z)^\rho+z\theta^2
      \le
      \delta^\rho+1-\kappa_1\eta^2
      \le
      1-\kappa\eta^2
   \]
   if $\delta^\rho$ is a sufficiently small multiple of $\eta^2$.

   For storage,
   \[
      G_4((1-z)m,R)+G_4(zm,R_C)
      =
      G_4(m,R)\bigl((1-z)^{1+\rho}+z^{1+\rho}\theta\bigr)
      \le
      G_4(m,R),
   \]
   because $\theta\le1$ and $x^{1+\rho}\le x$ for $x\in[0,1]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lineage thinning telescopes]\label{lem:thin-lineage}
   Consider a near-neighbor lineage in a randomized geometric branching tree. Residual edges are followed with probability one. For cap edges $i=1,\ldots,h$, let $z_i\ge1-\delta$ and
   \[
      \theta_i=\frac{\Psi(R_i)}{\Psi(R_{i-1})}.
   \]
   The probability that the query keeps all cap edges of the lineage is
   \[
      \prod_{i=1}^h z_i^s\theta_i
      \ge
      \exp(-2sh\delta)\,
      \frac{\Psi(R_h)}{\Psi(R_0)}
   \]
   whenever $\delta\le1/2$. In particular, if $R_0/r=\log^{O_c(1)}n$ and $h\delta=O(1)$, this probability is at least $\log^{-O_c(1)}n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The $\theta_i$ factors telescope exactly. Also
   \[
      \prod_i z_i^s
      \ge
      (1-\delta)^{sh}
      \ge
      \exp(-2sh\delta)
   \]
   for $\delta\le1/2$. Finally, $\Psi(R_h)\ge1$ and $\Psi(R_0)=\log^{O_c(1)}n$ on a bounded-diameter localized instance.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Prefix-slice lineage survival]\label{asm:prefix-slice-survival}
   In the cumulative score tree with prefix-conditioned recentering slices, fix a true near pair $(p,q)$. With probability at least $\log^{-O_c(1)}n$, there is one cumulative ordinary score path such that the following route succeeds:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item at any reached prefix whose bucket has no heavy compatible cap slice containing $p$, the cap-free residual scan exposes $p$;
      \item otherwise the path follows a certified prefix-conditioned slice containing $p$ and compatible with $q$, retaining the score prefix and continuing in the recentered coordinates;
      \item after finitely many such recenterings, the path reaches a resident scan containing $p$.
   \end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Polylogarithmic prefix-slice process]\label{prop:prefix-slice-process}
   Under \cref{asm:prefix-slice-survival}, and assuming normalized detector activations are materialized with only polylogarithmic concentration overhead, the cumulative score-and-ball process restricted to prefix-conditioned recentering slices has expected query time
   \[
      d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   and expected space
   \[
      dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   Use the radius-budgeted score block of \cref{cor:radius-budgeted-block} at every state. Normalized detector activations for prefix-conditioned child slices are charged by \cref{lem:prefix-slice-accounting}; the polylogarithmic number of peeled slices per prefix contributes only the $J^{1-\rho}=\log^{O_c(1)}n$ factor. Space is bounded by the storage part of \cref{lem:prefix-slice-accounting}. Residual scans are charged to ordinary far candidates as in \cref{lem:slice-residual}, after using the shell lemma to preserve the critical angular gap.

   By \cref{asm:prefix-slice-survival}, one copy reaches either a residual scan or a final resident scan containing the true near neighbor with inverse-polylogarithmic probability. Polylogarithmic amplification and the truncation paragraph in the preliminaries convert the expected bounds to the displayed capped bounds with constant success probability.
\end{proof}

The partition may come from $k$-center, $k$-means, cap proposals, or any other routine; correctness uses only the certified radius. A child is geometrically useful when $R_C=\lambda_C R_u$ with $\lambda_C<1$ bounded away from one, and small or shallow residual sets are stored for resident scan.

In the prefix-slice variant, preprocessing also attaches certified children to score prefixes. For a retained prefix $a$, let $P_u[a]$ be the bucket of resident points reaching that prefix. Heavy cap slices are peeled inside $P_u[a]$ exactly as in the restartable-slice construction, but the recursive child is the conditioned slice $C[a]\subseteq P_u[a]$. These children are not full clusters of $P_u$; their detector activation and storage costs are paid by \cref{lem:prefix-slice-accounting}.

For comparison with the randomized cap-thinning route, we also record a posterior-cohort variant. In that variant, queries keep two populations on the same cumulative score tree.

\emph{Ordinary paths} use the continuation-mass decoder from \cref{lem:continuation-work}. They find isolated near points and residual leaves.

\emph{Detector cohorts} are used only to activate geometric children. They follow the continuation-sampled one-token detector law of \cref{lem:continuation-detector}, not the raw ordinary branching multiplicity. A detector path votes for at most one child: the first child whose posterior reaches the radius threshold $\tau_C=\lambda_C^2$. Child $C$ is activated only after it receives
\[
   T_C=\log^{A_c}n\,|C|^\rho\left(\frac{R_C}{r}\right)^{2s}
\]
votes. The parent starts with
\[
   T_u=A\log^{A_c}n\,|P_u|^\rho\left(\frac{R_u}{r}\right)^{2s}
\]
detector budget. Because each detector path casts at most one vote, activated children consume the existing parent budget rather than multiplying it.

The deterministic accounting is \cref{lem:vote-budget}. The logarithmic factor in $T_u$ is chosen large enough that, for every child whose normalized detector mass is a constant multiple above its activation threshold, Chernoff bounds give the required votes simultaneously over all polynomially many stored child IDs. Children below threshold may fail to activate; the cohort-or-leaf condition is exactly the near-neighbor assertion that this does not strand the true lineage without an ordinary residual path.

When a query enters an activated child, the cumulative score prefix is retained. Recentered coordinates only change future score offsets; no new independent spherical decoder is started.

\begin{assumption}[Route cohort-or-leaf]\label{asm:cohort-or-leaf}
   Fix a true near pair $(p,q)$ and let
   \[
      u_0,u_1,\ldots,u_h
   \]
   be the deterministic hierarchy lineage containing $p$, stopping when the resident block containing $p$ is scanned. Write $C_i=P_{u_{i+1}}\subseteq P_{u_i}$ and
   \[
      \tau_i=\left(\frac{R_{u_{i+1}}}{R_{u_i}}\right)^2.
   \]
   In one shared cumulative score tree, the following route event has probability at least $\log^{-O_c(1)}n$:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item an ordinary path exposes $p$ in a searchable residual leaf before the lineage needs another activation; or
      \item for every $i<h$, detector paths generate enough first-hit votes to activate $C_i$, and the query reaches the final resident scan containing $p$.
   \end{enumerate}
   A useful local inequality to prove en route is, in the notation of the hand-off,
   \[
      \tau_C^{-\rho}\mathcal A_C^{\mathrm{hit}}
      +
      \mathcal L_C^{\mathrm{free}}
      \ge
      \log^{-O_c(1)}n.
   \]
   The assumption used here is stronger than this inequality level by level: the local quantities must telescope, or be coupled by a second-moment argument, into the displayed route event. Independent inverse-polylogarithmic success probabilities at the $h$ levels would not suffice.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Polylogarithmic score-and-ball process]\label{thm:polylog-process}
   Under \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf}, the cumulative score-and-ball process has expected query time
   \[
      d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   and expected space
   \[
      dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
   The radius-budgeted continuation block in \cref{cor:radius-budgeted-block} gives the parent potential
   \[
      F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2(1-\rho)}
   \]
   directly as score-tree work, with only polylogarithmic overhead and inverse-polylogarithmic near survival. The shell lemma preserves the critical Euclidean angular gap up to inverse-polylogarithmic slack, which is absorbed into the displayed logarithmic factors.

   For every certified child, \cref{lem:first-hit} bounds the normalized detector mass that can activate the child, and the choice $\tau_C=\lambda_C^2$ converts that activation cost into $\alpha_C F(m,R)$ by the Bellman cancellation. In the implemented integer process, \cref{lem:vote-budget} gives the stronger realized statement: an opened child has already received its recursive query budget from parent detector votes. Space is controlled by disjointness and convexity of $x^{1+\rho}$.

   Along the near-neighbor route, \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf} supplies an inverse-polylogarithmic probability for the entire lineage, not one independent loss per scale: either an ordinary path finds the near point before the next activation is needed, or detector cohorts activate every certified child containing it until a resident scan is reached. Polylogarithmic amplification gives constant success probability, and truncation/rebuilding converts expected bounds to capped bounds as in the preliminaries.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the main theorem}

The main theorem follows from the restartable slice milestone \cref{prop:slice-two-thirds}, applied after the projected bounded-diameter localization of \cref{lem:localization} and inverse-polylogarithmic shell decompositions. Expected bounds are converted to capped bounds by the truncation paragraph in the preliminaries.

The cumulative one-tree constructions below are stronger target routes. The randomized cap-thinning route gives the cleanest path to the $1/3$ and polylogarithmic rungs, but its global mixed stopping-frontier operator is kept as an explicit assumption rather than used in \cref{thm:main}.

\section{The candidate one-tree algorithm}

For clarity, we summarize the candidate cumulative data structure as a single tree with two edge types. The one-step accounting is proved below; the global non-restarted work bound is the mixed operator isolated in \cref{asm:mixed-continuation-operator}.

\subsection{Preprocessing}

\begin{enumerate}
   \item Apply the projected bounded-diameter localization of \cref{lem:localization} and build the associated polylogarithmic family of localized instances.
   \item Create a root state $(P,o,R)$ for each localized instance.
   \item At every nonterminal state, divide the resident points into radial shells of inverse-polylogarithmic relative width.
   \item Build the cumulative Gaussian score tree with continuation-mass pruning and effective budget determined by $F_4(m,R)=m^\rho\Psi(R)$.
   \item At retained score prefixes, run the almost-total cap test. When a $2\eta$-cap contains all but an $\varepsilon$ fraction of the current bucket, store an $\eta$-cap child, certify its actual radius
      \[
         R_C=\max_{p\in C}\|p-o_C\|_2,
      \]
      and keep the complement as the residual child.
   \item Store the cap-following probability
      \[
         a_C=z_C^s\frac{\Psi(R_C)}{\Psi(R_u)}
      \]
      for the cap edge; residual edges have probability one.
   \item Stop once the node radius or node cardinality is small enough for a resident scan.
\end{enumerate}

Correctness does not depend on the cap-finder heuristic used in preprocessing, because every proposed child is verified by scanning the bucket and computing its certified radius. If no verified almost-total cap exists at an accepting leaf, the leaf is scanned.

\subsection{Query}

A query traverses the cumulative score tree using continuation-mass pruning. At a verified cap split it always follows the residual child and independently follows the cap child with probability $a_C$. Both children retain the cumulative score prefix and only update the coordinate offset for future score increments. At cap-free accepting leaves and resident blocks, the query scans candidates and verifies true Euclidean distances.

At all scan points, candidates are verified in the original metric. Therefore approximate clustering can affect performance but not correctness.

\subsection{Simplicity and implementation}

The filtering work remains level-synchronous. At one checkpoint, all active score paths can be advanced by a batched matrix multiplication, followed by continuation-mass pruning, cap-test lookup, randomized child thinning, and resident scans. Recentering updates stored coordinate offsets; it does not start a fresh score tree.

The data structure stores only the tree metadata:
\[
   (\text{score seed},\text{bucket IDs},o_C,R_C,z_C,a_C,\text{resident block}).
\]
The same score kernels are used before and after recentering; only the coordinate offset and active bucket ID change.

\section{Why the one-tree route could remove compounded overhead}

The restart analysis pays for the same loss once per geometric level. The cumulative process is designed to avoid that multiplication in two ways.

\paragraph{The score process is global.}
Continuation-mass pruning controls the total number of explored prefixes in one cumulative tree. The budgeted form in \cref{cor:radius-budgeted-block} lets a node spend the full radius-weighted potential $F(m,R)$ inside the same score block by using the effective budget $N=m(R/r)^{2(1-\rho)/\rho}$. Recentered children inherit the score prefix and only change the coordinate offset for future increments. If the mixed stopping-frontier operator holds, there is no per-child independent spherical failure penalty in the target process.

\paragraph{Geometry is paid by randomized thinning.}
For an almost-total cap of relative size $z$ and radius ratio encoded by $\theta=\Psi(R_C)/\Psi(R)$, the query follows the cap child with probability $z^s\theta$ and always follows the residual. The expected recursive potential is
\[
   F_4((1-z)m,R)+z^s\theta F_4(zm,R_C)
   =
   F_4(m,R)\bigl((1-z)^\rho+z\theta^2\bigr),
\]
which contracts whenever $(1-z)^\rho\ll\eta^2$. The nontrivial part is summing these charges over all random cap-discovery frontiers of the branching decoder. Prefix-freeness alone is not enough, because the continuation decoder is a branching set of retained prefixes rather than one normalized path.

\paragraph{Terminal scans are low-correlation scans.}
At a cap-free accepting leaf, the query direction is not an almost-total cap. An inverse-polylogarithmic fraction of the bucket therefore has correlation at most $2\eta$ with the query, and the whole scan is charged to those ordinary low-correlation accepting-leaf collisions.

\section{An exponent-improvement ladder}

The proof program need not jump directly from the restart construction to the polylogarithmic target. A useful intermediate calculus is the following. Suppose the logarithm of the nonpolynomial overhead has the form
\[
   O_c\!\left(\eta^p L+\eta^{-q}L^\alpha\right),
   \qquad L=\log n,
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
   \item $L^\alpha$ is the loss of one score block;
   \item $\eta^{-q}$ is the number of independently paid geometric phases;
   \item $\eta^p L$ is the exponent cost of geometric slack.
\end{itemize}
Balancing the two terms gives
\[
   \eta=L^{-(1-\alpha)/(p+q)}
\]
and overhead exponent
\[
   \theta(p,q,\alpha)
   =
   \frac{q+p\alpha}{p+q},
   \qquad
   \exp(O_c(L^\theta)).
\]

\begin{table}[t]
   \centering
   \caption{Useful milestones for reducing the nonpolynomial overhead.}
   \label{tab:ladder}
   \begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
      \toprule
      Milestone & New ingredient & Parameters & Exponent \\
      \midrule
      Restart & $k^{1/3}$ spherical loss & $(1,2,1/3)$ & $7/9$ \\
      Score-only & polylog spherical decoder & $(1,2,0)$ & $2/3$ \\
      One geometric gain & fewer phases or quadratic slack & $(1,1,0)$ or $(2,2,0)$ & $1/2$ \\
      Randomized one tree & cube-root geometric frontier bound & no restart phases; $\eta=L^{-2/3}$ & $1/3$ \\
      Adaptive continuation & all-scale mixed continuation operator & $\eta\asymp(\log\log n)/L$ & $\polylog$ \\
      Bellman cohorts & no independent phase payment & no $\eta^{-q}$ term & $\polylog$ \\
      \bottomrule
   \end{tabular}
\end{table}

This ladder is useful for stress testing. The continuation-mass spherical theorem already moves the restart architecture from exponent $7/9$ to roughly $2/3$. The randomized one-tree route below removes the independent phase count for almost-total cap splits; a cube-root-scale geometric frontier inequality gives a conditional $1/3$ rung, while proving the same continuation operator uniformly down to $\eta\asymp(\log\log n)/L$ would give the polylogarithmic rung. The Bellman cohort mechanism is the broader endpoint for arbitrary certified children: if cohort-or-leaf holds, recursive geometry is paid by the parent potential and the $\eta^{-q}$ term disappears rather than being optimized.

\section{A restartable slice route to the 2/3 rung}

There is a more concrete way to reach the $2/3$ milestone without proving the persistent route cohort-or-leaf lemma. The point is to restart deliberately after every recentering, but to recurse only on leaf-conditioned slices. This avoids the obstruction in which one atypical score prefix from a large child would have to fund a recursive search of the whole child.

Fix one spherical stage on a shell in a ball $\Ball(o,R)$. Let $\eta>0$ and set
\[
   \tau=\log^{-A_c} n.
\]
Run the continuation-mass spherical decoder and regard its accepting buckets as hard leaves for this stage only. At each leaf bucket $U$, repeatedly search for a heavy cap of margin $2\eta$, meaning a direction $w$ such that
\[
   \left|\left\{x\in U:\ip{(x-o)/R}{w}\ge 2\eta\right\}\right|
   \ge \tau |U|.
\]
When such a cap is found, store an $\eta$-margin slice
\[
   D[U,v]=\left\{x\in U:\ip{(x-o)/R}{v}\ge \eta\right\}
\]
as a certified child. Since every point in it has inner product at least $\eta R$ with direction $v$,
\[
   D[U,v]\subseteq \Ball(o+\eta Rv,\,R\sqrt{1-\eta^2}),
\]
and the implementation certifies the actual child radius by computing the maximum distance to the proposed center. After no heavy $2\eta$-cap remains, the residual bucket is scanned. Every certified child starts a fresh spherical stage in its own coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Residual scan after slice peeling]\label{lem:slice-residual}
   In one restartable slice stage, scanning the final cap-free residual leaf buckets costs only a $(1+O(\tau))$ factor more than the ordinary far-candidate count of the spherical decoder with far correlation $2\eta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Fix a query $q$ reaching a final residual bucket $U$, and let
   \[
      E_q=\left\{x\in U:\ip{(x-o)/R}{(q-o)/R}>2\eta\right\}.
   \]
   If $|E_q|\ge\tau |U|$, then the query direction itself defines a heavy $2\eta$-cap that should have been peeled. Hence $|E_q|<\tau |U|$. Therefore
   \[
      |U|\le \frac{1}{1-\tau}\,|U\setminus E_q|.
   \]
   Every point in $U\setminus E_q$ has correlation at most $2\eta$ with the query direction, so the spherical far-candidate bound controls its expected contribution. Since $(1-\tau)^{-1}=1+O(\tau)$, scanning all of $U$ has the same bound up to this factor.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tuple cap finder]\label{lem:tuple-cap-finder}
   Let $U$ be a finite set of unit vectors, let $0<\eta<1/10$, and suppose some $2\eta$-cap
   \[
      C=\{x\in U:\ip{x}{w}\ge2\eta\}
   \]
   has size at least $\tau |U|$. There is a randomized proposal procedure which, using
   \[
      \tau^{-O(\eta^{-2})}\log(1/\delta)
   \]
   tuple proposals, finds with probability at least $1-\delta$ a direction $v$ whose $\eta$-cap contains at least $\Omega(\eta\tau)|U|$ points.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Let $X$ be uniform on $C$ and let $m_C=\E X$. Since every point of $C$ has projection at least $2\eta$ on $w$,
   \[
      \|m_C\|\ge \ip{m_C}{w}\ge2\eta.
   \]
   Sample
   \[
      k=\lceil C_0\eta^{-2}\rceil
   \]
   points from $U$ with replacement. With probability at least $\tau^k$, all sampled points lie in $C$. Conditioned on this event, their empirical mean $\widehat m$ satisfies
   \[
      \E\|\widehat m-m_C\|^2\le \frac1k.
   \]
   Taking $C_0$ large and applying Markov's inequality gives
   \[
      \|\widehat m-m_C\|\le\eta/4
   \]
   with probability at least a fixed positive constant. Let $v=\widehat m/\|\widehat m\|$. On this event,
   \[
      \ip{m_C}{v}
      \ge
      \|\widehat m\|-\|\widehat m-m_C\|
      \ge
      \|m_C\|-2\|\widehat m-m_C\|
      \ge
      3\eta/2.
   \]
   Therefore, for $X$ uniform on $C$,
   \[
      \E\ip{X}{v}\ge3\eta/2.
   \]
   Since $\ip{X}{v}\le1$, at least an $\Omega(\eta)$ fraction of $C$ must satisfy $\ip{X}{v}\ge\eta$. Hence the $\eta$-cap in direction $v$ contains $\Omega(\eta\tau)|U|$ points.

   A single random tuple is therefore successful with probability at least $c\tau^k$ for a constant $c>0$. Repeating $O(\tau^{-k}\log(1/\delta))$ times and verifying each proposed $\eta$-cap by scanning $U$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Leaf-conditioned slice budget]\label{lem:slice-budget}
   Let a hard leaf $U$ have data fraction $\mu=|U|/m$, and let $\nu_q(U)\in\{0,1\}$ indicate whether query $q$ reaches it. Suppose the peeled slices inside $U$ have relative sizes
   \[
      z_j=\frac{|C_j|}{|U|}
   \]
   and certified radii $R_j=\lambda_jR$. With $\rho=\rho_c+O_c(\eta)$ and $s=1-\rho$,
   \[
      F(m,R)\,\mu^\rho\nu_q(U)^s\,z_j^\rho\lambda_j^{2s}
      =
      F(|C_j|,R_j)\nu_q(U)^s.
   \]
   If at most $J$ slices are peeled from $U$, then summing over the slices loses at most a factor $J^{1-\rho}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The identity follows by substituting $|C_j|=z_j\mu m$ and $R_j=\lambda_jR$ into
   \[
      F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}.
   \]
   The slices are disjoint inside $U$, so $\sum_j z_j\le1$. Concavity of $x^\rho$ gives
   \[
      \sum_{j=1}^J z_j^\rho\le J^{1-\rho}\left(\sum_{j=1}^J z_j\right)^\rho\le J^{1-\rho}.
   \]
\end{proof}

By \cref{lem:tuple-cap-finder}, every successful peeled slice removes an $\Omega(\eta\tau)$ fraction of the current bucket. A bucket of size at most $n$ therefore produces only
\[
   J=O((\eta\tau)^{-1}\log n)=\log^{O_c(1)}n
\]
sequential slices. Thus \cref{lem:slice-budget} costs only a polylogarithmic factor per restartable stage.
The rejected tuple proposals from \cref{lem:tuple-cap-finder} are preprocessing-only and are not stored. If preprocessing time is tracked, their logarithmic overhead per bucket is
\[
   O(\eta^{-2}\log(1/\tau)+\log\log n)
   =
   O_c(\eta^{-2}\log\log n),
\]
which is below the final $2/3$ overhead after the choice of $\eta$ in \cref{prop:slice-two-thirds}.

\begin{proposition}[Restartable slice milestone]\label{prop:slice-two-thirds}
   For fixed $c>1$, the restartable slice construction gives a worst-case Euclidean ANN structure with
   \[
      Q(n,d)
      =
      d\,n^{\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(
         O_c\!\left((\log n)^{2/3}(\log\log n)^{2/3}\right)
      \right)
   \]
   and
   \[
      S(n,d)
      =
      dn+n^{1+\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(
         O_c\!\left((\log n)^{2/3}(\log\log n)^{2/3}\right)
      \right).
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   First consider one restartable stage. The continuation-mass decoder supplies the hard accepting leaves and has expected path work, far-candidate occurrences, storage, and inverse near-pair success all bounded by the ideal spherical powers times $\log^{O_c(1)}n$, by \cref{lem:continuation-work} and \cref{cor:spherical-survival}. Amplifying the stage to failure probability $1/\log^{O_c(1)}n$ costs another polylogarithmic factor.

   The final residual scan is controlled by \cref{lem:slice-residual}. Heavy cap discovery is handled by \cref{lem:tuple-cap-finder}; rejected tuple proposals affect preprocessing only, and accepted slices are verified before storage. By \cref{lem:slice-budget}, the recursive budgets of all slices peeled from a leaf cost only a further polylogarithmic factor. A query follows every certified child ball from its reached leaf that is compatible with the query, which adds at most the same polylogarithmic factor because each leaf has only $J=\log^{O_c(1)}n$ accepted slices. Thus one stage has polylogarithmic overhead in query work, space, and inverse success probability relative to the ideal radius-weighted power-law budget.

   Put
   \[
      L=\log n,
      \qquad
      \ell=\log\log n.
   \]
   By \cref{lem:rho-stability} and \cref{lem:shell}, using far correlation $2\eta$ and inverse-polynomial shell widths changes the balanced exponent from $\rho_c$ to
   \[
      \rho_c+O_c(\eta),
   \]
   contributing
   \[
      \exp(O_c(\eta L))
   \]
   to the overhead.

   Each accepted slice has certified radius at most $R\sqrt{1-\eta^2}\le R(1-\eta^2/2)$ for small enough $\eta$. After bounded-diameter localization, the relevant ratio $R_0/r$ is at most $\log^{O_c(1)}n$. Hence a near-neighbor lineage has at most
   \[
      H=O_c\!\left(\eta^{-2}\log\frac{R_0}{r}\right)
      =
      O_c(\eta^{-2}\ell)
   \]
   restarts before a resident scan. Paying an independent polylogarithmic stage overhead at every restart contributes
   \[
      \log^{O_c(H)}n
      =
      \exp(O_c(\eta^{-2}\ell^2)).
   \]
   Therefore the logarithm of the nonpolynomial overhead is
   \[
      O_c(\eta L+\eta^{-2}\ell^2).
   \]
   Balance the two terms by taking
   \[
      \eta=\Theta_c\!\left(\left(\frac{\ell^2}{L}\right)^{1/3}\right).
   \]
   Then both terms are $O_c(L^{2/3}\ell^{2/3})$, proving the displayed bounds. \Cref{fig:balance} shows the balance.
\end{proof}

\section{A randomized one-tree route to the 1/3 rung}

The restartable construction still turns each recentered slice into a fresh spherical instance. The following variant does not restart or re-channelize. Geometric splits are randomized edges inside one cumulative score tree, exactly as in \cref{lem:thin-cap-contraction}. This removes the $\eta^{-2}$ independent phase payment from \cref{prop:slice-two-thirds}; what remains is a mixed stopping-frontier work inequality for the branching continuation process.

Fix parameters $\eta,\delta>0$ and put
\[
   \varepsilon=\frac{\eta\delta}{16}.
\]
Inside a score bucket $U$ on the current shell, call a direction $w$ almost total if its $2\eta$-cap contains all but an $\varepsilon$ fraction of $U$. If such a direction exists at a retained prefix, preprocessing stores an $\eta$-cap child containing all but a $\delta$ fraction of $U$ and leaves the rest as the residual child. If no almost-total direction exists at an intermediate prefix, the prefix simply keeps advancing the continuation-mass score process. A cap-free bucket is scanned only when it is an accepting leaf of the current score block, or when it is already small enough to be a resident block. This is the point that lets cap-free scans be charged to ordinary low-correlation accepting-leaf collisions.

\begin{lemma}[Hinge certificate for almost-total caps]\label{lem:almost-total-cap-finder}
   Let $U$ be a finite set of unit vectors. Suppose there is a unit vector $w$ such that
   \[
      \left|\{x\in U:\ip{x}{w}\ge2\eta\}\right|
      \ge
      (1-\varepsilon)|U|,
      \qquad
      \varepsilon\le\frac{\eta\delta}{16}.
   \]
   Define the empirical hinge loss over the unit ball by
   \[
      L(v)=\frac1{|U|}\sum_{x\in U}(2\eta-\ip{x}{v})_+,
      \qquad
      \|v\|_2\le1.
   \]
   Any $v$ with $L(v)\le4\varepsilon$ has an $\eta$-cap containing at least $(1-\delta)|U|$ points. Such a certificate is checkable by scanning $U$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The witness $w$ has loss at most $(1+2\eta)\varepsilon\le2\varepsilon$, since only the exceptional points contribute and each contribution is at most $1+2\eta$. Hence an approximate hinge minimizer with loss at most $4\varepsilon$ is enough.

   If $\ip{x}{v}<\eta$, then $(2\eta-\ip{x}{v})_+\ge\eta$. Therefore the fraction of points outside the $\eta$-cap of $v$ is at most $L(v)/\eta\le4\varepsilon/\eta\le\delta/4$. Normalizing $v$ can only increase positive projections when $\|v\|_2\le1$, so the same cap certificate holds for the stored unit direction.
\end{proof}

Thus a complete almost-total cap search can be implemented by minimizing a convex empirical hinge loss to inverse-polylogarithmic accuracy and then verifying the resulting cap size directly. Conversely, if no almost-total $2\eta$-cap exists, then for every query direction at least an $\varepsilon$ fraction of the terminal bucket has correlation at most $2\eta$ with the query. Scanning the whole terminal bucket is therefore charged to low-correlation points with only an $\varepsilon^{-1}$ loss.

In the one-tree route we take the residual allowance at the largest scale compatible with deterministic cap-split contraction,
\[
   \delta=\delta_0\eta^{2/\rho_c},
\]
where $\delta_0=\delta_0(c)>0$ is a sufficiently small constant. This improves the polynomial logarithmic factors in the residual scan and cap-finder accuracy compared with the safer but unnecessary choice $\eta^{3/\rho_c}$. Since $\rho_c<1$ for every fixed $c>1$, the lineage-thinning condition $H\delta=o(1)$ still holds for both the cube-root and polylogarithmic parameter choices below.

The information-time tube calculation is still a useful sanity check. For a fixed predictable mixed lineage of total information $D$, exponential tilting makes the log-likelihood fluctuation a centered Gaussian martingale with quadratic variation $2D$. Keeping it in a tube of width $w$ costs $\exp(-O(D/w^2))$, while the likelihood ratio varies by $\exp(O(w))$; optimizing $w=D^{1/3}$ gives a fixed-lineage survival loss $\exp(-O(D^{1/3}))$.

We do not use that tube estimate as a formal hypothesis. The adaptive continuation lemma proved in \cref{lem:adaptive-continuation} is stronger for this algorithm: with continuation-mass pruning, predictable mixed score schedules have inverse-polylogarithmic near survival. Thus the cube-root checkpoint below has one conditional ingredient, namely the mixed stopping-frontier work inequality.

\begin{lemma}[Cap-free terminal scan accounting]\label{lem:cap-free-scan-accounting}
   Work inside one bounded-diameter localized state with $m$ points in radius $R$, and choose the score budget $N$ by
   \[
      N^{\rho_c}=F_4(m,R).
   \]
   Suppose a query scans a prefix-free family $\mathcal L(q)$ of cap-free accepting leaves, and in every scanned bucket at least an $\alpha$ fraction of the resident points have correlation at most $2\eta$ with the query direction. Then the expected score work and terminal scans satisfy
   \[
      \E\left[
         \mathrm{score\ work}
         +
         \sum_{u\in\mathcal L(q)} |P[u]|
      \right]
      \le
      \alpha^{-1}
      \exp(O_c(\eta\log n))
      F_4(m,R)\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The score-work term is at most $F_4(m,R)\log^{O_c(1)}n$ by the budgeted continuation work bound, and $\alpha\le1$.

   For a scanned bucket $U$, let $U_{\mathrm{lo}}$ be its points whose shell-normalized correlation with the query is at most $2\eta$. By assumption, $|U|\le\alpha^{-1}|U_{\mathrm{lo}}|$. Summing over scanned leaves, the scan count is at most $\alpha^{-1}$ times the number of low-correlation accepting candidate occurrences.

   By \cref{lem:rho-stability}, these low-correlation pairs have spherical exponent at most $\rho_c+O_c(\eta)$. The budgeted far-candidate bound with depth chosen from $N$ gives expected low-correlation accepting occurrences at most
   \[
      mN^{-(1-\rho_c-O_c(\eta))}\log^{O_c(1)}N
      \le
      N^{\rho_c}\exp(O_c(\eta\log N))\log^{O_c(1)}N.
   \]
   In bounded-diameter localized states, $N\le n\log^{O_c(1)}n$, so $\log N=O_c(\log n)$ and $N^{\rho_c}=F_4(m,R)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Cube-root geometric frontier inequality]\label{asm:mixed-operator}
   In the randomized one-tree process with almost-total cap splitting at the cube-root parameters
   \[
      L=\log n,
      \qquad
      \eta=L^{-2/3},
      \qquad
      \delta=\delta_0\eta^{2/\rho_c},
   \]
   Let $\mathcal G(q)$ be the collection of cap children reached with their thinning probabilities included. Let
   \[
      \alpha=\Theta(\eta\delta),
      \qquad
      F_4(m,R)=m^{\rho_c}\Psi(R).
   \]
   The thinned recursive-potential frontier satisfies
   \[
      \E\left[
         \sum_{v\in\mathcal G(q)} a_vF_4(|P[v]|,R_v)
      \right]
      \le
      \alpha^{-1}
      \exp(O_c(\eta L))
      F_4(n,R_0)\log^{O_c(1)}n,
   \]
   where $a_v$ is the product of cap-edge thinning probabilities on the route to $v$.
\end{assumption}

The terminal term is charged by \cref{lem:cap-free-scan-accounting} to the $\alpha$ fraction of low-correlation points in every cap-free bucket. The geometric term should be controlled by the contraction in \cref{lem:thin-cap-contraction}. The unresolved point is to make this argument commute with the random prefix-free stopping frontiers, again without restarting the score clock. The all-parameter version needed for the polylogarithmic endpoint is isolated later as \cref{asm:mixed-continuation-operator}.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional randomized one-tree \(1/3\) rung]\label{prop:one-third}
   Assume \cref{asm:mixed-operator}. For fixed $c>1$, the randomized one-tree construction gives worst-case Euclidean ANN bounds
   \[
      Q(n,d)
      =
      d\,n^{\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(O_c((\log n)^{1/3})\right)
      \log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   and
   \[
      S(n,d)
      =
      dn+n^{1+\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(O_c((\log n)^{1/3})\right)
      \log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   Work inside one bounded-diameter localized instance, so $R_0/r=\log^{O_c(1)}n$, and put
   \[
      L=\log n,
      \qquad
      \eta=L^{-2/3},
      \qquad
      \delta=\delta_0\eta^{2/\rho_c}.
   \]
   Choose $\delta_0$ small enough that $\delta^{\rho_c}$ is below the constant required in \cref{lem:thin-cap-contraction}. Then every almost-total cap split satisfies the contraction hypothesis. The storage potential is nonincreasing under the deterministic partition, and
   \[
      F_4(n,R_0)=n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]

   A cap edge decreases radius by a factor at most $\sqrt{1-\eta^2}$, so a lineage has at most
   \[
      H=O_c\!\left(\eta^{-2}\log\frac{R_0}{r}\right)
      =
      O_c(\eta^{-2}\log\log n)
   \]
   cap edges before the radius is terminal. With the chosen $\delta$,
   \[
      H\delta
      =
      O_c(\eta^{-2+2/\rho_c}\log\log n)
      =
      o(1).
   \]
   Therefore \cref{lem:thin-lineage} shows that all randomized cap-edge thinning along the true near lineage costs only $\log^{-O_c(1)}n$ success probability. Residual edges are followed with probability one.

   \Cref{lem:adaptive-continuation} supplies a common near score path with probability
   \[
      \log^{-O_c(1)}n.
   \]
   The score work and cap-free scans are bounded by \cref{lem:cap-free-scan-accounting}; the recursive cap-frontier term is bounded by \cref{asm:mixed-operator}. Since
   \[
      \eta L=L^{1/3},
      \qquad
      \alpha^{-1}=(\eta\delta)^{-1}=\eta^{-1-2/\rho_c}\delta_0^{-1}=\log^{O_c(1)}n,
   \]
   one copy has expected work and inverse success overhead
   \[
      \exp(O_c(L^{1/3}))\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   over the $n^{\rho_c}$ scale. Amplifying by this factor and applying the truncation paragraph in the preliminaries gives the displayed query bound. The space bound follows from the storage part of \cref{lem:thin-cap-contraction}, the same global score-clock overhead, and the bounded-diameter localization factors.
\end{proof}

\section{A continuation-mass route to the polylog rung}

The cube-root checkpoint in \cref{prop:one-third} is not caused by the score survival analysis. The continuation-mass proof from the spherical section extends to predictable mixed score schedules, so the score side has only inverse-polylogarithmic loss. Cap-free leaves are charged to low-correlation collisions by \cref{lem:cap-free-scan-accounting}. The remaining work accounting is the thinned cap-split potential over random first-hit frontiers. If that mixed frontier inequality is proved uniformly at smaller $\eta$, the same randomized one-tree algorithm reaches the polylogarithmic endpoint.

\begin{definition}[Regular adaptive score schedule]\label{def:regular-adaptive-score}
   Fix a compact interval $I=[a_-,a_+]\subset(-1,1)$ and a constant branching factor $B\ge2$. A regular adaptive score schedule of depth $K$ is a rooted score tree in which every score node has $B$ score children, and before an edge $e$ is sampled its coordinate system and a value $a_e\in I$ are measurable with respect to the previous score prefix. Conditional on the past, the two score increments of a fixed near pair are centered Gaussian with covariance
   \[
      \begin{pmatrix}1&a_e\\ a_e&1\end{pmatrix}.
   \]
   The edge threshold is
   \[
      t_e=\sqrt{(1+a_e)\log B}.
   \]
   A leaf accepts the near pair if both accumulated scores lie in their unit endpoint windows around
   \[
      T_v=\sum_{e\preceq v}t_e .
   \]
   Prefixes are retained by the exact one-point continuation mass for the remaining adaptive schedule, with cutoff $K^{-A}$.
\end{definition}

The exact calibration $t_e^2/(1+a_e)=\log B$ is only for the proof. In the ANN application one uses a certified lower correlation and absorbs the resulting $O_c(\eta)$ perturbation through \cref{lem:rho-stability}; the estimates below are uniform on compact neighborhoods of the critical parameters.

\begin{lemma}[Adaptive continuation-mass survival]\label{lem:adaptive-continuation}
   For every compact $I\subset(-1,1)$ there is $A=A_I$ such that every regular adaptive score schedule of depth $K$ has the following property. For any fixed near pair following any predictable mixed geometric lineage, continuation-mass pruning produces a common accepting score leaf with probability at least
   \[
      K^{-O_I(1)}.
   \]
   The expected retained-prefix work and stored one-point occurrences are also bounded by the corresponding one-point accepting-leaf expectations times $K^{O_I(1)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The work statement is identical to \cref{lem:continuation-work}. At any prefix, the continuation mass is the exact conditional expected number of accepting descendants for one point. Summing it over all prefixes at one score time counts accepting descendants, and retained prefixes have continuation mass at least $K^{-A}$.

   We prove the survival statement by the same first- and second-moment argument as \cref{lem:good-first-moment} and \cref{lem:tame-second-moment}. The only change is that the Gaussian increments form a predictable triangular array. All constants below are uniform for $a_e\in I$.

   Fix a score leaf $v$. Tilt each edge $e\preceq v$ by
   \[
      \theta_e=\frac{t_e}{1+a_e}(1,1).
   \]
   Under the tilted measure both coordinates have mean $t_e$, and
   \[
      \frac12\theta_e^\top
      \begin{pmatrix}1&a_e\\ a_e&1\end{pmatrix}
      \theta_e
      =
      \frac{t_e^2}{1+a_e}
      =
      \log B.
   \]
   Hence, on the endpoint unit square for $v$,
   \[
      B^K\frac{dP}{dQ_v}=\exp(O_I(1)).
   \]
   Under $Q_v$, the centered endpoint covariance is a sum of $K$ uniformly elliptic two-dimensional covariance matrices, so the endpoint unit square has probability $\Theta_I(K^{-1})$.

   The continuation lower barriers are one-point barriers written in the remaining conditional variance. Since every remaining variance increment is bounded above and below by constants depending only on $I$, the bridge estimate used in \cref{lem:good-first-moment} applies verbatim: far from the endpoint the barrier is linearly below the tilted mean, and during the final $O_I(\log K)$ variance units it is below by $\Omega_I(\sqrt{A r\log K})$. Taking $A$ large makes lower-barrier failure probability $o(1)$.

   Add the same analytical upper barrier in the centered common coordinate
   \[
      C_e=\frac{(X_e-t_e)+(Y_e-t_e)}{\sqrt{2(1+a_e)}}.
   \]
   These increments are standard normal under the tilted law, independently of $a_e$. For a Gaussian bridge of length $K$, the probability of staying below a fixed constant upper barrier, conditioned on a bounded endpoint, is $K^{-O_I(1)}$. Therefore the expected number $Z$ of adaptive tame-good leaves satisfies
   \[
      \E Z\ge K^{-O_I(1)}.
   \]

   For the second moment, decompose ordered pairs of score leaves by their lowest common ancestor after $j$ score edges. There are at most $B^{2K-j}$ such ordered pairs. Conditional on the predictable schedule up to the split, the two suffix schedules are independent after the split. The edgewise identity $t_e^2/(1+a_e)=\log B$ cancels the exponential growth from the $B^{2K-j}$ leaf pairs exactly as in the homogeneous calculation. The shared centered common coordinate is capped by the upper barrier at the split, so its integral contributes at most
   \[
      \frac{C_I}{\sqrt{j+1}\,(K-j)}
   \]
   after cancellation. The difference endpoint pair has covariance determinant $\Theta_I(K(K-j))$, hence density at most $C_I/\sqrt{K(K-j)}$. Thus the split depth $j<K$ contributes at most
   \[
      \frac{C_I K^{O_I(1)}}{\sqrt{j+1}\,(K-j)^{3/2}\sqrt K}.
   \]
   The sum over $j<K$ is $K^{O_I(1)}$, and the diagonal contribution is bounded by the first-moment endpoint calculation. Hence
   \[
      \E Z^2\le K^{O_I(1)}.
   \]
   Paley--Zygmund gives $\Pr[Z>0]\ge K^{-O_I(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Mixed continuation operator]\label{asm:mixed-continuation-operator}
   In one randomized continuation score block at a state with $m$ points in radius $R$, choose the effective budget $N$ so that
   \[
      N^\rho=F_4(m,R)=m^\rho\Psi(R).
   \]
   Let $\mathcal G(q)$ be the prefix-free collection of first geometric splits reached by query $q$, including residual children with query weight $1$ and cap children with query weight $a_C=z^s\Psi(R_C)/\Psi(R)$. For
   \[
      \delta=\delta_0\eta^{2/\rho_c},
      \qquad
      \alpha=\Theta(\eta\delta),
      \qquad
      L=\log n,
   \]
   the weighted recursive child potentials satisfy, for an absolute $\kappa>0$,
   \[
      \E\left[
         \sum_{v\in\mathcal G(q)} w_vF_4(|P[v]|,R_v)
      \right]
      \le
      (1-\kappa\eta^2)F_4(m,R),
   \]
   unless no geometric split is reached, in which case the left-hand side is zero.
   Here $w_v$ is the query weight of the child at its first split prefix.
\end{assumption}

The score-work and terminal-scan terms are handled by \cref{lem:cap-free-scan-accounting}. The remaining point is the geometric first-split sum. For a single split prefix the contraction in \cref{lem:thin-cap-contraction} is exact. What still needs proof is a power estimate for the random first-split frontier of the branching decoder, strong enough to replace the invalid normalized-path inequality
\[
   \sum_{u\in\mathcal A}\nu_q(u)\mu(u)^\rho\le 1.
\]
That inequality would be immediate for a single sampled path, but the continuation decoder retains a set of prefixes; a data point or query may contribute to many prefix nodes. The desired proof should therefore be an optional-stopping or continuation-mass argument for the branching score process itself.

\begin{lemma}[Bellman certificate for first-split frontiers]\label{lem:bellman-frontier-certificate}
   Fix one continuation score block at a state with $m$ points and radius $R$. Suppose that, for every query $q$, there is a nonnegative predictable prefix potential $\mathcal B_q(u)$ on retained score prefixes with the following properties.
   \[
      \mathcal B_q(\varnothing)\le F_4(m,R).
   \]
   For every retained score prefix $u$ before a geometric split or terminal scan, if $\mathrm{ch}(u)$ denotes the next retained score children, then
   \[
      \E\left[\sum_{w\in\mathrm{ch}(u)}\mathcal B_q(w)\,\middle|\,u\right]
      \le
      \mathcal B_q(u).
   \]
   Finally, at every first geometric split prefix $u$, the weighted recursive potential of the children opened from that prefix is at most
   \[
      (1-\kappa\eta^2)\mathcal B_q(u).
   \]
   Then the geometric part of \cref{asm:mixed-continuation-operator} holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Let $\mathcal A(q)$ be the random prefix-free frontier of first geometric split prefixes reached by the query in this block. The second displayed inequality says that $\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}\mathcal B_q(u)$ is a supermartingale when $\mathcal U$ is the current active retained prefix frontier. Optional stopping at the bounded-depth frontier $\mathcal A(q)$ gives
   \[
      \E\sum_{u\in\mathcal A(q)}\mathcal B_q(u)
      \le
      \mathcal B_q(\varnothing)
      \le
      F_4(m,R).
   \]
   Multiplying each stopped prefix by its local split charge and using the last hypothesis gives
   \[
      \E\left[
         \sum_{v\in\mathcal G(q)}w_vF_4(|P[v]|,R_v)
      \right]
      \le
      (1-\kappa\eta^2)F_4(m,R).
   \]
   If no split is reached, the child-potential sum is empty.
\end{proof}

This certificate is the local form of the remaining problem. It is analogous in proof shape to the robust Hamming hashing guarantee of Andoni and Beaglehole~\cite{AB22}: there a per-node min-max inequality over hash choices and adversarial query-neighbor pairs is enough, by induction, to lower-bound the success probability of the whole tree. Here the missing object is the continuation-mass analogue of that local Bellman potential. Proving such a potential would remove the global frontier assumption without replacing the branching decoder by a normalized one-path law.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical mixed continuation budget]\label{lem:canonical-mixed-budget}
   In one score block, let $H_0$ be the one-point continuation mass at the root for the chosen effective budget. For a retained prefix $u$, let $Q_q(u)$ be the exact query continuation mass from $u$, and let
   \[
      D(u)=\sum_{x\in P_u}H_x(u)
   \]
   be the aggregate data continuation mass of the resident bucket at $u$. Define
   \[
      \mathcal B_q^{\mathrm{mix}}(u)
      =
      \Psi(R)\frac{D(u)^\rho Q_q(u)^s}{H_0},
      \qquad s=1-\rho.
   \]
   Then $\mathcal B_q^{\mathrm{mix}}(\varnothing)=F_4(m,R)$ and $\mathcal B_q^{\mathrm{mix}}$ satisfies the prefix-supermartingale hypothesis of \cref{lem:bellman-frontier-certificate}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   At the root, $D(\varnothing)=mH_0$ and $Q_q(\varnothing)=H_0$, hence
   \[
      \mathcal B_q^{\mathrm{mix}}(\varnothing)
      =
      \Psi(R)m^\rho H_0^{\rho+s-1}
      =
      m^\rho\Psi(R)
      =
      F_4(m,R).
   \]

   Fix a retained prefix $u$ and reveal its retained score children $w$. For each realization, Hölder's inequality gives
   \[
      \sum_wD(w)^\rho Q_q(w)^s
      \le
      \left(\sum_wD(w)\right)^\rho
      \left(\sum_wQ_q(w)\right)^s .
   \]
   Before the next Gaussian children are realized, continuation mass is harmonic in expectation, and retention can only delete child mass. Therefore
   \[
      \E\!\left[\sum_wD(w)\,\middle|\,u\right]\le D(u),
      \qquad
      \E\!\left[\sum_wQ_q(w)\,\middle|\,u\right]\le Q_q(u).
   \]
   Since $(a,b)\mapsto a^\rho b^s$ is concave on $\R_+^2$, Jensen's inequality yields
   \[
      \E\left[\sum_wD(w)^\rho Q_q(w)^s\,\middle|\,u\right]
      \le
      D(u)^\rho Q_q(u)^s.
   \]
   Multiplying by $\Psi(R)/H_0$ proves the supermartingale inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Budget-gated cap splitting]\label{cor:budget-gated-splits}
   Fix any predictable nonnegative budget process $\mathcal B_q$ satisfying the first two hypotheses of \cref{lem:bellman-frontier-certificate}. Modify the one-tree query algorithm as follows: at a retained prefix $u$, a stored almost-total cap split is live only if its thinned recursive charge
   \[
      F_4((1-z)|P[u]|,R_u)
      +
      z^s\frac{\Psi(R_C)}{\Psi(R_u)}F_4(z|P[u]|,R_C)
   \]
   is at most $(1-\kappa\eta^2)\mathcal B_q(u)$. If the gate fails, the query ignores that geometric split and continues the score block. Then the weighted recursive child-potential bound in \cref{asm:mixed-continuation-operator} holds for the live splits, without any additional first-frontier assumption.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
   The budget gate enforces the local domination hypothesis in \cref{lem:bellman-frontier-certificate}. Applying that lemma to the live first-split frontier gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Budget-thinned first opportunities]\label{lem:budget-thinned-opportunities}
   Fix any predictable nonnegative budget process $\mathcal B_q$ satisfying the first two hypotheses of \cref{lem:bellman-frontier-certificate}. At a retained prefix $u$, let $\Gamma(u)$ denote the weighted recursive potential of the geometric children that would be opened from $u$; for the almost-total cap split above,
   \[
      \Gamma(u)=
      F_4((1-z)|P[u]|,R_u)
      +
      z^s\frac{\Psi(R_C)}{\Psi(R_u)}F_4(z|P[u]|,R_C).
   \]
   Let $\mathcal A(q)$ be the prefix-free frontier of first prefixes on which such a geometric opportunity is offered to query $q$. Modify the query rule at these first opportunities as follows: activate the geometric children at $u$ with probability
   \[
      \pi(u)=
      \min\left\{
         1,\,
         \frac{(1-\kappa\eta^2)\mathcal B_q(u)}{\Gamma(u)}
      \right\},
   \]
   with the convention $\pi(u)=1$ when $\Gamma(u)=0$. If the activation coin fails, this query branch opens no geometric child in the current score block; it may only continue toward a cap-free terminal scan. Then
   \[
      \E\left[
         \sum_{u\in\mathcal A(q)}
         \mathbf 1_{\{\text{$u$ activates}\}}\Gamma(u)
      \right]
      \le
      (1-\kappa\eta^2)F_4(m,R).
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Condition on the realized score tree and on the first-opportunity frontier $\mathcal A(q)$. The activation coins are independent of the score randomness after $\mathcal A(q)$ is determined, and
   \[
      \E\!\left[
         \mathbf 1_{\{\text{$u$ activates}\}}\Gamma(u)
         \,\middle|\,\mathcal A(q),u
      \right]
      =
      \pi(u)\Gamma(u)
      \le
      (1-\kappa\eta^2)\mathcal B_q(u).
   \]
   Optional stopping for the budget supermartingale gives
   \[
      \E\sum_{u\in\mathcal A(q)}\mathcal B_q(u)
      \le
      \mathcal B_q(\varnothing)
      \le
      F_4(m,R).
   \]
   Combining the two displays proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Canonical budget activation factor]\label{lem:canonical-budget-activation}
   Use the canonical mixed budget of \cref{lem:canonical-mixed-budget} inside
   one current geometric state. Thus the parent radius factor in the budget is
   the same $R_u$ that appears in the local split charge. Let $u$ be a retained
   prefix with nonempty bucket $P[u]$, let
   \[
      m_u=|P[u]|,
      \qquad
      \overline H(u)=\frac{D(u)}{m_u},
   \]
   and define the local thickness factor
   \[
      \beta_q(u)
      =
      \min\left\{
         1,\,
         \frac{\overline H(u)^\rho Q_q(u)^s}{H_0}
      \right\}.
   \]
   If an almost-total cap opportunity at $u$ has weighted recursive charge
   \[
      \Gamma(u)\le F_4(m_u,R_u),
   \]
   then the budget-thinned activation probability from
   \cref{lem:budget-thinned-opportunities} satisfies
   \[
      \pi(u)\ge (1-\kappa\eta^2)\beta_q(u).
   \]
   In particular, whenever
   \[
      \overline H(u)^\rho Q_q(u)^s\ge H_0,
   \]
   the canonical budget activates the opportunity with probability
   $1-O(\eta^2)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   By definition of the canonical budget,
   \[
      \mathcal B_q^{\mathrm{mix}}(u)
      =
      \Psi(R_u)\frac{D(u)^\rho Q_q(u)^s}{H_0}
      =
      F_4(m_u,R_u)
      \frac{\overline H(u)^\rho Q_q(u)^s}{H_0}.
   \]
   The budget-thinned rule uses
   \[
      \pi(u)
      =
      \min\left\{
         1,\,
         \frac{(1-\kappa\eta^2)\mathcal B_q^{\mathrm{mix}}(u)}
              {\Gamma(u)}
      \right\}.
   \]
   Since $\Gamma(u)\le F_4(m_u,R_u)$, this is at least
   \[
      \min\left\{
         1,\,
         (1-\kappa\eta^2)
         \frac{\overline H(u)^\rho Q_q(u)^s}{H_0}
      \right\}
      \ge
      (1-\kappa\eta^2)\beta_q(u).
   \]
   The final statement is the special case $\beta_q(u)=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Work can be bought by thinning]\label{rem:budget-thinning}
   \Cref{cor:budget-gated-splits} is deterministic: a split is live only when the local budget pays its whole charge. \Cref{lem:budget-thinned-opportunities} is the corresponding randomized rule. It converts any prefix-supermartingale budget, in particular the canonical mixed budget of \cref{lem:canonical-mixed-budget}, into an unconditional expected bound for the activated geometric work.

   In a homogeneous balanced prefix at depth $\ell$, the canonical budget is smaller than the local recursive charge by about $B^{(1-\rho)\ell}$, so the activation probability is about $B^{-(1-\rho)\ell}$. This is exactly the missing factor in the naive sum over the $B^\ell$ query prefixes. Thus the balanced-prefix obstruction is no longer a work blow-up; it becomes a near-lineage question: prove that the tame-good near paths supplied by \cref{lem:adaptive-continuation} contain enough budget-thinned first opportunities that one complete radius-decreasing lineage survives with inverse-polylogarithmic probability.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The remaining value-delivery target]\label{rem:value-delivery-target}
   \Cref{lem:canonical-budget-activation} makes the near-lineage part of the
   budget-thinned route quantitative. Along a candidate near path, every
   necessary cap opportunity contributes the usual geometric thinning factor
   from \cref{lem:thin-lineage} and, only when the canonical budget is locally
   insufficient, the additional factor $\beta_q(u)$. The work side is already
   handled by optional stopping in \cref{lem:budget-thinned-opportunities}.
   Thus one possible self-contained polylog proof is reduced to showing that,
   with inverse-polylogarithmic probability over the continuation score tree,
   there is a tame near path for which
   \[
      \prod_{u\in\mathcal O(p,q)}
      \beta_q(u)
      \ge
      \log^{-O_c(1)}n,
   \]
   where $\mathcal O(p,q)$ is the set of cap opportunities on that path that
   contain the true near point. Equivalently, whenever this product is too
   small, one must prove that the same score tree exposes the near point through
   a cap-free residual scan before those underfunded opportunities are needed.
   This is sharper than \cref{asm:mixed-continuation-operator}: the expected
   work bound is proved, and the remaining statement is only value delivery to
   the near lineage.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why normalized continuation budgets are too small]\label{rem:query-only-budget}
   The most obvious query-only budget,
   \[
      \mathcal B_q(u)=F_4(m,R)\frac{H_q(u)}{H_q(\varnothing)},
   \]
   is a normalized query continuation-mass martingale, but it cannot pay for early geometric splits by itself. The canonical mixed budget in \cref{lem:canonical-mixed-budget} has the same scale in the homogeneous balanced case. At an early level $\ell$, before the continuation barrier prunes many children, a typical query prefix has budget about $F_4(m,R)B^{-\ell}$. The corresponding bucket has size about $mB^{-\ell}$, so its recursive power charge is
   \[
      F_4(mB^{-\ell},R)
      \approx
      F_4(m,R)B^{-\rho\ell}.
   \]
   The ratio is $B^{(1-\rho)\ell}$. Thus the budget certificate cannot be query-only; it must include data-aware information about how the bucket mass contracts under the score process, or else the algorithm must gate early cap splits until such a budget is available.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional randomized one-tree polylog endpoint]\label{prop:randomized-polylog}
   Under \cref{asm:mixed-continuation-operator}, the randomized one-tree construction with predictable almost-total cap tests gives worst-case Euclidean ANN bounds
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n,
      \qquad
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   Work in one bounded-diameter localized instance and put
   \[
      L=\log n,
      \qquad
      \ell=\log\log n,
      \qquad
      \eta=\Theta_c(\ell/L),
      \qquad
      \delta=\delta_0\eta^{2/\rho_c}.
   \]
   Choose $\delta_0$ small enough that \cref{lem:thin-cap-contraction} applies to every almost-total cap split. Also
   \[
      \alpha^{-1}=(\eta\delta)^{-1}=\eta^{-1-2/\rho_c}\delta_0^{-1}=\log^{O_c(1)}n,
      \qquad
      \exp(O_c(\eta L))=\log^{O_c(1)}n,
      \qquad
      \eta^{-O_c(1)}=\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
   Let
   \[
      A(n)=\eta^{-O_c(1)}\alpha^{-1}\exp(O_c(\eta L))\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
   By \cref{lem:cap-free-scan-accounting}, the expected local score work and cap-free terminal scans at a state are at most $A(n)F_4(m,R)$, while \cref{asm:mixed-continuation-operator} bounds the expected sum of recursive child potentials by $(1-\kappa\eta^2)F_4(m,R)$. Unrolling over geometric generations gives total expected query work at most
   \[
      O(\eta^{-2})A(n)F_4(n,R_0).
   \]
   Since $F_4(n,R_0)=n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n$, the displayed parameter choice makes this $n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n$.

   The cap-edge thinning along the true near lineage still costs only a polylogarithmic factor. Indeed, the number of radius-reducing cap edges is
   \[
      H=O_c\!\left(\eta^{-2}\log\frac{R_0}{r}\right)
      =
      O_c(\eta^{-2}\ell),
   \]
   and
   \[
      H\delta=O_c(\eta^{-2+2/\rho_c}\ell)=o(1)
   \]
   for the displayed choice of $\delta$. Thus \cref{lem:thin-lineage} gives lineage-thinning probability at least $\log^{-O_c(1)}n$. The score depth is $K=O_c(L)$, so \cref{lem:adaptive-continuation} gives a common near score path with probability $K^{-O_c(1)}=\log^{-O_c(1)}n$. Polylogarithmic amplification and the truncation paragraph in the preliminaries give the query bound with constant success probability. The storage bound follows from the storage contraction in \cref{lem:thin-cap-contraction}, the stored-prefix part of \cref{lem:adaptive-continuation}, and bounded-diameter localization.
\end{proof}

\section{A structured posterior route}

\Cref{prop:randomized-polylog} keeps the cap geometry and asks for a branching first-split work estimate. There is another possible route: replace cap discovery by posterior-superlevel children of a stochastic score mechanism. This is closer to the LSH-forest negative-moment calculus and to the local surrogate-success viewpoint of Andoni and Beaglehole~\cite{AB22}. The advantage is that the recursion accounting becomes almost algebraic.

The important distinction is between explicit enumeration and structured decoding. The negative-moment recursion itself does not impose a coarse child mass. The coarse scale appears only when every possible outcome must be explicitly evaluated before the few useful ones can be found.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit-enumeration barrier]\label{lem:explicit-enumeration-barrier}
   Fix $\rho>0$ and a node of size $m$. Consider a one-scale posterior recursion whose useful children have mass
   \[
      \xi=m^{-\gamma}.
   \]
   Suppose one independent group must evaluate
   \[
      B\ge \xi^{-1}\log^{-O(1)}m
   \]
   possible outcomes, and that turning $\xi$-scale negative-moment value into constant hit probability requires
   \[
      T\ge \xi^{-\rho}\log^{-O(1)}m
   \]
   independent groups. If the local query budget is $m^\rho\log^{O(1)}m$, then
   \[
      \gamma\le \frac{\rho}{1+\rho}+O\!\left(\frac{\log\log m}{\log m}\right).
   \]
   In particular, for $\rho=\rho_c=1/(2c^2-1)$ the explicit balanced scale is
   \[
      m^{-\rho/(1+\rho)}=m^{-1/(2c^2)}
   \]
   up to polylogarithmic factors. If a structured decoder lists only active outcomes and avoids the factor $B$, the repetition cost $\xi^{-\rho}$ fits the $m^\rho$ budget for every nonempty scale $\xi\ge1/m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Explicit evaluation contributes at least
   \[
      BT\ge \xi^{-(1+\rho)}\log^{-O(1)}m
      =
      m^{\gamma(1+\rho)}\log^{-O(1)}m
   \]
   units of local work. Requiring this to be at most $m^\rho\log^{O(1)}m$ gives the displayed bound on $\gamma$. The identity
   \[
      \frac{\rho_c}{1+\rho_c}=\frac1{2c^2}
   \]
   is immediate from the definition of $\rho_c$. Without explicit enumeration, the one-scale amplification cost is only $\xi^{-\rho}$, and $\xi\ge1/m$ implies $\xi^{-\rho}\le m^\rho$.
\end{proof}

Thus the structured target is not a finite quantization theorem. It is an output-sensitive decoding theorem: the outcome space may be implicit or enormous, provided the query lists only the active children and the listed children obey the moment bounds below. One must still remember the scale. A raw lower bound on
\[
   \sum_{C\ni p}\lambda_q(C)\mu(C)^{-\rho}
\]
is a value statement, not by itself a hit-probability statement; a singleton child hit with one-group intensity $m^{-\rho}$ already contributes constant negative-moment value but has only $m^{-\rho}$ hit intensity before amplification. The dyadic amplification lemmas below keep the scale $\xi$ explicit and insert exactly the $\xi^{-\rho}$ repetitions needed to convert value into constant success probability.

We first write the posterior identity in the finite notation, because it is the cleanest algebraic form. Let a node contain a set $S$ of $m$ points. A finite score channel assigns probabilities $K_x(j)$, $j\in[B]$, to every $x\in S$, with $\sum_jK_x(j)=1$. Put
\[
   \mu_j=\frac1m\sum_{x\in S}K_x(j),
   \qquad
   R_j(x)=\frac{K_x(j)}{\mu_j}.
\]
For a threshold $u$, the posterior-superlevel child is
\[
   C_{j,u}=\{x\in S:R_j(x)\ge u\},
   \qquad
   \Phi_j(u)=\frac{|C_{j,u}|}{m}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Posterior superlevel compiler]\label{lem:posterior-superlevel-compiler}
   Fix $0<\beta<\rho$ and put $s_\beta=1-\beta$. Store child $(j,u)$ with Poisson intensity
   \[
      \mu_j\,\beta u^{\beta-1}\Phi_j(u)^\rho\,du,
   \]
   and let query $q$ assign each stored copy weight $R_j(q)^{s_\beta}$ in the ideal fractional calculation. Equivalently, one may materialize active copies from a query-dependent Poisson intensity multiplied by this factor. For every data point $p$,
   \[
      \E\sum_{\substack{C\text{ active}\\p\in C}}
      \left(\frac{|C|}{m}\right)^{-\rho}
      =
      \sum_jK_p(j)^\beta K_q(j)^{s_\beta}.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The negative moment cancels the stored factor $\Phi_j(u)^\rho$:
   \[
   \begin{aligned}
      \E\sum_{\substack{C\text{ active}\\p\in C}}
      \left(\frac{|C|}{m}\right)^{-\rho}
      &=
      \sum_j\mu_jR_j(q)^{s_\beta}
      \int_0^{R_j(p)}\beta u^{\beta-1}\,du\\
      &=
      \sum_j\mu_jR_j(p)^\beta R_j(q)^{s_\beta}\\
      &=
      \sum_jK_p(j)^\beta K_q(j)^{s_\beta},
   \end{aligned}
   \]
   since $\beta+s_\beta=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scale-window negative-moment amplification]\label{lem:scale-window-compiler}
   Fix a scale $\xi\in[1/m,1]$ and a value lower bound $h>0$. Consider one independent structured channel group whose active children are generated by a query-dependent Poisson process on certified sets $C\subseteq S$. Write
   \[
      \mu(C)=\frac{|C|}{m}.
   \]
   Suppose every active child has
   \[
      \xi/2\le\mu(C)\le\xi,
   \]
   and let $\lambda_q(C)$ be its active intensity for query $q$. Assume that, for a near pair $(p,q)$ not handled by the resident guard,
   \[
      \sum_{C\ni p}\lambda_q(C)\mu(C)^{-\rho}\ge h.
   \]
   Also assume one group has output-sensitive decoding cost $d\log^{O_c(1)}m$ plus the listed output, and satisfies the scale-normalized moment bounds
   \[
      \sum_C\lambda_q(C)\mu(C)^\rho
      \le A\xi^\rho,
      \qquad
      \sum_C\sigma(C)\mu(C)^{1+\rho}
      \le A\xi^\rho,
   \]
   where $\sigma(C)$ is the preprocessing intensity with which child $C$ is stored. Then
   \[
      T=\left\lceil 2^{\rho+1}\xi^{-\rho}h^{-1}\right\rceil
   \]
   independent groups give a constant-probability hit of some active child containing $p$, while their total active-child $\rho$-moment and stored-child $(1+\rho)$-moment are both $O(Ah^{-1})$. Their decoding cost is
   \[
      d\,\xi^{-\rho}h^{-1}\log^{O_c(1)}m,
   \]
   which is at most $d\,m^\rho h^{-1}\log^{O_c(1)}m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Let
   \[
      \Lambda_p(q)=\sum_{C\ni p}\lambda_q(C)
   \]
   be the one-group Poisson intensity of active children containing $p$. Since $\mu(C)\ge\xi/2$,
   \[
      \mu(C)^{-\rho}\le 2^\rho\xi^{-\rho}.
   \]
   The negative-moment lower bound therefore implies
   \[
      \Lambda_p(q)\ge 2^{-\rho}\xi^\rho h.
   \]
   Across $T$ independent groups, the number of active children containing $p$ is Poisson with mean at least $2$, so the hit probability is at least $1-e^{-2}$.

   The active and stored moment bounds multiply by $T$:
   \[
      T A\xi^\rho=O(Ah^{-1}).
   \]
   The decoding bound is the same multiplication. Finally $\xi\ge1/m$ gives $\xi^{-\rho}\le m^\rho$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Dyadic structured amplification]\label{lem:dyadic-structured-amplification}
   Let $\mathcal I$ be dyadic scales $\xi\in[1/m,\eta]$. At each scale $\xi$, suppose one independent structured group has children with
   \[
      \xi/2\le\mu(C)\le\xi,
   \]
   output-sensitive decoding cost $d\log^{O_c(1)}m$ plus output, and moment bounds
   \[
      \sum_{C\text{ at scale }\xi}\lambda_q(C)\mu(C)^\rho
      \le A\xi^\rho,
      \qquad
      \sum_{C\text{ at scale }\xi}\sigma(C)\mu(C)^{1+\rho}
      \le A\xi^\rho .
   \]
   Suppose that, for a near pair $(p,q)$, the total negative-moment value over all scales is
   \[
      \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal I}
      \sum_{\substack{C\text{ at scale }\xi\\p\in C}}
      \lambda_q(C)\mu(C)^{-\rho}
      \ge h .
   \]
   If scale $\xi$ is repeated
   \[
      T_\xi=\left\lceil 2^{\rho+1}\xi^{-\rho}h^{-1}\right\rceil
   \]
   times, then the probability of activating some child containing $p$ is at least $1-e^{-2}$. The total decoding and active-output work is
   \[
      d\,m^\rho h^{-1}\log^{O_c(1)}m,
   \]
   and the total expected number of active children is at most the same bound without the factor $d$. If each stored child is represented by $O(1)$ words, the total expected stored-child descriptions over all repetitions are at most
   \[
      m^{1+\rho}h^{-1}\log^{O_c(1)}m.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Let $V_\xi$ be the negative-moment contribution at scale $\xi$ and let $\Lambda_\xi$ be the one-group active intensity of children at that scale containing $p$. Since $\mu(C)\le\xi$ and $\mu(C)\ge\xi/2$ on the scale,
   \[
      \Lambda_\xi
      =
      \sum_{C\ni p}\lambda_q(C)
      \ge
      2^{-\rho}\xi^\rho V_\xi .
   \]
   After $T_\xi$ repetitions, the total hit intensity over all scales is at least
   \[
      \sum_\xi T_\xi\Lambda_\xi
      \ge
      2h^{-1}\sum_\xi V_\xi
      \ge2,
   \]
   so the miss probability is at most $e^{-2}$.

   The decoding cost is proportional to
   \[
      \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal I}\xi^{-\rho}h^{-1}
      \le
      m^\rho h^{-1}\log^{O_c(1)}m,
   \]
   because the dyadic sum is dominated by the smallest scale $1/m$. The one-group expected number of active children at scale $\xi$ is at most
   \[
      (\xi/2)^{-\rho}\sum_C\lambda_q(C)\mu(C)^\rho
      \le
      2^\rho A,
   \]
   so the same dyadic sum bounds the active output.

   Finally, the one-group expected number of stored child descriptions at scale $\xi$ is at most
   \[
      (\xi/2)^{-(1+\rho)}
      \sum_C\sigma(C)\mu(C)^{1+\rho}
      \le
      2^{1+\rho}A\xi^{-1}.
   \]
   Multiplying by $T_\xi$ and summing $\xi^{-(1+\rho)}$ over dyadic scales gives the stated $m^{1+\rho}h^{-1}\log^{O_c(1)}m$ bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous likelihood-ball geometry]\label{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls}
   Let a node contain $S\subset\R^d$, $|S|=m$, and let $\gamma_t$ denote $N(0,tI_d)$. For $Y\sim\gamma_t$ define
   \[
      L_Y(x)=\exp\!\left(\ip{Y}{x}-\frac t2\|x\|^2\right),
      \qquad
      C_{Y,u}=\{x\in S:L_Y(x)\ge u\},
      \qquad
      \Phi_Y(u)=\frac{|C_{Y,u}|}{m}.
   \]
   The set $C_{Y,u}$ is a Euclidean ball. For $0<\alpha<\rho$ and $s_\alpha=1-\alpha$,
   \[
      \int L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}\mathbf 1_{\{p\in C_{Y,u}\}}\Phi_Y(u)^{-\rho}
      \,d\gamma_t(Y)\,\alpha u^{\alpha-1}\Phi_Y(u)^\rho\,du
      =
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{\alpha s_\alpha t}{2}\|p-q\|^2\right).
   \]

   Moreover fix $c>1$ and $\eta\in[1/m,1]$. Let $p,q$ satisfy $\|p-q\|\le r$, and suppose fewer than $\eta m/2$ data points are within distance $cr$ of $q$. With
   \[
      \alpha=\kappa/\log m,
      \qquad
      t=C_c\frac{\log m}{r^2}
   \]
   for sufficiently small $\kappa=\kappa(c)>0$ and sufficiently large $C_c=C_c(c)$, the pure restricted value
   \[
      \mathcal P_\eta(p,q)
      =
      \int d\gamma_t(Y)L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}
      \int_0^{L_Y(p)}
      \alpha u^{\alpha-1}
      \mathbf 1_{\{\Phi_Y(u)\le\eta,\ C_{Y,u}\subseteq \Ball(q,cr)\}}\,du
   \]
   is at least a positive constant $\delta_c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
   The level-set identity
   \[
      L_Y(x)\ge u
      \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
      \left\|x-\frac Yt\right\|^2
      \le
      \left\|\frac Yt\right\|^2-\frac{2\log u}{t}
   \]
   shows that $C_{Y,u}$ is a ball. The negative moment cancels $\Phi_Y(u)^\rho$, and
   \[
   \begin{aligned}
      \int d\gamma_t(Y)L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}
      \int_0^{L_Y(p)}\alpha u^{\alpha-1}\,du
      &=
      \int L_Y(p)^\alpha L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}\,d\gamma_t(Y)\\
      &=
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{\alpha s_\alpha t}{2}\|p-q\|^2\right),
   \end{aligned}
   \]
   by completing the square for the Gaussian shift family.

   For the pure bound, translate so that $q=0$ and write $d=\|p-q\|\le r$. Under the tilted measure proportional to
   \[
      L_Y(p)^\alpha L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}\,d\gamma_t(Y),
   \]
   the vector $Y$ has distribution $N(t\alpha p,tI_d)$, up to the affinity factor displayed above. For an invalid point $x$ with $\|x-q\|>cr$, put
   \[
      \ell_x=\log\frac{L_Y(x)}{L_Y(p)}.
   \]
   Under this tilted law, $\ell_x$ is Gaussian with variance $t\|x-p\|^2$ and mean
   \[
      -\frac t2\Delta_x,
      \qquad
      \Delta_x
      =
      \alpha\|x-p\|^2+s_\alpha(\|x-q\|^2-d^2).
   \]
   Since $\|x-q\|\ge cr$ and $d\le r$,
   \[
      \Delta_x\ge s_\alpha(c^2-1)r^2,
      \qquad
      \frac{\Delta_x}{\|x-p\|}\ge \|x-q\|-d\ge(c-1)r .
   \]
   Therefore, for
   \[
      h_0=\frac{ts_\alpha(c^2-1)r^2}{4},
   \]
   a Gaussian tail bound gives
   \[
      \Pr[\ell_x\ge-h_0]\le
      \exp\!\left(-\Omega_c(tr^2)\right)\le m^{-3}
   \]
   after increasing $C_c$. A union bound over $S$ shows that, with probability $1-O(m^{-2})$ under the tilted law, every invalid point has $L_Y(x)<e^{-h_0}L_Y(p)$.

   On this event, every threshold $u\in[e^{-h_0}L_Y(p),L_Y(p)]$ produces a child containing $p$ and no invalid point. Since fewer than $\eta m/2$ points are valid, such a child also has mass at most $\eta$. Thus
   \[
      \int_0^{L_Y(p)}
      \alpha u^{\alpha-1}
      \mathbf 1_{\{\Phi_Y(u)\le\eta,\ C_{Y,u}\subseteq \Ball(q,cr)\}}\,du
      \ge
      L_Y(p)^\alpha(1-e^{-\alpha h_0})
   \]
   except on a tilted event of probability $O(m^{-2})$. Integrating back gives
   \[
      \mathcal P_\eta(p,q)
      \ge
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{\alpha s_\alpha tr^2}{2}\right)
      (1-e^{-\alpha h_0})(1-O(m^{-2})).
   \]
   The choices $\alpha=\kappa/\log m$ and $t=C_c\log m/r^2$ make both $\alpha tr^2$ and $\alpha h_0$ positive constants depending only on $c$, proving the claim after fixing $\kappa,C_c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ideal likelihood scale profile]\label{lem:ideal-likelihood-scale-profile}
   In the notation of \cref{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls}, fix $0<\alpha<\rho$ and $s_\alpha=1-\alpha$. For a dyadic scale $\xi\in[1/m,1]$, restrict the continuous children to
   \[
      \xi/2\le \Phi_Y(u)\le \xi
   \]
   and give child $(Y,u)$ preprocessing intensity
   \[
      d\sigma(Y,u)=d\gamma_t(Y)\,\alpha u^{\alpha-1}\Phi_Y(u)^\rho\,du
   \]
   and query-active intensity
   \[
      d\lambda_q(Y,u)=L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}\,d\sigma(Y,u).
   \]
   Then, uniformly in the data set and query,
   \[
      \int_{\xi/2\le\Phi_Y(u)\le\xi}
      \Phi_Y(u)^\rho\,d\lambda_q(Y,u)
      \le
      C_{\rho,\alpha}\xi^\rho,
   \]
   and
   \[
      \int_{\xi/2\le\Phi_Y(u)\le\xi}
      \Phi_Y(u)^{1+\rho}\,d\sigma(Y,u)
      \le
      C_{\rho,\alpha}\xi^\rho.
   \]
   Moreover, under the guard-failure hypothesis of \cref{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls}, the ideal pure multiscale value satisfies
   \[
      \sum_{\xi\le\eta}
      \int_{\substack{\xi/2\le\Phi_Y(u)\le\xi\\
                      p\in C_{Y,u},\ C_{Y,u}\subseteq\Ball(q,cr)}}
      \Phi_Y(u)^{-\rho}\,d\lambda_q(Y,u)
      \ge
      \delta_c,
   \]
   where the sum ranges over dyadic $\xi\in[1/m,\eta]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   For $a>\alpha$, put
   \[
      Z_Y=\frac1m\sum_{x\in S}L_Y(x),
      \qquad
      R_Y(x)=\frac{L_Y(x)}{Z_Y},
   \]
   and let $F_Y(v)=m^{-1}|\{x:R_Y(x)\ge v\}|$. Since $R_Y(X)$ has mean one for a uniform random data point $X$, Markov's inequality gives
   \[
      F_Y(v)\le\min\{1,v^{-1}\}.
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
      \int_0^\infty \alpha u^{\alpha-1}\Phi_Y(u)^a\,du
      =
      Z_Y^\alpha
      \int_0^\infty \alpha v^{\alpha-1}F_Y(v)^a\,dv
      \le
      C_{a,\alpha}Z_Y^\alpha.
   \]

   Jensen's inequality gives
   \[
      Z_Y^\alpha\le\frac1m\sum_{x\in S}L_Y(x)^\alpha.
   \]
   Hence
   \[
      \int L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}Z_Y^\alpha\,d\gamma_t(Y)
      \le
      \frac1m\sum_{x\in S}
      \int L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}L_Y(x)^\alpha\,d\gamma_t(Y)
      \le1,
   \]
   by the same Gaussian affinity identity used in \cref{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls}. Similarly,
   \[
      \int Z_Y^\alpha\,d\gamma_t(Y)
      \le
      \frac1m\sum_{x\in S}\int L_Y(x)^\alpha\,d\gamma_t(Y)
      \le1.
   \]

   On the scale $\Phi_Y(u)\le\xi$,
   \[
      \Phi_Y(u)^{2\rho}\le\xi^\rho\Phi_Y(u)^\rho.
   \]
   Taking $a=\rho$ in the previous bound proves
   \[
      \int_{\xi/2\le\Phi_Y(u)\le\xi}
      \Phi_Y(u)^\rho\,d\lambda_q(Y,u)
      =
      \int_{\xi/2\le\Phi_Y(u)\le\xi}
      L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}\alpha u^{\alpha-1}\Phi_Y(u)^{2\rho}\,du\,d\gamma_t(Y)
      \le
      C_{\rho,\alpha}\xi^\rho .
   \]
   Likewise, on the same scale
   \[
      \Phi_Y(u)^{1+2\rho}\le\xi^\rho\Phi_Y(u)^{1+\rho},
   \]
   and taking $a=1+\rho$ gives the stored-child moment bound.

   Finally, for any child containing $p$, its mass is at least $1/m$. Thus summing the dyadic scales up to $\eta$ partitions the pure event in \cref{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls}. Since
   \[
      \Phi_Y(u)^{-\rho}\,d\lambda_q(Y,u)
      =
      L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}\alpha u^{\alpha-1}\,du\,d\gamma_t(Y),
   \]
   the displayed pure multiscale value is exactly $\mathcal P_\eta(p,q)$, and \cref{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls} lower-bounds it by $\delta_c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rank-prefix form of likelihood balls]\label{lem:likelihood-rank-prefix}
   In the notation of \cref{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls}, fix $Y$. Let
   \[
      r_1>r_2>\cdots>r_M>0
   \]
   be the distinct values taken by $L_Y$ on $S$, set $r_{M+1}=0$, and put
   \[
      P_i(Y)=\{x\in S:L_Y(x)\ge r_i\},
      \qquad
      k_i(Y)=|P_i(Y)|.
   \]
   For every $a\ge0$ and every nonnegative functional $G$ of the nonempty
   superlevel set,
   \[
      \int_0^{r_1}\alpha u^{\alpha-1}\Phi_Y(u)^a
      G(C_{Y,u})\,du
      =
      \sum_{i=1}^M
      \left(\frac{k_i(Y)}m\right)^a
      \left(r_i^\alpha-r_{i+1}^\alpha\right)G(P_i(Y)).
   \]
   Consequently, the ideal active process at dyadic scale $\xi$ can be written
   as a Poisson process over score prefixes $(Y,i)$ with
   \[
      \xi/2\le k_i(Y)/m\le\xi
   \]
   and query intensity
   \[
      d\lambda_q(Y,i)
      =
      L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}
      \left(\frac{k_i(Y)}m\right)^\rho
      \left(r_i^\alpha-r_{i+1}^\alpha\right)\,d\gamma_t(Y).
   \]
   The negative-moment contribution of such a prefix is obtained by multiplying
   by $(k_i(Y)/m)^{-\rho}$, so the factor $(k_i(Y)/m)^\rho$ cancels exactly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   For $u\in(r_{i+1},r_i]$, the superlevel set is $C_{Y,u}=P_i(Y)$ and
   $\Phi_Y(u)=k_i(Y)/m$. Integrating $\alpha u^{\alpha-1}$ over this interval
   gives $r_i^\alpha-r_{i+1}^\alpha$. Summing over the intervals proves the
   identity. The Poisson-process statement is the same identity applied to the
   preprocessing intensity
   $\alpha u^{\alpha-1}\Phi_Y(u)^\rho du\,d\gamma_t(Y)$ and then multiplied by
   the query weight $L_Y(q)^{s_\alpha}$.
\end{proof}

Choose $\eta_m=m^{-\rho}\log^{A_c}m$. A uniform guard of size $m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m$ handles the case where at least $\eta_m m/2$ points are already valid answers. Otherwise \cref{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls} supplies constant pure negative-moment value below mass $\eta_m$, and \cref{lem:ideal-likelihood-scale-profile} shows that the same ideal continuum already has the scale-normalized query and storage moments needed by \cref{lem:dyadic-structured-amplification}. The value need not sit at the top scale $\eta_m$; it may be concentrated near singleton children. This is why the amplification lemma runs all dyadic scales down to $1/m$. The geometric cost of doing so is still only $m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m$.

Thus the remaining posterior problem is no longer geometric. By \cref{lem:likelihood-rank-prefix}, it is also not inherently a finite-channel quantization problem: after the Gaussian score $Y$ is fixed, the threshold continuum collapses to score-prefix rank gaps. The missing operation is a structured rank-prefix reporter: sample or decode the few active dyadic prefixes of this random score process and return one stored representative from each listed prefix. If such a prefix is pure for the query, any representative is a valid answer. Uniformly choosing a representative from the whole node would lose a factor equal to the prefix mass, so this output-sensitive reporting step is the real data-structural content.

There is an even more collapsed non-channel target. Taking the likelihood exponent to the $\alpha=0$ endpoint turns the posterior child family into one smoothed score order. Then it is enough to draw a point from the high-score part of that order; no threshold continuum, dyadic scale, or stored representative is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Smoothed maximum-score margin]\label{lem:smoothed-max-score-margin}
   Let $S\subset\R^d$, $|S|=m$, and suppose $\|p-q\|\le r$. For
   \[
      A_Y(x)=\ip{Y}{x}-\frac t2\|x\|^2,
      \qquad
      Y\sim N(tq,tI_d),
   \]
   choose any
   \[
      t\ge C_c\frac{\log m}{r^2}
   \]
   with $C_c$ sufficiently large. Then, with probability at least
   $1-m^{-2}$ over $Y$, every invalid point $x\in S$ with
   $\|x-q\|>cr$ satisfies
   \[
      A_Y(x)\le A_Y(p)-\delta_c t r^2
   \]
   for a constant $\delta_c>0$ depending only on $c$. Consequently, on this
   event, every point $\widehat x$ satisfying
   \[
      A_Y(\widehat x)\ge\max_{z\in S}A_Y(z)-\frac12\delta_c t r^2
   \]
   is a valid $cr$-neighbor of $q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Write $G=Y-tq$. Up to constants independent of $x$,
   \[
      A_Y(x)
      =
      \ip{G}{x-q}-\frac t2\|x-q\|^2+\mathrm{const}(Y,q).
   \]
   Thus we may translate so that $q=0$ and write $Y$ for
   $G\sim N(0,tI_d)$. For an invalid point $x$, put
   \[
      \ell_x=A_Y(x)-A_Y(p).
   \]
   Since $Y\sim N(0,tI_d)$, this is a Gaussian random variable with variance
   $t\|x-p\|^2$ and mean
   \[
      -\frac t2(\|x\|^2-\|p\|^2).
   \]
   Also
   \[
      \|x\|^2-\|p\|^2\ge(c^2-1)r^2,
      \qquad
      \frac{\|x\|^2-\|p\|^2}{\|x-p\|}
      \ge
      \|x\|-\|p\|
      \ge(c-1)r .
   \]
   Taking $\delta_c=(c^2-1)/4$ and
   \[
      \delta_c t r^2=\frac14t(c^2-1)r^2
   \]
   and applying a Gaussian tail bound gives
   \[
      \Pr\!\left[\ell_x\ge-\delta_c t r^2\right]
      \le
      \exp(-\Omega_c(tr^2))
      \le
      m^{-3}
   \]
   after increasing $C_c$. A union bound over $S$ proves the simultaneous
   score gap.

   On that event, every invalid point has score at most
   $A_Y(p)-\delta_c t r^2$. Since $\max_{z\in S}A_Y(z)\ge A_Y(p)$, an additive
   $\frac12\delta_c t r^2$ maximizer cannot be invalid.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Score maximization is additive nearest neighbor]\label{lem:score-max-additive-nn}
   Fix $t>0$ and write $y=Y/t$. For every $\varepsilon>0$, a point
   $\widehat x\in S$ satisfies
   \[
      A_Y(\widehat x)
      \ge
      \max_{z\in S}A_Y(z)-\varepsilon t r^2
   \]
   if and only if
   \[
      \|\widehat x-y\|^2
      \le
      \min_{z\in S}\|z-y\|^2+2\varepsilon r^2.
   \]
   Thus \cref{asm:smoothed-score-max} is exactly a smoothed additive nearest
   neighbor primitive with additive squared-distance error $\delta_c r^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Since $Y=ty$,
   \[
      A_Y(x)
      =
      t\ip{y}{x}-\frac t2\|x\|^2
      =
      \frac t2\|y\|^2-\frac t2\|x-y\|^2.
   \]
   The first term is independent of $x$, so losing $\varepsilon t r^2$ in
   score is equivalent to increasing squared distance from $y$ by
   $2\varepsilon r^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Smoothed dominated kernel sampling suffices]\label{lem:smoothed-gibbs-suffices}
   In the setting of \cref{lem:smoothed-max-score-margin}, define the Gibbs law
   on the resident points by
   \[
      \mathsf G_Y(x)
      =
      \frac{\exp(A_Y(x))}
           {\sum_{z\in S}\exp(A_Y(z))}.
   \]
   Equivalently, for $y=Y/t$,
   \[
      \mathsf G_Y(x)
      =
      \frac{\exp(-t\|x-y\|^2/2)}
           {\sum_{z\in S}\exp(-t\|z-y\|^2/2)} .
   \]
   After increasing the constant $C_c$ in the lower bound on $t$, with
   probability at least $1-m^{-2}$ over $Y$ the Gibbs mass of invalid points is
   at most $m^{-3}$.

   Consequently, let $Q_Y$ be any output law satisfying the one-sided
   domination
   \[
      Q_Y(x)\le L_m\,\mathsf G_Y(x)
      \qquad\text{for all }x\in S.
   \]
   Then $Q_Y$ returns an invalid point with probability at most
   $L_m m^{-3}$ on this event. In particular $L_m=\log^{O_c(1)}m$ gives a
   valid $cr$-neighbor with probability $1-o(1)$. A sampler within total
   variation distance $\varepsilon$ from $\mathsf G_Y$ also returns a valid
   neighbor with probability at least $1-\varepsilon-m^{-3}$.

   Finally, exact Gibbs sampling is the same as Gumbel-perturbed score
   maximization: if $(\Gamma_x)_{x\in S}$ are independent standard Gumbel
   variables, then
   \[
      \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\in S}\{A_Y(x)+\Gamma_x\}
   \]
   has law $\mathsf G_Y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The kernel identity follows from
   \[
      A_Y(x)=\frac t2\|y\|^2-\frac t2\|x-y\|^2 .
   \]
   On the event of \cref{lem:smoothed-max-score-margin}, every invalid point
   $x$ satisfies
   \[
      \exp(A_Y(x))\le \exp(A_Y(p))\exp(-\delta_c t r^2).
   \]
   Hence the total invalid Gibbs weight is at most
   \[
      \frac{m\exp(A_Y(p))\exp(-\delta_c t r^2)}
           {\exp(A_Y(p))}
      =
      m\exp(-\delta_c t r^2)
      \le m^{-3}
   \]
   after increasing $C_c$ so that $\delta_c t r^2\ge4\log m$. The dominated
   and total-variation statements are immediate.

   For the Gumbel representation, let $(E_x)_{x\in S}$ be independent
   exponential random variables with rates $\exp(A_Y(x))$. The probability
   that $E_x$ is the minimum equals its rate divided by the sum of all rates,
   namely $\mathsf G_Y(x)$. Since $E_x=\exp(-A_Y(x))E'_x$ with
   $E'_x\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$, minimizing $E_x$ is the same as maximizing
   $A_Y(x)-\log E'_x$, and $-\log E'_x$ is standard Gumbel.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Captured Gibbs mass gives domination]\label{lem:captured-gibbs-domination}
   Fix $Y$ and a distribution $\mathsf G_Y$ on $S$. Suppose a randomized
   reporter outputs a nonempty set $L_Y\subseteq S$ and, for some
   $\theta>0$, always satisfies
   \[
      \mathsf G_Y(L_Y)\ge\theta.
   \]
   If the final output is sampled from $\mathsf G_Y$ conditioned on $L_Y$,
   then its law $Q_Y$ satisfies
   \[
      Q_Y(x)\le \theta^{-1}\mathsf G_Y(x)
      \qquad\text{for every }x\in S.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Averaging over the reporter randomness,
   \[
      Q_Y(x)
      =
      \E\left[
         \mathbf 1_{\{x\in L_Y\}}
         \frac{\mathsf G_Y(x)}{\mathsf G_Y(L_Y)}
      \right]
      \le
      \theta^{-1}\mathsf G_Y(x).
   \]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Additive Gumbel optimization gives domination]\label{lem:gumbel-additive-domination}
   Fix real scores $(a_x)_{x\in S}$ and let $(\Gamma_x)_{x\in S}$ be
   independent standard Gumbel variables. Let
   \[
      G(x)=\frac{\exp(a_x)}{\sum_{z\in S}\exp(a_z)}.
   \]
   Suppose a certified algorithm, given the realized Gumbel marks, outputs
   $I\in S$ satisfying
   \[
      a_I+\Gamma_I\ge
      \max_{z\in S}(a_z+\Gamma_z)-\Delta
   \]
   always. Then its output law obeys the pointwise domination
   \[
      \Pr[I=x]\le e^\Delta G(x)
      \qquad\text{for every }x\in S.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   If $I=x$, then
   \[
      a_x+\Delta+\Gamma_x
      \ge
      \max_{z\ne x}(a_z+\Gamma_z).
   \]
   The right-hand event is exactly the event that $x$ wins the Gumbel-max race
   after its log-weight is increased by $\Delta$. Hence
   \[
      \Pr[I=x]
      \le
      \frac{\exp(a_x+\Delta)}
           {\exp(a_x+\Delta)+\sum_{z\ne x}\exp(a_z)}
      \le
      e^\Delta G(x).
   \]
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Certified additive Gumbel-MIPS]\label{asm:gumbel-mips}
   Fix $c>1$ and $\rho=\rho_c$. For every bounded-diameter localized node with
   $m$ resident points there is a data structure for one independent marked
   trial. During preprocessing it assigns independent standard Gumbel marks
   $(\Gamma_x)_{x\in S}$. For every query $q$, the query algorithm chooses
   \[
      C_c\log m/r^2\le t\le \log^{O_c(1)}m/r^2,
      \qquad
      Y\sim N(tq,tI_d),
   \]
   and returns a point $I\in S$ with the certified additive guarantee
   \[
      A_Y(I)+\Gamma_I
      \ge
      \max_{x\in S}\{A_Y(x)+\Gamma_x\}
      -O_c(\log\log m)
   \]
   with probability at least $\log^{-O_c(1)}m$, using query time
   \[
      d\,m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m
   \]
   and space
   \[
      m^{1+\rho}\log^{O_c(1)}m
   \]
   over all localized nodes. The additive guarantee is certified or Las Vegas:
   undetected failure is not counted as success.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional additive Gumbel endpoint]\label{prop:gumbel-mips-polylog}
   Under \cref{asm:gumbel-mips}, worst-case Euclidean ANN has query time
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   and space
   \[
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   Work after bounded-diameter localization and repeat the primitive a
   polylogarithmic number of times in a good localized copy containing a near
   pair. For a successful certified call, apply
   \cref{lem:gumbel-additive-domination} with $a_x=A_Y(x)$. Since
   $\Delta=O_c(\log\log m)$, the output law is pointwise dominated by
   $\log^{O_c(1)}m\,\mathsf G_Y$. By
   \cref{lem:smoothed-gibbs-suffices}, the returned point is a valid
   $cr$-neighbor with probability $1-o(1)$ over $Y$ and the Gumbels.
   Polylogarithmic repetition amplifies the inverse-polylogarithmic certified
   success probability to a constant. The query and space bounds sum over the
   localized copies exactly as in \cref{prop:smoothed-gibbs-polylog}.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Smoothed Gaussian guard-or-capture reporter]\label{asm:smoothed-gibbs}
   Fix $c>1$ and $\rho=\rho_c$. For every bounded-diameter localized node with
   $m$ resident points, and for every query $q$, there is a data structure with
   the following guarantee. Choose a parameter
   \[
      C_c\log m/r^2\le t\le \log^{O_c(1)}m/r^2,
   \]
   sample $Y\sim N(tq,tI_d)$, and either report failure or report a nonempty
   list $L_Y\subseteq S$. For every near pair $(p,q)$ in the node, at least one
   of the following alternatives holds.
   \begin{itemize}
      \item The resident guard density is inverse-polylogarithmic at the
      $m^{-\rho}$ scale:
      \[
         \frac1m|\{x\in S:\|x-q\|\le cr\}|
         \ge
         m^{-\rho}\log^{-O_c(1)}m .
      \]
      \item With probability at least $\log^{-O_c(1)}m$ over $Y$ and the
      reporter randomness, the returned list satisfies, for
   \[
      W_Y(A)=\sum_{x\in A}\exp(A_Y(x)),
   \]
      one has
   \[
      W_Y(L_Y)\ge \log^{-O_c(1)}m\,W_Y(S).
   \]
   \end{itemize}
   One trial uses query time
   \[
      d\,m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m
   \]
   and space
   \[
      m^{1+\rho}\log^{O_c(1)}m
   \]
   over all localized nodes.
\end{assumption}

The reporter need not certify which alternative holds, certify the displayed
mass inequality, or estimate $W_Y(S)$. It only has to output the list when it
does not fail; the final restricted Gibbs sample uses the explicitly computable
weights on that list. The inverse-polylogarithmic success probabilities are
amplified in the next proposition.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional smoothed guard-or-capture endpoint]\label{prop:smoothed-gibbs-polylog}
   Under \cref{asm:smoothed-gibbs}, worst-case Euclidean ANN has query time
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   and space
   \[
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   Apply the projected bounded-diameter localization of \cref{lem:localization}
   and work in a good localized copy containing a true near pair. First sample
   a uniform resident guard of size $m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m$ and verify every
   sampled point in the original metric. If the guard-density alternative in
   \cref{asm:smoothed-gibbs} holds, this finds a valid point with constant
   probability after increasing the logarithmic constant.

   Otherwise call the smoothed mass-capture reporter a polylogarithmic number
   of times. Whenever a trial returns a list $L_Y$, sample one point from the
   weights $\exp(A_Y(x))$ restricted to $L_Y$, and verify it in the original
   metric. On a mass-capturing trial, \cref{lem:captured-gibbs-domination}
   makes this output law pointwise dominated by a $\log^{O_c(1)}m$ multiple of
   $\mathsf G_Y$. By \cref{lem:smoothed-gibbs-suffices}, such a trial returns a
   valid point with probability $1-o(1)$. Since the trial is mass-capturing
   with inverse-polylogarithmic probability, polylogarithmic repetition gives
   constant success probability.

   The guard, reporter, and localization costs all fit
   $d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n$, and the assumed local space bounds sum over
   the polylogarithmic localization copies as before.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Smoothed additive score maximization]\label{asm:smoothed-score-max}
   Fix $c>1$ and $\rho=\rho_c$. For every bounded-diameter localized node with
   $m$ resident points, and for every query $q$, there is a data structure that
   chooses a parameter
   \[
      C_c\log m/r^2\le t\le \log^{O_c(1)}m/r^2,
   \]
   samples $Y\sim N(tq,tI_d)$, and returns a point $\widehat x\in S$ satisfying
   \[
      A_Y(\widehat x)
      \ge
      \max_{z\in S}A_Y(z)-\delta_c t r^2/2
   \]
   with probability at least $\log^{-O_c(1)}m$, using query time
   \[
      d\,m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m
   \]
   and space
   \[
      m^{1+\rho}\log^{O_c(1)}m
   \]
   over all localized nodes.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional smoothed score-max endpoint]\label{prop:smoothed-score-max-polylog}
   Under \cref{asm:smoothed-score-max}, worst-case Euclidean ANN has query time
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   and space
   \[
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   Apply the projected bounded-diameter localization of \cref{lem:localization}.
   In each localized node, call the smoothed score-max structure a
   polylogarithmic number of times and verify every returned point in the
   original metric. For any true near pair in a good localized copy,
   \cref{lem:smoothed-max-score-margin} says that an additive
   $\delta_c t r^2/2$ maximizer is valid with probability $1-m^{-2}$ over the
   sampled score direction. The oracle success probability is inverse
   polylogarithmic by assumption, so polylogarithmic amplification gives a
   valid answer with constant probability.

   The assumed local query and space bounds sum over the polylogarithmic
   localization copies exactly as in \cref{prop:multiscale-structured-polylog}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What this shortcut does and does not prove]\label{rem:score-max-shortcut}
   \Cref{lem:smoothed-max-score-margin} is the $\alpha=0$ endpoint of the
   likelihood-ball geometry: if one can approximately maximize the smoothed
   score $A_Y$ for a query-centered Gaussian score direction, the hierarchy,
   dyadic scales, and representative materialization are unnecessary. This is
   a sharper possible route than finite channels.

   The lemma is not itself an ANN data structure. By
   \cref{lem:score-max-additive-nn}, the open algorithmic question becomes
   whether this smoothed additive nearest-neighbor problem has an
   optimal-exponent, output-sensitive implementation. Without such an
   implementation, \cref{asm:smoothed-score-max} should be viewed as a clean
   alternative target, not as a proof of the polylogarithmic endpoint.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why ordinary ANN does not implement the score-max target]\label{rem:score-max-ordinary-ann}
   One can force the perturbed center $y=Y/t$ to be close to $q$ by taking
   $t$ large. In dimension $D$, if
   \[
      t\ge C(D+\log m)/(a^2r^2),
   \]
   then $\|y-q\|\le ar$ with probability at least $1-m^{-3}$. On that event,
   any true near point is within $(1+a)r$ of $y$, while every invalid point is
   farther than $(c-a)r$ from $y$. Thus an ordinary
   \[
      \kappa(a)=\frac{c-a}{1+a}
   \]
   approximate-nearest-neighbor oracle for query center $y$ would return a
   valid $cr$-neighbor after verification.

   This observation is only a sanity check. Since
   $\rho_{\kappa(a)}=\rho_c+O_c(a)$, keeping the exponent loss polylogarithmic
   requires $a=O_c((\log\log m)/\log m)$. The resulting subproblem is then just
   ANN at approximation $c-O_c(a)$ with essentially the same optimal exponent.
   Hence a black-box ordinary ANN call would make the score-max route circular;
   a proof of \cref{asm:smoothed-score-max} must exploit the smoothed
   maximum-score structure itself.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why multiplicative ANN does not implement Gumbel-MIPS]\label{rem:gumbel-mips-not-ann}
   The Gumbel primitive needs an additive score guarantee, not a multiplicative
   distance guarantee. If $B\ge\max_x\Gamma_x$, one may lift
   \[
      X_x=\left(x,\sqrt{2(B-\Gamma_x)/t}\right),
      \qquad
      Y'=(y,0),
   \]
   so that minimizing $\|X_x-Y'\|^2$ is equivalent to maximizing
   $A_Y(x)+\Gamma_x$. However, a $c$-ANN answer in the lifted space gives only
   \[
      \|X_I-Y'\|^2
      \le
      c^2\min_x\|X_x-Y'\|^2,
   \]
   which can translate into additive score error
   \[
      \frac t2(c^2-1)\min_x\|X_x-Y'\|^2.
   \]
   Under the smoothing distribution, this minimum can contain the common
   $\|y-q\|^2$ term, of order $d/t$, so the additive score error can be
   $\Theta(d)$ rather than $O(\log\log m)$.

   The obstruction is real even without Gumbel marks. Take $p=q=0$ and
   $x=Lr e_1$ with $L>c$. For $y=q+g$ and $g\sim N(0,I_d/t)$,
   \[
      \|x-y\|^2-\|p-y\|^2=L^2r^2-2Lrg_1.
   \]
   With high probability this is $\Omega(L^2r^2)$, so the Gaussian weight of
   $x$ is polynomially smaller than that of $p$ when $t=\Theta(\log m/r^2)$.
   In high dimension, nevertheless, the shared orthogonal term
   $\|g_\perp\|^2$ can dominate both distances, making
   $\|x-y\|/\|p-y\|$ arbitrarily close to one. A multiplicative ANN oracle may
   then return $x$ with constant probability, which would violate the
   pointwise domination required by \cref{lem:gumbel-additive-domination}.
\end{remark}

The rank-prefix posterior target itself is captured by the following
assumption, which allows either an implicit channel, a signature tree, a
multiprobe score index, or a direct sampled-prefix reporter as the
implementation.

\begin{assumption}[Multiscale structured likelihood decoder]\label{asm:multiscale-structured-likelihood}
   Fix $c>1$ and $\rho=\rho_c$. For every bounded-diameter localized node with $m$ resident points there is a structured, output-sensitive posterior decoder over dyadic mass scales
   \[
      \xi\in[1/m,\eta_m],
      \qquad
      \eta_m=m^{-\rho}\log^{A_c}m,
   \]
   with the following properties.
   \begin{itemize}
      \item At scale $\xi$, children are certified subsets $C\subseteq S$ with $\xi/2\le |C|/m\le\xi$, and each stored child has a representative point.
      \item One independent group at one scale can be decoded for a query in $d\log^{O_c(1)}m$ work plus output work.
      \item For every query $q$ and every scale $\xi$, the one-group active-child and stored-child moments satisfy
      \[
         \sum_{C\text{ at scale }\xi}\lambda_q(C)\mu(C)^\rho
         \le
         \xi^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m,
         \qquad
         \sum_{C\text{ at scale }\xi}\sigma(C)\mu(C)^{1+\rho}
         \le
         \xi^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m.
      \]
      \item For every near pair $(p,q)$ in the node, either a uniform resident guard of size $m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m$ finds a valid $cr$-neighbor with constant probability, or the pure multiscale value is inverse-polylogarithmic:
      \[
         \sum_{\xi\le\eta_m}
         \sum_{\substack{C\text{ at scale }\xi\\p\in C,\ C\subseteq\Ball(q,cr)}}
         \lambda_q(C)\mu(C)^{-\rho}
         \ge
         \log^{-O_c(1)}m.
      \]
   \end{itemize}
\end{assumption}

The last bullet is deliberately a pre-amplification value condition. At a fixed
scale $\xi$, value $V_\xi$ only implies hit intensity on the order of
$\xi^\rho V_\xi$ in one independent group; the query algorithm below supplies
$\xi^{-\rho}$ groups at that scale. This is the scale-aware version of the
structured-channel condition and is the reason the assumption does not impose a
coarse lower bound such as $\xi\ge m^{-1/(2c^2)}$.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional multiscale structured endpoint]\label{prop:multiscale-structured-polylog}
   Under \cref{asm:multiscale-structured-likelihood}, worst-case Euclidean ANN has query time
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   and space
   \[
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   Work after bounded-diameter localization. At a node of size $m$, first sample the resident guard from \cref{asm:multiscale-structured-likelihood}. If it fails, apply \cref{lem:dyadic-structured-amplification} with
   \[
      h=\log^{-O_c(1)}m,
      \qquad
      \eta=\eta_m=m^{-\rho}\log^{O_c(1)}m.
   \]
   The smallest dyadic scale is $1/m$, so the total local decoding and active-output work is
   \[
      d\,m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m.
   \]
   With constant probability, the decoder activates a stored child $C$ containing the true near point and satisfying $C\subseteq\Ball(q,cr)$. Returning and verifying the stored representative of $C$ therefore succeeds. The guard case also succeeds by definition.

   The expected number of stored child descriptions over all repeated scales is
   \[
      m^{1+\rho}\log^{O_c(1)}m
   \]
   by \cref{lem:dyadic-structured-amplification}. Adding the original data and the bounded-diameter localization copies gives the displayed space bound. Standard truncation and a constant number of independent copies give capped query bounds with constant success probability.
\end{proof}

For comparison, the coarse finite-channel route is recovered by collapsing the continuum of thresholds to one explicit top-$\xi$ child per outcome. Let $\xi\in(0,1)$ and let $C_j^\xi$ be the top $\lceil \xi m\rceil$ points by posterior ratio $R_j(x)$. If $r_{j,1}\ge\cdots\ge r_{j,m}$ are these ratios, put
\[
   \Delta_j=r_{j,1}^{\beta}-r_{j,\lceil\xi m\rceil+1}^{\beta}.
\]
Store $C_j^\xi$ with Poisson mean
\[
   \Lambda_j=\xi^\rho\mu_j^\beta\Delta_j
\]
and activate each copy with probability $K_q(j)^{s_\beta}$. Then the single top-$\xi$ child dominates the restricted part of the negative moment coming from all thresholds with $\Phi_j(u)\le\xi$. Moreover $\mu_j^\beta\Delta_j\le1$, so the expected number of active top-$\xi$ children in one channel group is at most
\[
   \xi^\rho\sum_jK_q(j)^{s_\beta}
   \le
   \xi^\rho B^\beta .
\]
Thus if $B\le\xi^{-1}\log^{O(1)}m$ and $\beta=O(1/\log m)$, one group has only $O_c(\xi^\rho)$ expected active children. This is precisely where explicit enumeration imposes the coarse scale $\xi=m^{-\rho/(1+\rho)}$: evaluating all $B\simeq\xi^{-1}$ outcomes in $\xi^{-\rho}$ repetitions costs $\xi^{-(1+\rho)}$, so fitting the $m^\rho$ budget forces $\xi\ge m^{-\rho/(1+\rho)}$. This finite specialization is therefore a baseline and diagnostic, not the structured target.

\begin{assumption}[Finite restricted channel]\label{asm:finite-restricted-channel}
   Fix $c>1$ and let
   \[
      \rho=\rho_c,
      \qquad
      \vartheta=\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}=\frac1{2c^2},
      \qquad
      \xi=m^{-\vartheta},
      \qquad
      \beta=\Theta_c(1/\log m).
   \]
   There is a query-decodable finite channel with
   \[
      B\le\xi^{-1}\log^{O_c(1)}m
   \]
   outcomes, evaluable on the node in $dB\log^{O_c(1)}m$ work, such that for every near pair $p,q$ in the node, either a uniformly sampled resident guard block of size $O(\xi^{-1}\log n)$ returns a valid $cr$-neighbor with constant probability, or the restricted top-$\xi$ posterior-superlevel negative moment for $p,q$ is at least $\log^{-O_c(1)}m$.
\end{assumption}

\Cref{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls} proves the geometric part of the posterior approach: likelihood level sets are Euclidean balls, and unless a resident guard succeeds there is constant negative-moment value on children that contain the near point and no invalid point. \Cref{lem:ideal-likelihood-scale-profile} proves that the ideal continuous measure also has the exact dyadic query and storage moments required for $m^\rho$ amplification. What is not yet proved is the structured decoding step in \cref{asm:multiscale-structured-likelihood}: an implicit decoder must materialize stored representatives and preserve that pure value across dyadic mass scales with output-sensitive listing. The finite special case below remains useful as a coarse diagnostic; there the missing statement is balanced, query-decodable compilation with only $B\simeq\xi^{-1}$ outcomes.

The corresponding finite special-case diagnostic is to measure the left-hand side of \cref{asm:finite-restricted-channel}. For a proposed finite channel, let $\tau_j$ be the top-$\xi$ posterior threshold of $R_j(x)$ over the data, and for each planted near pair $(p,q)$ compute
\[
   \mathcal H_\xi(p,q)
   =
   \sum_j
   \mu_j^\beta K_q(j)^{1-\beta}
   \left(R_j(p)^\beta-\tau_j^\beta\right)_+ .
\]
Also compute the resident-guard density
\[
   g(q)=\frac1m|\{x\in S:\|x-q\|\le cr\}|.
\]
The conjecture predicts that the lower quantiles of $g(q)+\mathcal H_\xi(p,q)$ should be at worst inverse-polylogarithmic in $m$, and ideally bounded below by a constant.

\begin{lemma}[Posterior-rank margin certificate]\label{lem:posterior-rank-margin}
   Let
   \[
      \mathsf A_\beta(p,q)=\sum_j K_p(j)^\beta K_q(j)^{1-\beta}
   \]
   If $\mathsf A_\beta(p,q)=0$, then the right-hand side below is interpreted as $0$. Otherwise let $W_{p,q}$ be the tilted outcome law
   \[
      W_{p,q}(j)=\frac{K_p(j)^\beta K_q(j)^{1-\beta}}{\mathsf A_\beta(p,q)}.
   \]
   For $\Delta\ge0$, define
   \[
      G_\Delta(p)=\{j:R_j(p)\ge e^\Delta\tau_j\}.
   \]
   Then
   \[
      \mathcal H_\xi(p,q)
      \ge
      (1-e^{-\beta\Delta})\,
      \mathsf A_\beta(p,q)\,
      W_{p,q}(G_\Delta(p)).
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   On $G_\Delta(p)$,
   \[
      R_j(p)^\beta-\tau_j^\beta
      \ge (1-e^{-\beta\Delta})R_j(p)^\beta.
   \]
   Multiplying by $\mu_j^\beta K_q(j)^{1-\beta}$ gives
   \[
      (1-e^{-\beta\Delta})K_p(j)^\beta K_q(j)^{1-\beta}.
   \]
   Summing over $G_\Delta(p)$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

Thus the finite special case would follow from a posterior-rank margin statement: for every near pair not handled by the resident guard, a non-negligible fraction of the tilted affinity must lie on outcomes for which $p$ is inside the top-$\xi$ posterior child by enough margin. The diagnostic should therefore report not only $\mathcal H_\xi$, but also $\mathsf A_\beta(p,q)$, $W_{p,q}(G_\Delta(p))$, and the certified lower bound in \cref{lem:posterior-rank-margin}, for a margin such as $\Delta=1/\beta$. For the balanced-softmax candidate
\[
   K_x(j)=
   \frac{\exp(\lambda_{\rm sm}\ip{a_j}{x}+b_j)}
   {\sum_\ell\exp(\lambda_{\rm sm}\ip{a_\ell}{x}+b_\ell)},
\]
the offsets $b_j$ should first be balanced so that $\mu_j\approx1/B$, and the inverse temperature $\lambda_{\rm sm}$ should be swept rather than fixed by an arbitrary normalization of the panel. Writing
\[
   A(x)=\log\sum_j\exp(\lambda_{\rm sm}\ip{a_j}{x}+b_j),
\]
the unrestricted affinity has the exact form
\[
   \sum_j K_p(j)^\beta K_q(j)^{1-\beta}
   =
   \exp\!\left(A(z)-\beta A(p)-(1-\beta)A(q)\right),
   \qquad z=\beta p+(1-\beta)q.
\]
Moreover the tilted law $W_{p,q}$ is exactly $K_z$. Thus $\lambda_{\rm sm}$ controls both the local curvature $\nabla^2A(x)=\lambda_{\rm sm}^2\operatorname{Cov}_{K_x}(a_j)$ and the effective support of the certificate distribution $K_z$. The diagnostic should compare Gaussian, whitened Gaussian, cross-polytope, and learned panels; report lower quantiles rather than only averages; and track the effective support and Hessian condition number of $K_z$. These measurements distinguish the two hoped-for regimes: well-conditioned softmax curvature gives score separation, while concentrated $K_z$ should give posterior contraction by placing most tilted mass on $G_\Delta(p)$.
One should also separate the resident-guard regime from the genuine finite-channel regime. On the critical random sphere, the guard density can already be constant, so $g(q)$ masks the posterior term. A sharper stress test uses isolated planted pairs with smaller radius, where $g(q)\simeq1/m$ and the lower quantiles of $\mathcal H_\xi(p,q)$ carry the conjectural content.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional finite-channel polylog endpoint]\label{prop:finite-channel-polylog}
   Under \cref{asm:finite-restricted-channel}, the posterior-superlevel recursion gives worst-case Euclidean ANN bounds
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n,
      \qquad
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   At a node of size $m$, repeat the finite channel
   \[
      R=\xi^{-\rho}\log^{O_c(1)}m
   \]
   times. By \cref{asm:finite-restricted-channel} and the top-$\xi$ collapse, a true near point enters at least one active child with inverse-polylogarithmic probability, unless the resident guard already succeeds. The local channel-evaluation work is
   \[
      dRB\log^{O_c(1)}m
      \le
      d\,\xi^{-(1+\rho)}\log^{O_c(1)}m
      =
      d\,m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m,
   \]
   because $\vartheta(1+\rho)=\rho$.

   The expected number of active recursive children over all repetitions is polylogarithmic, since one group contributes $O_c(\xi^\rho)$ active children. Every recursive child has size at most $\xi m=m^{1-\vartheta}$. Hence the query recurrence is
   \[
      Q(m)\le d\,m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m+\log^{O_c(1)}m\,Q(m^{1-\vartheta}),
   \]
   which solves to $d\,m^\rho\log^{O_c(1)}m$ over $O_c(\log\log m)$ macro levels.

   For space, each stored child has size at most $\xi m$. The total stored-child $(1+\rho)$ power over all repeated channel groups is bounded by
   \[
      R\cdot B\cdot(\xi m)^{1+\rho}\log^{O_c(1)}m
      \le
      m^{1+\rho}\log^{O_c(1)}m,
   \]
   again using $RB=\xi^{-(1+\rho)}\log^{O_c(1)}m$. Summing over macro levels and adding the original data storage gives the displayed space bound. Polylogarithmic amplification and the standard truncation paragraph convert the inverse-polylogarithmic local success into constant success probability.
\end{proof}

\section{Audit trail and extensions}

The main self-contained proof path is the restartable slice route of \cref{prop:slice-two-thirds}. The following notes explain which pieces are closed, which one-tree milestones are conditional, and which older formulations remain useful as comparisons or possible extensions.

\paragraph{Spherical stage.}
The continuation-mass spherical decoder is proved in \cref{lem:continuation-work}, \cref{lem:good-first-moment}, \cref{lem:tame-second-moment}, and \cref{cor:spherical-survival}. The one-sided analytical upper barrier controls prolific early prefixes in the proof, while the algorithm itself enforces only continuation-mass pruning. Independently, \cref{thm:smooth-product-terminal} gives a constant-recall terminal spherical engine by smooth product filtering. The adaptive decoder is for carrying score through localization; the smooth product filter is for the final localized spherical search.

\paragraph{Restartable \(2/3\) stage.}
\Cref{prop:slice-two-thirds} proves the restartable $2/3$ rung and is the proof of \cref{thm:main}. It reaches a subpolynomial overhead using deliberate restarts and leaf-conditioned slices.

\paragraph{Randomized \(1/3\) stage.}
\Cref{prop:one-third} records the cube-root parameter choice from the one-tree notes. The direct tube calculation is kept only as intuition; the formal proposition uses \cref{lem:adaptive-continuation} for whole-lineage score survival and assumes only the cube-root geometric frontier inequality \cref{asm:mixed-operator}. Thus the central open issue is already the work-side first-hit accounting, not near-pair score survival.

\paragraph{Randomized polylog stage.}
\Cref{lem:adaptive-continuation} replaces the tube proof in the predictable-score setting, so near-pair survival is no longer the obstacle for the randomized one-tree endpoint. \Cref{asm:mixed-continuation-operator} is the direct remaining work-side target after \cref{lem:cap-free-scan-accounting}: it must prove thinned cap-split contraction over branching cap-discovery frontiers. Conditional on that operator, \cref{prop:randomized-polylog} shows that choosing $\eta\asymp(\log\log n)/\log n$ gives polylogarithmic query and space overhead. A sharper budget-thinned variant is partly proved: \cref{lem:canonical-mixed-budget} and \cref{lem:budget-thinned-opportunities} give the activated geometric work bound by optional stopping, while \cref{lem:canonical-budget-activation} reduces the remaining near-lineage issue to the value-delivery product in \cref{rem:value-delivery-target}.

\paragraph{Structured posterior route.}
\Cref{lem:posterior-superlevel-compiler} records the negative-moment identity suggested by the latest notes. \Cref{lem:explicit-enumeration-barrier} makes the coarse-child obstruction precise: the scale $\xi=m^{-1/(2c^2)}$ is the price of explicitly enumerating $B\simeq\xi^{-1}$ outcomes, not a geometric limit. \Cref{lem:scale-window-compiler} and \cref{lem:dyadic-structured-amplification} isolate the important arithmetic: if mass windows can be decoded output-sensitively, then $\xi^{-\rho}$ repetitions at each dyadic scale convert negative-moment value into constant hit probability, and summing all scales down to $1/m$ still fits the $m^\rho$ query budget. This scale-aware amplification is essential; a raw negative-moment diagnostic alone does not imply that the near point is actually listed with inverse-polylogarithmic probability.

The continuous likelihood-ball family now proves both analytic sides of the desired posterior decoder: \cref{thm:continuous-likelihood-balls} gives the guard-or-pure-child value, and \cref{lem:ideal-likelihood-scale-profile} gives the scale-normalized query and storage moments. \Cref{lem:likelihood-rank-prefix} further collapses the threshold continuum to rank gaps of a random score order. The cleanest structured target is therefore \cref{asm:multiscale-structured-likelihood}: implement this ideal rank-prefix measure as a query-decodable family of stored representatives. Conditional on that theorem, \cref{prop:multiscale-structured-polylog} gives the desired polylogarithmic ANN bounds in one posterior layer; if the activated prefix is pure for the query, its representative is already a valid answer.

\Cref{lem:smoothed-max-score-margin} gives a still simpler non-channel target: a query-centered Gaussian score direction separates every invalid point from the true near point by an additive $\Theta_c(tr^2)$ score margin, with high probability. The softest resulting primitive is \cref{asm:smoothed-gibbs}: with inverse-polylogarithmic probability, report a list carrying inverse-polylogarithmic mass under the Gaussian Gibbs kernel on data points. Sampling from the kernel restricted to that list gives a pointwise dominated output law by \cref{lem:captured-gibbs-domination}, and \cref{lem:smoothed-gibbs-suffices} then returns a valid answer because invalid points have negligible Gibbs mass and cannot be oversampled too much. A more exact sampling route is certified additive Gumbel-MIPS: \cref{lem:gumbel-additive-domination} shows that an $O(\log\log m)$ additive optimizer for the Gumbel-perturbed scores gives precisely the required one-sided Gibbs domination, yielding \cref{prop:gumbel-mips-polylog}. A stronger but different implementation route is smoothed additive nearest-neighbor search for the perturbed center $Y/t$ with additive squared-distance error $\Theta_c(r^2)$, exactly equivalent to additive score maximization by \cref{lem:score-max-additive-nn}. These oracles would bypass dyadic scales and representatives entirely, as recorded in \cref{prop:smoothed-gibbs-polylog}, \cref{prop:gumbel-mips-polylog}, and \cref{prop:smoothed-score-max-polylog}. They are targets, not implemented theorems; \cref{rem:gumbel-mips-not-ann} explains why black-box multiplicative ANN does not supply the Gumbel primitive. The older finite restricted-channel compiler \cref{asm:finite-restricted-channel} and \cref{prop:finite-channel-polylog} remain as a coarse explicit-enumeration special case and as a useful diagnostic framework.

\paragraph{Cohort-or-leaf.}
\Cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf} is the original full-child route-level condition for the broader posterior-cohort endpoint, but \cref{lem:prefix-slice-accounting} shows that a prefix-conditioned formulation is cleaner. Work accounting for opened children is no longer the hard part: \cref{lem:vote-budget} pays opened recursive calls from realized detector votes. The preferred remaining target for that more general route is \cref{asm:prefix-slice-survival}: prove that one cumulative near-pair score path either reaches a cap-free residual scan or receives enough detector votes to follow prefix-conditioned certified slices to a resident scan. \Cref{lem:detector-inflation} and \cref{lem:off-spine-inflation} reduce the detector-shadowing part of this target to early activation prefixes with large enough continuation mass $H(v)$ and logarithmic spine-ratio sum $\sum H(u^+)/H(u)$; \cref{rem:linear-spine-ratio} explains why long score routes need an aggregate detector-mass argument instead. This should be tested against the hard cases: an isolated near point next to a massive far cluster, a near point on a cluster boundary, a tiny child that becomes heavy only late, and symmetric far shells. The proof must show a whole-lineage inverse-polylogarithmic event, not merely an inverse-polylogarithmic event at each level.

\section{Conclusion}

The algorithmic target is a cumulative score-and-ball tree with query time
\[
   d\,n^{1/(2c^2-1)}\log^{O_c(1)}n
\]
and space
\[
   dn+n^{1+1/(2c^2-1)}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
\]
The exact pieces now aligned for the randomized cap-thinning route are: continuation-mass pruning gives optimal expected spherical work, adaptive continuation gives whole-lineage near survival, thinned cap splitting gives radius-potential contraction, and cap-free terminal scans are paid by low-correlation collisions.

For the randomized cap-thinning route, one remaining task is the branching first-split work estimate in \cref{asm:mixed-continuation-operator}: prove that the thinned geometric child potentials contract over random cap-discovery frontiers without replacing the branching decoder by a normalized path law. The newer budget-thinned variant proves the expected work bound from a canonical prefix supermartingale, and leaves the sharper value-delivery target in \cref{rem:value-delivery-target}. This is a fully non-channel route; the smooth product terminal filter can be used after localization and no longer contributes an inverse-polylogarithmic table-recall penalty. The structured posterior route replaces the cap-frontier obstacle by \cref{asm:multiscale-structured-likelihood}: prove an output-sensitive reporter for pure likelihood rank prefixes, not a small explicitly enumerable channel. The smoothed sampling route replaces both by \cref{asm:smoothed-gibbs} or \cref{asm:gumbel-mips}: prove either an optimal-exponent reporter that captures inverse-polylogarithmic Gaussian Gibbs mass, or a certified $O(\log\log n)$-additive Gumbel-MIPS primitive. Smoothed additive nearest neighbor is another stronger sufficient primitive. The detector-vote cohort-or-leaf route is a separate possible generalization to arbitrary certified child hierarchies, where the remaining issue is near-lineage coupling between ordinary continuation paths and detector activations.

\section{More detail on the spherical exponent calculation}

For completeness, we verify the algebra leading to $\rho(a,b)$. Let $B$ be the branching factor and $\ell_B=\log B$. With
\[
   t^2=(1+a)\ell_B
\]
and
\[
   k=
   \frac{2(1+b)\log m}
   {(1+a)(1-b)\ell_B}
   +O(1),
\]
the expected number of accepting leaves for one point has logarithm
\begin{align*}
   k\ell_B-\frac{kt^2}{2}+O(\log k)
                                                                                                                  &=
                                                                                                                  \frac{k\ell_B(1-a)}{2}+O(\log k)\\
                                                                                                                                                                                                 &=
                                                                                                                                                                                                 \frac{(1-a)(1+b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}\log m+O(\log\log m).
\end{align*}
Thus it equals
\[
   m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O(1)}m.
\]

For a pair of correlation at most $b$, the expected number of common accepting leaves has logarithm at most
\begin{align*}
   k\ell_B-\frac{kt^2}{1+b}
                                                                                                                  &=
                                                                                                                  k\ell_B\left(1-\frac{1+a}{1+b}\right)\\
                                                                                                                                                                                                 &=-\frac{k\ell_B(a-b)}{1+b}\\
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           &=-\frac{2(a-b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}\log m+O(1).
\end{align*}
A direct calculation shows
\[
   1-\rho(a,b)
   =
   \frac{2(a-b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}.
\]
Hence one ordinary far point contributes at most
\[
   m^{-(1-\rho(a,b))}\log^{O(1)}m
\]
common leaves, and all $m$ ordinary far points contribute at most
\[
   m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O(1)}m.
\]

\section{Bellman cancellation algebra}

We record the cancellation with no surrounding notation. Let $s=1-\rho$, let a parent contain $m$ points in radius $R$, and let a child have relative size $\alpha$ and relative radius $\lambda$. With
\[
   F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}
\]
and activation threshold $\tau=\lambda^2$, the first-hit bound charges at most $(\alpha/\lambda^2)^s$ query mass to the child. The recursive charge is therefore
\begin{align*}
   \left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda^2}\right)^s
   F(\alpha m,\lambda R)
   &=
   \alpha^s\lambda^{-2s}
   \alpha^\rho m^\rho
   \lambda^{2s}\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}\\
   &=
   \alpha F(m,R).
\end{align*}
Thus the radius improvement pays exactly for the posterior threshold, and disjoint children sum to at most the parent potential.
