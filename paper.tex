\title{A Cumulative-Score Route to Polylogarithmic Loss\\
for Optimal Data-Dependent Euclidean ANN}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{Draft, June 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
   For fixed $c>1$, the optimal balanced exponent for data-dependent approximate nearest neighbor search in Euclidean space is
   \[
      \rho_c=\frac{1}{2c^2-1}.
   \]
   Known worst-case constructions achieve $n^{\rho_c+o(1)}$ query time and $n^{1+\rho_c+o(1)}$ space. The goal here is to remove the subpolynomial loss, not merely optimize it. We describe a single cumulative score-and-ball process intended to achieve
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n,
      \qquad
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
   The process has three parts. First, local spherical filtering uses a cumulative Gaussian score tree with continuation-mass pruning rather than levelwise hard thresholds. Second, geometric children are activated by an exact posterior stopping rule: a child of radius $\lambda R$ is entered when its posterior reaches $\tau=\lambda^2$. The resulting Bellman cancellation pays for recursion with no dependence on the number or balance of children. Third, tempered detector cohorts materialize this fractional activation while ordinary paths continue to search residual leaves. We prove the spherical continuation-mass decoder with polylogarithmic loss and prove the exact Bellman identities. The remaining probabilistic task is the route-level cohort-or-leaf lemma. Under that lemma, the structure has the polylogarithmic bounds above.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The $(c,r)$-approximate nearest neighbor problem asks us to preprocess a set $P\subseteq\R^d$ of $n$ points so that, given a query $q\in\R^d$ satisfying
\[
   \min_{p\in P}\|p-q\|_2\le r,
\]
the data structure returns some $p'\in P$ with
\[
   \|p'-q\|_2\le cr.
\]
Throughout the paper, $c>1$ is fixed. The data structure is randomized and data-dependent, and succeeds with a fixed constant probability. Candidate points are verified using their true Euclidean distances, so the structure never reports an incorrect answer.

For Euclidean space, the optimal balanced data-dependent exponent is
\[
   \rho_c=\frac{1}{2c^2-1}.
\]
The landmark construction of Andoni and Razenshteyn obtains query time $d\,n^{\rho_c+o(1)}$ and space $dn+n^{1+\rho_c+o(1)}$ for worst-case data sets~\cite{AR15}. Its geometric component removes dense spherical caps, recenters them as smaller certified balls, and applies a spherical filtering primitive to the residual data. The $o(1)$ term absorbs several losses: shell discretization, geometric slack, spherical list decoding, and the repeated restarting of the filtering process after recentering.

The purpose of this draft is to stop optimizing that restart loss and instead remove it. The central design choice is to keep one cumulative score process across geometric recentering. Recentered children are not fresh spherical instances; they are cohorts inside the same score tree. This forces two changes:
\begin{itemize}
   \item the spherical decoder must survive with only polylogarithmic loss under continuation-mass pruning;
   \item geometric recursion must be paid by posterior activation rather than by restarting a full score tree in every child.
\end{itemize}
The first point is a one-sided branching-random-walk problem. The second point has an exact algebraic cancellation, proved below. The only remaining coupling issue is to turn the fractional posterior activation into integer cohorts without losing the exponent.

\subsection{Main result}

All logarithms are natural.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional main theorem]\label{thm:main}
   Fix $c>1$. Assume the route-level cohort-or-leaf statement of \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf}. Then there is a randomized data-dependent data structure for worst-case $(c,r)$-approximate nearest neighbor search in $\R^d$ with constant success probability, query time
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n,
   \]
   and space
   \[
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n,
   \]
   where $\rho_c=1/(2c^2-1)$. The bounds hold in expectation over preprocessing; standard truncation and rebuilding convert them to capped bounds with only constant-factor changes in success probability and asymptotic cost.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{What changes}

The algorithm in this draft differs from a restart construction in the following ways:
\begin{itemize}
   \item score prefixes are kept or discarded by continuation mass, not by a fresh hard threshold at every level;
   \item recentering changes the geometric coordinates but preserves the cumulative score state through an explicit offset;
   \item a child of relative radius $\lambda$ is activated at posterior threshold $\lambda^2$;
   \item ordinary paths and detector cohorts are separate populations, so cluster activation does not consume ordinary near-pair search paths;
   \item every geometric child is certified by its actual maximum radius.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where the proof stands}

The expected-work calculation for continuation-mass pruning is exact. The posterior stopping inequality and radius Bellman cancellation are exact. The remaining work is probabilistic: prove that, over the whole near-neighbor lineage, the true near point is either exposed by ordinary continuation-mass pruning in a searchable leaf, or every certified child on the lineage receives enough detector votes to be activated. We state this as \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf} because it is the only place where ordinary paths, detector cohorts, and posterior martingales must be coupled. A merely local inverse-polylogarithmic guarantee at each geometric level would not be enough; those losses could multiply over polylogarithmically many levels.

\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Correlation parameters}

For unit vectors $x,q\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$,
\[
   \|x-q\|_2^2=2-2\ip{x}{q}.
\]
A spherical filtering instance is specified by two correlations
\[
   -1<b<a<1.
\]
A near pair satisfies $\ip{x}{q}\ge a$, while an ordinary far pair satisfies $\ip{x}{q}\le b$. The balanced Gaussian filtering exponent is
\[
   \rho(a,b)
   =
   \frac{(1-a)(1+b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}.
\]
At the critical Euclidean normalization,
\[
   a_0=1-\frac{1}{c^2},
   \qquad
   b_0=0,
\]
and therefore
\[
   \rho(a_0,b_0)=\frac{1}{2c^2-1}=\rho_c.
\]

We repeatedly use the smoothness of $\rho$ near $(a_0,b_0)$.

\begin{lemma}[Stability of the balanced exponent]\label{lem:rho-stability}
   For every fixed $c>1$, there are constants $C_c>0$ and $\eta_c>0$ such that, whenever $0<\eta\le\eta_c$ and
   \[
      a\ge a_0-C_c\eta,
      \qquad
      b\le C_c\eta,
   \]
   we have
   \[
      \rho(a,b)\le \rho_c+C_c\eta.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The denominator $(1+a)(1-b)$ is bounded away from zero in a fixed neighborhood of $(a_0,0)$. The rational function $\rho(a,b)$ is continuously differentiable there. The claim follows from the mean-value theorem, after enlarging $C_c$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Polylogarithmic slack}

All geometric discretization parameters are chosen as inverse powers of $\log n$ with constants depending only on $c$. We write $\log^{O_c(1)}n$ for any resulting polynomial in these logarithmic factors. The point of the cumulative-score construction is that every nonconstant loss should be absorbed into this notation; no factor of the form $\exp((\log n)^\beta)$ is allowed in the target theorem.

\subsection{Expected cost and truncation}

The filtering constructions naturally give expected numbers of explored nodes and candidate occurrences. We use the following standard conversion. If one copy succeeds with probability at least $p_0>0$ and has expected query work at most $T$, stop it after $K T$ work for a sufficiently large constant $K=K(p_0)$. Markov's inequality shows that the truncation probability is at most $1/K$, so the truncated copy still succeeds with constant probability. A constant number of independent copies then restores any desired fixed success probability below one. The same idea applies to space: rebuild whenever the realized space exceeds a fixed multiple of its expectation. We therefore state expected bounds in intermediate lemmas and capped bounds in the final theorem.

\section{The cumulative-score spherical primitive}

The spherical stage uses one cumulative Gaussian score tree and prunes by continuation mass. This avoids the two-sided tube that causes the $\exp(O(k^{1/3}))$ loss in the earlier analysis.

Choose a constant branching factor $B\ge2$, write $\ell_B=\log B$, and let
\[
   t^2=(1+a)\ell_B.
\]
A path of depth $k$ carries independent Gaussian edge vectors $g_e\sim N(0,I_d)$ and cumulative score
\[
   S_v(x)=\sum_{e\preceq v}\ip{g_e}{x}.
\]
The depth is
\[
   k
   =
   \left\lceil
      \frac{2(1+b)\log m}
      {(1+a)(1-b)\ell_B}
   \right\rceil
   =
   \Theta_{a,b}(\log m),
\]
and a leaf accepts $x$ when $S_v(x)\ge kt$.

For a node $v$ at level $\ell$ with score $s$, put $r=k-\ell$ and define the continuation mass
\[
   H_\ell(s)
   =
   B^r
   \overline\Phi\!\left(\frac{kt-s}{\sqrt r}\right).
\]
This is exactly the expected number of accepting descendant leaves of $v$, conditional on the current score $s$. The decoder retains a prefix only while
\[
   H_\ell(S_v(x))\ge k^{-A}
\]
for a sufficiently large constant $A=A_{a,b}$. Equivalently, up to lower-order tail terms, a retained prefix satisfies
\[
   S_v(x)\ge
   kt-\sqrt{2r(r\log B+A\log k)}.
\]
Early scores may lie well below the final linear trajectory; the barrier tightens only near the leaf. See \cref{fig:barrier}.

\begin{lemma}[Exact expected work]\label{lem:continuation-work}
   For fixed $-1<b<a<1$, continuation-mass pruning gives expected query path work
   \[
      m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m,
   \]
   expected ordinary-far candidate occurrences
   \[
      m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m,
   \]
   and stored point-path occurrences
   \[
      m^{1+\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   At any fixed level, summing $H_\ell(S_v(x))$ over all prefixes $v$ counts the expected number of accepting descendant leaves for $x$. Since every retained prefix has continuation mass at least $k^{-A}$, the expected number of retained prefixes at that level is at most $k^A$ times the expected number of accepting leaves. Summing over $k$ levels gives only a polynomial factor in $k$.

   The expected number of accepting leaves for one point is
   \[
      B^k\overline\Phi(t\sqrt{k})
      =
      m^{\rho(a,b)}k^{O(1)}.
   \]
   For a pair of correlation at most $b$, the Gaussian joint-tail estimate
   \[
      \Prb[X\ge u,\,Y\ge u]
      \le
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{1+b}\right)
   \]
   gives expected common accepting leaves at most
   \[
      m^{-(1-\rho(a,b))}k^{O(1)}.
   \]
   Summing over $m$ ordinary far points proves the far-candidate bound. Summing the one-point bound over stored data points proves the storage bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Tame good near leaf]\label{def:good-near-leaf}
   Fix a near pair $(p,q)$ with $\ip{p}{q}=a$. For a prefix $u$ at level $\ell$, write
   \[
      Y_u(x)=S_u(x)-\ell t
   \]
   and define the centered common coordinate
   \[
      M_u=\frac{Y_u(p)+Y_u(q)}{\sqrt{2(1+a)}}.
   \]
   A leaf $v$ is tame good for $(p,q)$ if
   \[
      S_v(p),S_v(q)\in[kt,kt+1]
   \]
   every prefix $u\preceq v$ satisfies the continuation-mass pruning rule for both $p$ and $q$, and every prefix satisfies the analytical upper barrier
   \[
      M_u\le H_{a,b}
   \]
   for a sufficiently large fixed constant $H_{a,b}$. This upper barrier is used only in the proof; the algorithm does not enforce it.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Tame-good first moment]\label{lem:good-first-moment}
   For every fixed $-1<b<a<1$ and sufficiently large pruning constant $A$, if $Z$ is the number of tame good near leaves for a pair of correlation exactly $a$, then
   \[
      \E Z\ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Work at the critical correlation $a$, which is the least favorable near case. Exponentially tilt the two-dimensional Gaussian increment $(X,Y)$ so that both coordinates have mean $t$. Since
   \[
      \Sigma=
      \begin{pmatrix}
         1&a\\
         a&1
      \end{pmatrix},
      \qquad
      \theta=\frac{t}{1+a}(1,1),
   \]
   the tilted law has mean $\Sigma\theta=(t,t)$ and
   \[
      \frac12\theta^\top\Sigma\theta=\frac{t^2}{1+a}=\ell_B.
   \]
   Let $Q$ denote the tilted measure. On the endpoint event
   \[
      S_v(p),S_v(q)\in[kt,kt+1],
   \]
   the likelihood ratio satisfies
   \[
      B^k\frac{dP}{dQ}=\exp(O_{a,b}(1)).
   \]
   Therefore the expected number of tame good leaves under the original measure is, up to constant factors, the $Q$-probability that one tilted two-dimensional walk has endpoint in the unit square, stays above the two continuation barriers, and stays below the common-coordinate upper barrier.

   Under $Q$, after subtracting the linear drift $\ell t$, each coordinate is a centered Gaussian walk. The endpoint has covariance $k\Sigma$, so the probability that both centered endpoints lie in $[0,1]$ is $\Theta_{a,b}(1/k)$.

   It remains to check that the path barriers do not cost more than a polynomial factor. Write $r=k-\ell$ and let
   \[
      d_r=\sqrt{2r(r\ell_B+A\log k)}-rt.
   \]
   A prefix at level $\ell$ is retained whenever the centered coordinate is at least $-d_r$, up to harmless constant tail corrections. Since $t^2=(1+a)\ell_B<2\ell_B$, there is a constant $c_{a,b}>0$ such that
   \[
      d_r\ge c_{a,b}r
      \qquad\text{for } r\ge C_{a,b}\log k,
   \]
   and, for $1\le r\le C_{a,b}\log k$,
   \[
      d_r\ge c_{a,b}\sqrt{A r\log k}
   \]
   after increasing $A$. Conditioned on endpoints in $[0,1]^2$, each coordinate is a Gaussian bridge whose variance at distance $r$ from the endpoint is $O(r)$. A union bound over $r$ therefore gives failure probability
   \[
      \sum_{r\ge C\log k}\exp(-\Omega_{a,b}(r))
      +
      \sum_{1\le r\le C\log k} k^{-\Omega_{a,b}(A)}
      =
      o(1)
   \]
   for $A$ large enough.

   The common coordinate $M_u$ is a standard one-dimensional centered Gaussian bridge, independent of the difference coordinate. By the reflection principle for a Gaussian bridge, conditioned on its endpoint lying in a fixed bounded interval below $H_{a,b}$,
   \[
      \Prb_Q\!\left[\max_{u\preceq v}M_u\le H_{a,b}\right]
      \ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
   On this upper-barrier event, violation of either continuation lower barrier would force the difference-coordinate bridge to make an excursion of size at least $d_r-O_{a,b}(1)$ at distance $r$ from the endpoint. The same union bound as above shows that this has probability $o(1)$ after increasing $A$. Thus, conditional on the endpoint event, all barriers in the definition of tame good hold with probability at least $k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}$. Hence $\E Z\ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tame-good second moment]\label{lem:tame-second-moment}
   For every fixed $-1<b<a<1$, the tame-good leaf count $Z$ from \cref{lem:good-first-moment} satisfies
   \[
      \E Z^2\le k^{O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   We decompose pairs of leaves by the depth $j$ of their lowest common ancestor. Write $r=k-j$, and first consider $0\le j<k$. There are at most $B^{2k-j}$ ordered pairs with split depth $j$.

   Use the independent common and difference coordinates
   \[
      C_\ell=\frac{S_\ell(p)+S_\ell(q)}{\sqrt{2(1+a)}},
      \qquad
      D_\ell=\frac{S_\ell(p)-S_\ell(q)}{\sqrt{2(1-a)}}.
   \]
   The common coordinate has unit Gaussian increments and endpoint threshold
   \[
      \mu k,
      \qquad
      \mu=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+a}}\,t=\sqrt{2\ell_B}.
   \]
   If both leaves are tame good, then at the split prefix the centered common value $y=C_j-\mu j$ satisfies $y\le H_{a,b}$, and both common-coordinate suffixes end in a fixed unit interval around $\mu k$. Ignoring all other constraints, the common-coordinate probability is at most
   \[
      \int_{-\infty}^{H_{a,b}}
      \frac{1}{\sqrt{j+1}}
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\mu j+y)^2}{2j}\right)
      \left[
         \frac{C}{\sqrt r}
         \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\mu r-y)^2}{2r}\right)
      \right]^2dy,
   \]
   with the evident interpretation when $j=0$. Multiplying by the pair count $B^{2k-j}$ and using $\mu^2=2\ell_B$ cancels the exponential main term and leaves
   \[
      C_{a,b}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j+1}\,r}
      \int_{-\infty}^{H_{a,b}}\exp(\mu y)\,dy
      \le
      \frac{C_{a,b}}{\sqrt{j+1}\,r}.
   \]

   The difference coordinates for the two leaves are centered jointly Gaussian with variances $k$ and covariance $j$. The event that both endpoint differences lie in the fixed interval forced by
   \[
      S_v(p),S_v(q),S_w(p),S_w(q)\in[kt,kt+1]
   \]
   has probability at most the maximum bivariate Gaussian density times a constant area:
   \[
      O_{a,b}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^2-j^2}}\right)
      =
      O_{a,b}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{kr}}\right).
   \]
   Since the common and difference coordinates are independent, the total contribution of split depth $j<k$ to $\E Z^2$ is at most
   \[
      \frac{C_{a,b}}{\sqrt{j+1}\,r^{3/2}\sqrt k}.
   \]
   Summing over $j<k$ gives a bounded quantity:
   \[
      \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
      \frac{1}{\sqrt{j+1}\,(k-j)^{3/2}\sqrt k}
      =
      O(1).
   \]

   The diagonal contribution $v=w$ is at most the first moment of accepting near leaves, hence at most $k^{O_{a,b}(1)}$ by the same endpoint calculation used in \cref{lem:good-first-moment}. Therefore $\E Z^2\le k^{O_{a,b}(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-sided spherical survival]\label{cor:spherical-survival}
   If a query $q$ has a data point $p$ with $\ip{p}{q}\ge a$, then the continuation-mass decoder produces a common accepting leaf for $(p,q)$ with probability at least $\log^{-O_{a,b}(1)}m$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
   By \cref{lem:good-first-moment} and \cref{lem:tame-second-moment}, Paley--Zygmund gives
   \[
      \Prb[Z>0]\ge \frac{(\E Z)^2}{\E Z^2}
      \ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
   Since $k=\Theta_{a,b}(\log m)$, this is the desired inverse-polylogarithmic survival probability. Correlation larger than $a$ can only improve the tilted endpoint exponent; the same constants cover a fixed compact neighborhood of the critical parameters after the usual slack adjustment.
\end{proof}

The spherical primitive therefore has polylogarithmic loss: expected work follows from continuation mass, and near-pair survival follows from the tame-good first and second moments. No algorithmic two-sided tube is imposed.

\subsection{Budgeted score blocks}

The spherical block can be run with a budget parameter larger than the number of resident points. This is useful in the Euclidean recursion because the natural query potential is
\[
   F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2(1-\rho)},
\]
not just $m^\rho$.

\begin{corollary}[Budgeted continuation block]\label{cor:budgeted-block}
   Fix $-1<b<a<1$ and write $\rho=\rho(a,b)$. Let a spherical node contain $m$ points, and let $N\ge m$. Run the continuation-mass decoder exactly as above, except choose the depth
   \[
      k
      =
      \left\lceil
         \frac{2(1+b)\log N}
         {(1+a)(1-b)\ell_B}
      \right\rceil .
   \]
   Then the expected query path work is
   \[
      N^\rho\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}N,
   \]
   the expected ordinary-far candidate occurrences are at most
   \[
      mN^{-(1-\rho)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}N
      \le
      N^\rho\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}N,
   \]
   and the stored point-path occurrences are at most
   \[
      mN^\rho\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}N.
   \]
   If a query has a near point with correlation at least $a$, the success probability is at least $\log^{-O_{a,b}(1)}N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
   The proof of \cref{lem:continuation-work} depends on the depth only through the expected one-point accepting-leaf count and the far joint-tail count. With the displayed depth these become $N^\rho\log^{O(1)}N$ and $N^{-(1-\rho)}\log^{O(1)}N$, respectively. Summing the latter over the $m$ resident points gives the stated far-candidate bound, and $m\le N$ gives the displayed simplification. The storage bound is the one-point accepting count summed over $m$ data points.

   The first- and second-moment near-pair arguments in \cref{lem:good-first-moment}, \cref{lem:tame-second-moment}, and \cref{cor:spherical-survival} use only the score depth $k=\Theta_{a,b}(\log N)$ and the fixed correlations. Replacing $m$ by $N$ in that depth therefore gives success probability $\log^{-O_{a,b}(1)}N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Radius-budgeted score block]\label{cor:radius-budgeted-block}
   Fix $\rho=\rho_c$ and $s=1-\rho$. At a Euclidean node with $m$ points in radius $R\ge r$, set
   \[
      N=m\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s/\rho}.
   \]
   Then one continuation-mass score block has expected query path work and ordinary-far candidate occurrences
   \[
      F(m,R)\log^{O_c(1)}N,
      \qquad
      F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s},
   \]
   stored point-path occurrences
   \[
      mF(m,R)\log^{O_c(1)}N,
   \]
   and inverse-polylogarithmic near-pair survival.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
   Since $R\ge r$, the chosen $N$ is at least $m$. Also
   \[
      N^\rho=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}=F(m,R).
   \]
   Apply \cref{cor:budgeted-block}. In the bounded-diameter instances used below, $\log N=\log^{O_c(1)}n$, so the logarithmic factors are absorbed into $\log^{O_c(1)}n$.
\end{proof}

\section{The certified score-and-ball process}

We now describe the Euclidean part as one branching process. A node of the process carries both a cumulative Gaussian score state and a certified Euclidean ball. Gaussian score edges perform filtering inside the current ball; recentering edges are deterministic branches that move to a smaller certified ball while retaining the score prefix and updating the coordinate offset. Thus the geometric recursion is not a separate wrapper around the score tree.

\subsection{Two elementary geometric facts}

The first fact certifies the radius decrease obtained from a spherical cap.

\begin{lemma}[Cap recentering]\label{lem:cap}
   Let $P$ lie on the sphere of radius $R$ centered at $o$, and suppose
   \[
      \ip{(p-o)/R}{w}\ge\gamma
   \]
   for every $p$ in a cap $C\subseteq P$, where $\|w\|_2=1$. Then
   \[
      C\subseteq
      \Ball\!\left(o+\gamma Rw,\,R\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}\right).
   \]
   In particular, if $\gamma=\Theta_c(\eta)$, the certified child radius satisfies
   \[
      R_C\le R(1-\Theta_c(\eta^2)).
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   For $p\in C$,
   \begin{align*}
      \|p-(o+\gamma Rw)\|_2^2
                                           &=\|p-o\|_2^2+\gamma^2R^2-2\gamma R\ip{p-o}{w}\\
                                           &\le R^2+\gamma^2R^2-2\gamma^2R^2\\
                                           &=R^2(1-\gamma^2).
   \end{align*}
   The final estimate follows from $\sqrt{1-x}=1-\Theta(x)$ for sufficiently small positive $x$.
\end{proof}

The second fact shows that thin radial shells do not degrade the approximation factor.

\begin{lemma}[Angular gap on two shells]\label{lem:shell}
   Let data points have radius $S$ and let the query have radius $T$ around a common center. Put $\Delta=|S-T|$. If a data-query pair has Euclidean distance at most $r$, its squared angular chord distance is at most
   \[
      d_1^2=\frac{r^2-\Delta^2}{ST}.
   \]
   If its Euclidean distance is greater than $cr$, its squared angular chord distance is greater than
   \[
      d_2^2=\frac{c^2r^2-\Delta^2}{ST}.
   \]
   Whenever the near case is feasible,
   \[
      \frac{d_2^2}{d_1^2}\ge c^2.
   \]
   The same statements hold with $1+O_c(\eta)$ multiplicative slack for shells of relative width $O_c(\eta)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   For normalized directions $u=(p-o)/S$ and $v=(q-o)/T$,
   \[
      \|p-q\|_2^2=(S-T)^2+ST\|u-v\|_2^2.
   \]
   Solving for $\|u-v\|_2^2$ gives the first two formulas. Moreover,
   \[
      \frac{c^2r^2-\Delta^2}{r^2-\Delta^2}
      \ge c^2
   \]
   because $(c^2-1)\Delta^2\ge0$. Shell discretization perturbs $S$, $T$, and $\Delta$ by relative $O_c(\eta)$, which yields the final claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Posterior stopping}

Fix a geometric node containing $m$ points in a certified ball $\Ball(o,R)$. Let $\mu(u)$ be the probability that a uniformly random data point in the node reaches score prefix $u$, and let $\nu_q(u)$ be the corresponding probability for query $q$. For a deterministic child cluster $C$ of relative size $\alpha=|C|/m$, let $\mu_C(u)$ be the score-prefix law conditioned on the data point belonging to $C$. The posterior mass of $C$ at prefix $u$ is
\[
   Z_C(u)=\frac{\alpha\mu_C(u)}{\mu(u)}.
\]
Let $\mathcal A_C$ be the first-hit frontier on which $Z_C(u)\ge\tau$.

Put
\[
   \rho=\rho_c,
   \qquad
   s=1-\rho.
\]
The Bellman query mass assigned to prefix $u$ is
\[
   w_q(u)=\mu(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Posterior first-hit inequality]\label{lem:first-hit}
   For every prefix-free first-hit frontier $\mathcal A_C$,
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C} w_q(u)
      \le
      \left(\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\right)^s.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   On $\mathcal A_C$, the inequality $Z_C(u)\ge\tau$ gives
   \[
      \mu(u)\le \frac{\alpha}{\tau}\mu_C(u).
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho
      \le
      \left(\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\right)^s
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu_C(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho.
   \]
   Since $s+\rho=1$ and $\mathcal A_C$ is prefix-free, Hölder's inequality gives
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu_C(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho
      \le
      \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu_C(u)\right)^s
      \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\nu_q(u)\right)^\rho
      \le1.
   \]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Radius Bellman cancellation}

Use the recursive query potential
\[
   F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}.
\]
Suppose child $C$ has size $\alpha m$ and certified radius $R_C=\lambda R$. Activate it at posterior threshold
\[
   \tau=\lambda^2.
\]
Then the fractional cost of entering and recursively searching $C$ is exactly
\[
   \left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda^2}\right)^s
   F(\alpha m,\lambda R)
   =
   \alpha F(m,R).
\]
Thus disjoint activated children consume at most the parent potential, because $\sum_C\alpha_C\le1$. No cap geometry, balance condition, or bound on the number of children appears in this cancellation. Geometry enters only through the certified radii $R_C$. \Cref{fig:bellman} shows the cancellation schematically.

The same idea controls space:
\[
   \sum_C |C|^{1+\rho}
   \le
   m^{1+\rho}
\]
for disjoint children. Polynomially many shells and bounded-diameter roots contribute only $\log^{O_c(1)}n$ factors when shell widths and distortions are inverse-polynomial in $\log n$.

At a state $u=(P_u,o_u,R_u)$, preprocessing proposes a disjoint partition of $P_u$ into certified children $C$ with centers $o_C$ and radii
\[
   R_C=\max_{p\in C}\|p-o_C\|_2.
\]
The partition may come from $k$-center, $k$-means, cap proposals, or any other routine; correctness uses only the certified radius. A child is geometrically useful when $R_C=\lambda_C R_u$ with $\lambda_C<1$ bounded away from one, and small or shallow residual sets are stored for resident scan.

Queries keep two populations on the same cumulative score tree.

\emph{Ordinary paths} use the continuation-mass decoder from \cref{lem:continuation-work}. They find isolated near points and residual leaves.

\emph{Detector cohorts} are used only to activate geometric children. A detector path votes for at most one child: the first child whose posterior reaches the radius threshold $\tau_C=\lambda_C^2$. Child $C$ is activated only after it receives
\[
   T_C=\log^{A_c}n\,|C|^\rho\left(\frac{R_C}{r}\right)^{2s}
\]
votes. The parent starts with
\[
   T_u=\log^{A_c}n\,|P_u|^\rho\left(\frac{R_u}{r}\right)^{2s}
\]
detector budget. Because each detector path casts at most one vote, activated children consume the existing parent budget rather than multiplying it.

When a query enters an activated child, the cumulative score prefix is retained. Recentered coordinates only change future score offsets; no new independent spherical decoder is started.

\begin{assumption}[Route cohort-or-leaf]\label{asm:cohort-or-leaf}
   Fix a true near pair $(p,q)$ and let
   \[
      u_0,u_1,\ldots,u_h
   \]
   be the deterministic hierarchy lineage containing $p$, stopping when the resident block containing $p$ is scanned. Write $C_i=P_{u_{i+1}}\subseteq P_{u_i}$ and
   \[
      \tau_i=\left(\frac{R_{u_{i+1}}}{R_{u_i}}\right)^2.
   \]
   In one shared cumulative score tree, the following route event has probability at least $\log^{-O_c(1)}n$:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item an ordinary path exposes $p$ in a searchable residual leaf before the lineage needs another activation; or
      \item for every $i<h$, detector paths generate enough first-hit votes to activate $C_i$, and the query reaches the final resident scan containing $p$.
   \end{enumerate}
   A useful local inequality to prove en route is, in the notation of the hand-off,
   \[
      \tau_C^{-\rho}\mathcal A_C^{\mathrm{hit}}
      +
      \mathcal L_C^{\mathrm{free}}
      \ge
      \log^{-O_c(1)}n.
   \]
   The assumption used here is stronger than this inequality level by level: the local quantities must telescope, or be coupled by a second-moment argument, into the displayed route event. Independent inverse-polylogarithmic success probabilities at the $h$ levels would not suffice.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Polylogarithmic score-and-ball process]\label{thm:polylog-process}
   Under \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf}, the cumulative score-and-ball process has expected query time
   \[
      d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   and expected space
   \[
      dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
   The radius-budgeted continuation block in \cref{cor:radius-budgeted-block} gives the parent potential
   \[
      F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2(1-\rho)}
   \]
   directly as score-tree work, with only polylogarithmic overhead and inverse-polylogarithmic near survival. The shell lemma preserves the critical Euclidean angular gap up to inverse-polylogarithmic slack, which is absorbed into the displayed logarithmic factors.

   For every certified child, \cref{lem:first-hit} bounds the fractional query mass that can activate the child, and the choice $\tau_C=\lambda_C^2$ converts that activation cost into $\alpha_C F(m,R)$ by the Bellman cancellation. Summing over disjoint children is therefore bounded by the parent potential. The detector-vote rule is the integer version of this accounting: an activated child has already received its own recursive budget from parent detector votes. Space is controlled by disjointness and convexity of $x^{1+\rho}$.

   Along the near-neighbor route, \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf} supplies an inverse-polylogarithmic probability for the entire lineage, not one independent loss per scale: either an ordinary path finds the near point before the next activation is needed, or detector cohorts activate every certified child containing it until a resident scan is reached. Polylogarithmic amplification gives constant success probability, and truncation/rebuilding converts expected bounds to capped bounds as in the preliminaries.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the main theorem}

The conditional main theorem is exactly \cref{thm:polylog-process} with $\rho=\rho_c$. The only remaining assumption named in \cref{thm:main} is \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf}. Expected bounds are converted to capped bounds by the truncation paragraph in the preliminaries.

\section{The algorithm as one tree}

For clarity, we summarize the actual data structure as a single tree with two edge types.

\subsection{Preprocessing}

\begin{enumerate}
   \item Apply the usual bounded-diameter localization and build the associated polylogarithmic family of localized instances.
   \item Create a root state $(P,o,R)$ for each localized instance.
   \item At every nonterminal state, divide the resident points into radial shells of inverse-polylogarithmic relative width.
   \item Build a deterministic certified child partition for each shell. Certify every proposed child by explicitly computing
      \[
         R_C=\max_{p\in C}\|p-o_C\|_2.
      \]
   \item Store the activation threshold $\tau_C=(R_C/R_u)^2$ and vote budget
      \[
         T_C=\log^{A_c}n\,|C|^\rho\left(\frac{R_C}{r}\right)^{2(1-\rho)}.
      \]
   \item Build the cumulative Gaussian score tree with continuation-mass pruning. The same score prefixes support ordinary paths and detector cohorts.
   \item Stop once the node radius or node cardinality is small enough for a resident scan.
\end{enumerate}

Correctness does not depend on how a proposed child is found. A dense-cap routine, $k$-center, $k$-means, or a score-prefix detector may propose the subset and center; the proof uses only disjointness, posterior activation, and the certified radius.

\subsection{Query}

A query traverses the cumulative score tree. Ordinary paths use continuation-mass pruning and scan candidate buckets or resident blocks. Detector cohorts compute child posteriors along the same prefixes. A detector path votes for the first child whose posterior reaches $\tau_C$; the child is activated only when its vote budget is full. When a child activates, the query recenters the coordinates but retains the cumulative score prefix with the appropriate stored offset.

At all scan points, candidates are verified in the original metric. Therefore approximate clustering can affect performance but not correctness.

\subsection{Simplicity and implementation}

The filtering work remains level-synchronous. At one checkpoint, all active score paths can be advanced by a batched matrix multiplication, followed by continuation-mass pruning, posterior histogramming, detector-vote compaction, child activation, and resident scans. Recentering updates stored coordinate offsets; it does not start a fresh score tree.

The data structure stores only the tree metadata:
\[
   (\text{score seed},\text{cluster IDs},o_C,R_C,\tau_C,T_C,\text{resident block}).
\]
The same kernels support ordinary paths and detector cohorts; only their transition probabilities and counters differ.

\section{Why the compounded overhead disappears}

The restart analysis pays for the same loss once per geometric level. The cumulative process avoids that multiplication in two ways.

\paragraph{The score process is global.}
Continuation-mass pruning controls the total number of explored prefixes in one cumulative tree. The budgeted form in \cref{cor:radius-budgeted-block} lets a node spend the full radius-weighted potential $F(m,R)$ inside the same score block by using the effective budget $N=m(R/r)^{2(1-\rho)/\rho}$. Recentered children inherit the score prefix and only change the coordinate offset for future increments. Therefore there is no per-child independent spherical failure penalty in the target process.

\paragraph{Geometry is paid by Bellman cancellation.}
The child activation threshold $\tau_C=\lambda_C^2$ makes the radius decrease cancel the posterior stopping cost. The recursive potential of all activated children is at most the parent potential:
\[
   \sum_C
   \left(\frac{\alpha_C}{\lambda_C^2}\right)^s
   F(\alpha_Cm,\lambda_CR)
   \le
   F(m,R).
\]
This is the mechanism that removes dependence on the number of recenterings.

\paragraph{Integer materialization is isolated.}
The remaining issue is not an exponent calculation; it is the cohort-or-leaf probability. Ordinary paths must either find the near point before its geometric child becomes posterior-heavy, or detector cohorts must activate that child using the same path budget. This is the only unresolved place where the algorithm can still lose more than a polylogarithm.

\section{An exponent-improvement ladder}

The proof program need not jump directly from the restart construction to the polylogarithmic target. A useful intermediate calculus is the following. Suppose the logarithm of the nonpolynomial overhead has the form
\[
   O_c\!\left(\eta^p L+\eta^{-q}L^\alpha\right),
   \qquad L=\log n,
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
   \item $L^\alpha$ is the loss of one score block;
   \item $\eta^{-q}$ is the number of independently paid geometric phases;
   \item $\eta^p L$ is the exponent cost of geometric slack.
\end{itemize}
Balancing the two terms gives
\[
   \eta=L^{-(1-\alpha)/(p+q)}
\]
and overhead exponent
\[
   \theta(p,q,\alpha)
   =
   \frac{q+p\alpha}{p+q},
   \qquad
   \exp(O_c(L^\theta)).
\]

This ladder is useful for stress testing. The continuation-mass spherical theorem already moves the restart architecture from exponent $7/9$ to roughly $2/3$. Any partial geometric result that either reduces the number of independently paid phases or improves the slack perturbation gives a next rung. The Bellman cohort mechanism is the endpoint: if cohort-or-leaf holds, recursive geometry is paid by the parent potential and the $\eta^{-q}$ term disappears rather than being optimized.

\section{A restartable slice route to the 2/3 rung}

There is a more concrete way to reach the $2/3$ milestone without proving the persistent route cohort-or-leaf lemma. The point is to restart deliberately after every recentering, but to recurse only on leaf-conditioned slices. This avoids the obstruction in which one atypical score prefix from a large child would have to fund a recursive search of the whole child.

Fix one spherical stage on a shell in a ball $\Ball(o,R)$. Let $\eta>0$ and set
\[
   \tau=\log^{-A_c} n.
\]
Run the continuation-mass spherical decoder and regard its accepting buckets as hard leaves for this stage only. At each leaf bucket $U$, repeatedly search for a heavy cap of margin $2\eta$, meaning a direction $w$ such that
\[
   \left|\left\{x\in U:\ip{(x-o)/R}{w}\ge 2\eta\right\}\right|
   \ge \tau |U|.
\]
When such a cap is found, store an $\eta$-margin slice
\[
   D[U,v]=\left\{x\in U:\ip{(x-o)/R}{v}\ge \eta\right\}
\]
as a certified child. Since every point in it has inner product at least $\eta R$ with direction $v$,
\[
   D[U,v]\subseteq \Ball(o+\eta Rv,\,R\sqrt{1-\eta^2}),
\]
and the implementation certifies the actual child radius by computing the maximum distance to the proposed center. After no heavy $2\eta$-cap remains, the residual bucket is scanned. Every certified child starts a fresh spherical stage in its own coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Residual scan after slice peeling]\label{lem:slice-residual}
   In one restartable slice stage, scanning the final cap-free residual leaf buckets costs only a $(1+O(\tau))$ factor more than the ordinary far-candidate count of the spherical decoder with far correlation $2\eta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Fix a query $q$ reaching a final residual bucket $U$, and let
   \[
      E_q=\left\{x\in U:\ip{(x-o)/R}{(q-o)/R}>2\eta\right\}.
   \]
   If $|E_q|\ge\tau |U|$, then the query direction itself defines a heavy $2\eta$-cap that should have been peeled. Hence $|E_q|<\tau |U|$. Therefore
   \[
      |U|\le \frac{1}{1-\tau}\,|U\setminus E_q|.
   \]
   Every point in $U\setminus E_q$ has correlation at most $2\eta$ with the query direction, so the spherical far-candidate bound controls its expected contribution. Since $(1-\tau)^{-1}=1+O(\tau)$, scanning all of $U$ has the same bound up to this factor.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tuple cap finder]\label{lem:tuple-cap-finder}
   Let $U$ be a finite set of unit vectors, let $0<\eta<1/10$, and suppose some $2\eta$-cap
   \[
      C=\{x\in U:\ip{x}{w}\ge2\eta\}
   \]
   has size at least $\tau |U|$. There is a randomized proposal procedure which, using
   \[
      \tau^{-O(\eta^{-2})}\log(1/\delta)
   \]
   tuple proposals, finds with probability at least $1-\delta$ a direction $v$ whose $\eta$-cap contains at least $\Omega(\eta\tau)|U|$ points.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Let $X$ be uniform on $C$ and let $m_C=\E X$. Since every point of $C$ has projection at least $2\eta$ on $w$,
   \[
      \|m_C\|\ge \ip{m_C}{w}\ge2\eta.
   \]
   Sample
   \[
      k=\lceil C_0\eta^{-2}\rceil
   \]
   points from $U$ with replacement. With probability at least $\tau^k$, all sampled points lie in $C$. Conditioned on this event, their empirical mean $\widehat m$ satisfies
   \[
      \E\|\widehat m-m_C\|^2\le \frac1k.
   \]
   Taking $C_0$ large and applying Markov's inequality gives
   \[
      \|\widehat m-m_C\|\le\eta/4
   \]
   with probability at least a fixed positive constant. Let $v=\widehat m/\|\widehat m\|$. On this event,
   \[
      \ip{m_C}{v}
      \ge
      \|\widehat m\|-\|\widehat m-m_C\|
      \ge
      \|m_C\|-2\|\widehat m-m_C\|
      \ge
      3\eta/2.
   \]
   Therefore, for $X$ uniform on $C$,
   \[
      \E\ip{X}{v}\ge3\eta/2.
   \]
   Since $\ip{X}{v}\le1$, at least an $\Omega(\eta)$ fraction of $C$ must satisfy $\ip{X}{v}\ge\eta$. Hence the $\eta$-cap in direction $v$ contains $\Omega(\eta\tau)|U|$ points.

   A single random tuple is therefore successful with probability at least $c\tau^k$ for a constant $c>0$. Repeating $O(\tau^{-k}\log(1/\delta))$ times and verifying each proposed $\eta$-cap by scanning $U$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Leaf-conditioned slice budget]\label{lem:slice-budget}
   Let a hard leaf $U$ have data fraction $\mu=|U|/m$, and let $\nu_q(U)\in\{0,1\}$ indicate whether query $q$ reaches it. Suppose the peeled slices inside $U$ have relative sizes
   \[
      z_j=\frac{|C_j|}{|U|}
   \]
   and certified radii $R_j=\lambda_jR$. With $\rho=\rho_c+O_c(\eta)$ and $s=1-\rho$,
   \[
      F(m,R)\,\mu^\rho\nu_q(U)^s\,z_j^\rho\lambda_j^{2s}
      =
      F(|C_j|,R_j)\nu_q(U)^s.
   \]
   If at most $J$ slices are peeled from $U$, then summing over the slices loses at most a factor $J^{1-\rho}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The identity follows by substituting $|C_j|=z_j\mu m$ and $R_j=\lambda_jR$ into
   \[
      F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}.
   \]
   The slices are disjoint inside $U$, so $\sum_j z_j\le1$. Concavity of $x^\rho$ gives
   \[
      \sum_{j=1}^J z_j^\rho\le J^{1-\rho}\left(\sum_{j=1}^J z_j\right)^\rho\le J^{1-\rho}.
   \]
\end{proof}

By \cref{lem:tuple-cap-finder}, every successful peeled slice removes an $\Omega(\eta\tau)$ fraction of the current bucket. A bucket of size at most $n$ therefore produces only
\[
   J=O((\eta\tau)^{-1}\log n)=\log^{O_c(1)}n
\]
sequential slices. Thus \cref{lem:slice-budget} costs only a polylogarithmic factor per restartable stage.
The rejected tuple proposals from \cref{lem:tuple-cap-finder} are preprocessing-only and are not stored. If preprocessing time is tracked, their logarithmic overhead per bucket is
\[
   O(\eta^{-2}\log(1/\tau)+\log\log n)
   =
   O_c(\eta^{-2}\log\log n),
\]
which is below the final $2/3$ overhead after the choice of $\eta$ in \cref{prop:slice-two-thirds}.

\begin{proposition}[Restartable slice milestone]\label{prop:slice-two-thirds}
   For fixed $c>1$, the restartable slice construction gives a worst-case Euclidean ANN structure with
   \[
      Q(n,d)
      =
      d\,n^{\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(
         O_c\!\left((\log n)^{2/3}(\log\log n)^{2/3}\right)
      \right)
   \]
   and
   \[
      S(n,d)
      =
      dn+n^{1+\rho_c}
      \exp\!\left(
         O_c\!\left((\log n)^{2/3}(\log\log n)^{2/3}\right)
      \right).
   \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
   First consider one restartable stage. The continuation-mass decoder supplies the hard accepting leaves and has expected path work, far-candidate occurrences, storage, and inverse near-pair success all bounded by the ideal spherical powers times $\log^{O_c(1)}n$, by \cref{lem:continuation-work} and \cref{cor:spherical-survival}. Amplifying the stage to failure probability $1/\log^{O_c(1)}n$ costs another polylogarithmic factor.

   The final residual scan is controlled by \cref{lem:slice-residual}. Heavy cap discovery is handled by \cref{lem:tuple-cap-finder}; rejected tuple proposals affect preprocessing only, and accepted slices are verified before storage. By \cref{lem:slice-budget}, the recursive budgets of all slices peeled from a leaf cost only a further polylogarithmic factor. A query follows every certified child ball from its reached leaf that is compatible with the query, which adds at most the same polylogarithmic factor because each leaf has only $J=\log^{O_c(1)}n$ accepted slices. Thus one stage has polylogarithmic overhead in query work, space, and inverse success probability relative to the ideal radius-weighted power-law budget.

   Put
   \[
      L=\log n,
      \qquad
      \ell=\log\log n.
   \]
   By \cref{lem:rho-stability} and \cref{lem:shell}, using far correlation $2\eta$ and inverse-polynomial shell widths changes the balanced exponent from $\rho_c$ to
   \[
      \rho_c+O_c(\eta),
   \]
   contributing
   \[
      \exp(O_c(\eta L))
   \]
   to the overhead.

   Each accepted slice has certified radius at most $R\sqrt{1-\eta^2}\le R(1-\eta^2/2)$ for small enough $\eta$. After bounded-diameter localization, the relevant ratio $R_0/r$ is at most $\log^{O_c(1)}n$. Hence a near-neighbor lineage has at most
   \[
      H=O_c\!\left(\eta^{-2}\log\frac{R_0}{r}\right)
      =
      O_c(\eta^{-2}\ell)
   \]
   restarts before a resident scan. Paying an independent polylogarithmic stage overhead at every restart contributes
   \[
      \log^{O_c(H)}n
      =
      \exp(O_c(\eta^{-2}\ell^2)).
   \]
   Therefore the logarithm of the nonpolynomial overhead is
   \[
      O_c(\eta L+\eta^{-2}\ell^2).
   \]
   Balance the two terms by taking
   \[
      \eta=\Theta_c\!\left(\left(\frac{\ell^2}{L}\right)^{1/3}\right).
   \]
   Then both terms are $O_c(L^{2/3}\ell^{2/3})$, proving the displayed bounds. \Cref{fig:balance} shows the balance.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof obligations}

The draft now separates two proof tracks.

\paragraph{Spherical stage.}
The continuation-mass spherical decoder is proved in \cref{lem:continuation-work}, \cref{lem:good-first-moment}, \cref{lem:tame-second-moment}, and \cref{cor:spherical-survival}. The one-sided analytical upper barrier controls prolific early prefixes in the proof, while the algorithm itself enforces only continuation-mass pruning.

\paragraph{Restartable \(2/3\) stage.}
\Cref{prop:slice-two-thirds} proves the restartable $2/3$ rung at the current level of detail. The remaining audits for a polished version are to write the high-probability truncation constants for every stage explicitly and to align the bounded-diameter and ball-to-shell localization with the exact constants in the slice construction.

\paragraph{Cohort-or-leaf.}
\Cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf} is the route-level condition needed for the polylogarithmic endpoint. It should be tested against the hard cases: an isolated near point next to a massive far cluster, a near point on a cluster boundary, a tiny child that becomes heavy only late, and symmetric far shells. The proof must show a whole-lineage inverse-polylogarithmic event, not merely an inverse-polylogarithmic event at each level.

\section{Conclusion}

The algorithmic target is a cumulative score-and-ball tree with query time
\[
   d\,n^{1/(2c^2-1)}\log^{O_c(1)}n
\]
and space
\[
   dn+n^{1+1/(2c^2-1)}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
\]
The exact pieces are already simple: continuation-mass pruning gives optimal expected spherical work, posterior first-hit gives the stopping inequality, and $\tau=\lambda^2$ gives exact radius Bellman cancellation. The central remaining task is the probabilistic cohort-or-leaf lemma that couples ordinary paths to detector cohorts without paying the exponent twice.

\section{More detail on the spherical exponent calculation}

For completeness, we verify the algebra leading to $\rho(a,b)$. Let $B$ be the branching factor and $\ell_B=\log B$. With
\[
   t^2=(1+a)\ell_B
\]
and
\[
   k=
   \frac{2(1+b)\log m}
   {(1+a)(1-b)\ell_B}
   +O(1),
\]
the expected number of accepting leaves for one point has logarithm
\begin{align*}
   k\ell_B-\frac{kt^2}{2}+O(\log k)
                                                                                                                  &=
                                                                                                                  \frac{k\ell_B(1-a)}{2}+O(\log k)\\
                                                                                                                                                                                                 &=
                                                                                                                                                                                                 \frac{(1-a)(1+b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}\log m+O(\log\log m).
\end{align*}
Thus it equals
\[
   m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O(1)}m.
\]

For a pair of correlation at most $b$, the expected number of common accepting leaves has logarithm at most
\begin{align*}
   k\ell_B-\frac{kt^2}{1+b}
                                                                                                                  &=
                                                                                                                  k\ell_B\left(1-\frac{1+a}{1+b}\right)\\
                                                                                                                                                                                                 &=-\frac{k\ell_B(a-b)}{1+b}\\
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           &=-\frac{2(a-b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}\log m+O(1).
\end{align*}
A direct calculation shows
\[
   1-\rho(a,b)
   =
   \frac{2(a-b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}.
\]
Hence one ordinary far point contributes at most
\[
   m^{-(1-\rho(a,b))}\log^{O(1)}m
\]
common leaves, and all $m$ ordinary far points contribute at most
\[
   m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O(1)}m.
\]

\section{Bellman cancellation algebra}

We record the cancellation with no surrounding notation. Let $s=1-\rho$, let a parent contain $m$ points in radius $R$, and let a child have relative size $\alpha$ and relative radius $\lambda$. With
\[
   F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}
\]
and activation threshold $\tau=\lambda^2$, the first-hit bound charges at most $(\alpha/\lambda^2)^s$ query mass to the child. The recursive charge is therefore
\begin{align*}
   \left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda^2}\right)^s
   F(\alpha m,\lambda R)
   &=
   \alpha^s\lambda^{-2s}
   \alpha^\rho m^\rho
   \lambda^{2s}\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}\\
   &=
   \alpha F(m,R).
\end{align*}
Thus the radius improvement pays exactly for the posterior threshold, and disjoint children sum to at most the parent potential.
