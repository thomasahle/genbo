\title{A Cumulative-Score Route to Polylogarithmic Loss\\
for Optimal Data-Dependent Euclidean ANN}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{Draft, June 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
   For fixed $c>1$, the optimal balanced exponent for data-dependent approximate nearest neighbor search in Euclidean space is
   \[
      \rho_c=\frac{1}{2c^2-1}.
   \]
   Known worst-case constructions achieve $n^{\rho_c+o(1)}$ query time and $n^{1+\rho_c+o(1)}$ space. The goal here is to remove the subpolynomial loss, not merely optimize it. We describe a single cumulative score-and-ball process intended to achieve
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n,
      \qquad
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
   The process has three parts. First, local spherical filtering uses a cumulative Gaussian score tree with continuation-mass pruning rather than levelwise hard thresholds. Second, geometric children are activated by an exact posterior stopping rule: a child of radius $\lambda R$ is entered when its posterior reaches $\tau=\lambda^2$. The resulting Bellman cancellation pays for recursion with no dependence on the number or balance of children. Third, tempered detector cohorts materialize this fractional activation while ordinary paths continue to search residual leaves. We prove the exact work and Bellman identities, prove the spherical good-leaf first moment, and isolate the remaining probabilistic tasks as a spherical second-moment lemma and a cohort-or-leaf lemma. Under those lemmas, the structure has the polylogarithmic bounds above.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The $(c,r)$-approximate nearest neighbor problem asks us to preprocess a set $P\subseteq\R^d$ of $n$ points so that, given a query $q\in\R^d$ satisfying
\[
   \min_{p\in P}\|p-q\|_2\le r,
\]
the data structure returns some $p'\in P$ with
\[
   \|p'-q\|_2\le cr.
\]
Throughout the paper, $c>1$ is fixed. The data structure is randomized and data-dependent, and succeeds with a fixed constant probability. Candidate points are verified using their true Euclidean distances, so the structure never reports an incorrect answer.

For Euclidean space, the optimal balanced data-dependent exponent is
\[
   \rho_c=\frac{1}{2c^2-1}.
\]
The landmark construction of Andoni and Razenshteyn obtains query time $d\,n^{\rho_c+o(1)}$ and space $dn+n^{1+\rho_c+o(1)}$ for worst-case data sets~\cite{AR15}. Its geometric component removes dense spherical caps, recenters them as smaller certified balls, and applies a spherical filtering primitive to the residual data. The $o(1)$ term absorbs several losses: shell discretization, geometric slack, spherical list decoding, and the repeated restarting of the filtering process after recentering.

The purpose of this draft is to stop optimizing that restart loss and instead remove it. The central design choice is to keep one cumulative score process across geometric recentering. Recentered children are not fresh spherical instances; they are cohorts inside the same score tree. This forces two changes:
\begin{itemize}
   \item the spherical decoder must survive with only polylogarithmic loss under continuation-mass pruning;
   \item geometric recursion must be paid by posterior activation rather than by restarting a full score tree in every child.
\end{itemize}
The first point is a one-sided branching-random-walk problem. The second point has an exact algebraic cancellation, proved below. The only remaining coupling issue is to turn the fractional posterior activation into integer cohorts without losing the exponent.

\subsection{Main result}

All logarithms are natural.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional main theorem]\label{thm:main}
   Fix $c>1$. Assume the one-sided spherical second-moment statement of \cref{asm:spherical-second-moment} and the cohort-or-leaf statement of \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf}. Then there is a randomized data-dependent data structure for worst-case $(c,r)$-approximate nearest neighbor search in $\R^d$ with constant success probability, query time
   \[
      Q(n,d)=d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n,
   \]
   and space
   \[
      S(n,d)=dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n,
   \]
   where $\rho_c=1/(2c^2-1)$. The bounds hold in expectation over preprocessing; standard truncation and rebuilding convert them to capped bounds with only constant-factor changes in success probability and asymptotic cost.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{What changes}

The algorithm in this draft differs from a restart construction in the following ways:
\begin{itemize}
   \item score prefixes are kept or discarded by continuation mass, not by a fresh hard threshold at every level;
   \item recentering changes the geometric coordinates but preserves the cumulative score state through an explicit offset;
   \item a child of relative radius $\lambda$ is activated at posterior threshold $\lambda^2$;
   \item ordinary paths and detector cohorts are separate populations, so cluster activation does not consume ordinary near-pair search paths;
   \item every geometric child is certified by its actual maximum radius.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where the proof stands}

The expected-work calculation for continuation-mass pruning is exact. The posterior stopping inequality and radius Bellman cancellation are exact. The remaining work is probabilistic: prove that either the true near point survives ordinary continuation-mass pruning to a searchable leaf, or its deterministic geometric child receives enough detector votes to be activated. We state this as \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf} because it is the only place where ordinary paths, detector cohorts, and posterior martingales must be coupled.

\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Correlation parameters}

For unit vectors $x,q\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$,
\[
   \|x-q\|_2^2=2-2\ip{x}{q}.
\]
A spherical filtering instance is specified by two correlations
\[
   -1<b<a<1.
\]
A near pair satisfies $\ip{x}{q}\ge a$, while an ordinary far pair satisfies $\ip{x}{q}\le b$. The balanced Gaussian filtering exponent is
\[
   \rho(a,b)
   =
   \frac{(1-a)(1+b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}.
\]
At the critical Euclidean normalization,
\[
   a_0=1-\frac{1}{c^2},
   \qquad
   b_0=0,
\]
and therefore
\[
   \rho(a_0,b_0)=\frac{1}{2c^2-1}=\rho_c.
\]

We repeatedly use the smoothness of $\rho$ near $(a_0,b_0)$.

\begin{lemma}[Stability of the balanced exponent]\label{lem:rho-stability}
   For every fixed $c>1$, there are constants $C_c>0$ and $\eta_c>0$ such that, whenever $0<\eta\le\eta_c$ and
   \[
      a\ge a_0-C_c\eta,
      \qquad
      b\le C_c\eta,
   \]
   we have
   \[
      \rho(a,b)\le \rho_c+C_c\eta.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   The denominator $(1+a)(1-b)$ is bounded away from zero in a fixed neighborhood of $(a_0,0)$. The rational function $\rho(a,b)$ is continuously differentiable there. The claim follows from the mean-value theorem, after enlarging $C_c$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Polylogarithmic slack}

All geometric discretization parameters are chosen as inverse powers of $\log n$ with constants depending only on $c$. We write $\log^{O_c(1)}n$ for any resulting polynomial in these logarithmic factors. The point of the cumulative-score construction is that every nonconstant loss should be absorbed into this notation; no factor of the form $\exp((\log n)^\beta)$ is allowed in the target theorem.

\subsection{Expected cost and truncation}

The filtering constructions naturally give expected numbers of explored nodes and candidate occurrences. We use the following standard conversion. If one copy succeeds with probability at least $p_0>0$ and has expected query work at most $T$, stop it after $K T$ work for a sufficiently large constant $K=K(p_0)$. Markov's inequality shows that the truncation probability is at most $1/K$, so the truncated copy still succeeds with constant probability. A constant number of independent copies then restores any desired fixed success probability below one. The same idea applies to space: rebuild whenever the realized space exceeds a fixed multiple of its expectation. We therefore state expected bounds in intermediate lemmas and capped bounds in the final theorem.

\section{The cumulative-score spherical primitive}

The spherical stage uses one cumulative Gaussian score tree and prunes by continuation mass. This avoids the two-sided tube that causes the $\exp(O(k^{1/3}))$ loss in the earlier analysis.

Choose a constant branching factor $B\ge2$, write $\ell_B=\log B$, and let
\[
   t^2=(1+a)\ell_B.
\]
A path of depth $k$ carries independent Gaussian edge vectors $g_e\sim N(0,I_d)$ and cumulative score
\[
   S_v(x)=\sum_{e\preceq v}\ip{g_e}{x}.
\]
The depth is
\[
   k
   =
   \left\lceil
      \frac{2(1+b)\log m}
      {(1+a)(1-b)\ell_B}
   \right\rceil
   =
   \Theta_{a,b}(\log m),
\]
and a leaf accepts $x$ when $S_v(x)\ge kt$.

For a node $v$ at level $\ell$ with score $s$, put $r=k-\ell$ and define the continuation mass
\[
   H_\ell(s)
   =
   B^r
   \overline\Phi\!\left(\frac{kt-s}{\sqrt r}\right).
\]
This is exactly the expected number of accepting descendant leaves of $v$, conditional on the current score $s$. The decoder retains a prefix only while
\[
   H_\ell(S_v(x))\ge k^{-A}
\]
for a sufficiently large constant $A=A_{a,b}$. Equivalently, up to lower-order tail terms, a retained prefix satisfies
\[
   S_v(x)\ge
   kt-\sqrt{2r(r\log B+A\log k)}.
\]
Early scores may lie well below the final linear trajectory; the barrier tightens only near the leaf.

\begin{lemma}[Exact expected work]\label{lem:continuation-work}
   For fixed $-1<b<a<1$, continuation-mass pruning gives expected query path work
   \[
      m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m,
   \]
   expected ordinary-far candidate occurrences
   \[
      m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m,
   \]
   and stored point-path occurrences
   \[
      m^{1+\rho(a,b)}\log^{O_{a,b}(1)}m.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   At any fixed level, summing $H_\ell(S_v(x))$ over all prefixes $v$ counts the expected number of accepting descendant leaves for $x$. Since every retained prefix has continuation mass at least $k^{-A}$, the expected number of retained prefixes at that level is at most $k^A$ times the expected number of accepting leaves. Summing over $k$ levels gives only a polynomial factor in $k$.

   The expected number of accepting leaves for one point is
   \[
      B^k\overline\Phi(t\sqrt{k})
      =
      m^{\rho(a,b)}k^{O(1)}.
   \]
   For a pair of correlation at most $b$, the Gaussian joint-tail estimate
   \[
      \Prb[X\ge u,\,Y\ge u]
      \le
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{1+b}\right)
   \]
   gives expected common accepting leaves at most
   \[
      m^{-(1-\rho(a,b))}k^{O(1)}.
   \]
   Summing over $m$ ordinary far points proves the far-candidate bound. Summing the one-point bound over stored data points proves the storage bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Good near leaf]\label{def:good-near-leaf}
   Fix a near pair $(p,q)$ with $\ip{p}{q}=a$. A leaf $v$ is good for $(p,q)$ if
   \[
      S_v(p),S_v(q)\in[kt,kt+1]
   \]
   and every prefix $u\preceq v$ satisfies the continuation-mass pruning rule for both $p$ and $q$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Good-leaf first moment]\label{lem:good-first-moment}
   For every fixed $-1<b<a<1$ and sufficiently large pruning constant $A$, if $Z$ is the number of good near leaves for a pair of correlation exactly $a$, then
   \[
      \E Z\ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   Work at the critical correlation $a$, which is the least favorable near case. Exponentially tilt the two-dimensional Gaussian increment $(X,Y)$ so that both coordinates have mean $t$. Since
   \[
      \Sigma=
      \begin{pmatrix}
         1&a\\
         a&1
      \end{pmatrix},
      \qquad
      \theta=\frac{t}{1+a}(1,1),
   \]
   the tilted law has mean $\Sigma\theta=(t,t)$ and
   \[
      \frac12\theta^\top\Sigma\theta=\frac{t^2}{1+a}=\ell_B.
   \]
   Let $Q$ denote the tilted measure. On the endpoint event
   \[
      S_v(p),S_v(q)\in[kt,kt+1],
   \]
   the likelihood ratio satisfies
   \[
      B^k\frac{dP}{dQ}=\exp(O_{a,b}(1)).
   \]
   Therefore the expected number of good leaves under the original measure is, up to constant factors, the $Q$-probability that one tilted two-dimensional walk has endpoint in the unit square and stays above the two continuation barriers.

   Under $Q$, after subtracting the linear drift $\ell t$, each coordinate is a centered Gaussian walk. The endpoint has covariance $k\Sigma$, so the probability that both centered endpoints lie in $[0,1]$ is $\Theta_{a,b}(1/k)$.

   It remains to check that the pruning barriers do not cost more than a polynomial factor. Write $r=k-\ell$ and let
   \[
      d_r=\sqrt{2r(r\ell_B+A\log k)}-rt.
   \]
   A prefix at level $\ell$ is retained whenever the centered coordinate is at least $-d_r$, up to harmless constant tail corrections. Since $t^2=(1+a)\ell_B<2\ell_B$, there is a constant $c_{a,b}>0$ such that
   \[
      d_r\ge c_{a,b}r
      \qquad\text{for } r\ge C_{a,b}\log k,
   \]
   and, for $1\le r\le C_{a,b}\log k$,
   \[
      d_r\ge c_{a,b}\sqrt{A r\log k}
   \]
   after increasing $A$. Conditioned on an endpoint in $[0,1]$, each coordinate is a Gaussian bridge whose variance at distance $r$ from the endpoint is $O(r)$. A union bound over $r$ therefore gives failure probability
   \[
      \sum_{r\ge C\log k}\exp(-\Omega_{a,b}(r))
      +
      \sum_{1\le r\le C\log k} k^{-\Omega_{a,b}(A)}
      =
      o(1)
   \]
   for $A$ large enough. Applying this to both coordinates proves that the bridge satisfies both continuation barriers with probability at least a positive constant conditional on the endpoint event. Hence $\E Z\ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[One-sided spherical second moment]\label{asm:spherical-second-moment}
   For every fixed $-1<b<a<1$, the good-leaf count $Z$ from \cref{lem:good-first-moment} satisfies
   \[
      \E Z^2\le k^{O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
\end{assumption}

\begin{corollary}[One-sided spherical survival]\label{cor:spherical-survival}
   Under \cref{asm:spherical-second-moment}, if a query $q$ has a data point $p$ with $\ip{p}{q}\ge a$, then the continuation-mass decoder produces a common accepting leaf for $(p,q)$ with probability at least $\log^{-O_{a,b}(1)}m$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
   By \cref{lem:good-first-moment} and \cref{asm:spherical-second-moment}, Paley--Zygmund gives
   \[
      \Prb[Z>0]\ge \frac{(\E Z)^2}{\E Z^2}
      \ge k^{-O_{a,b}(1)}.
   \]
   Since $k=\Theta_{a,b}(\log m)$, this is the desired inverse-polylogarithmic survival probability. Correlation larger than $a$ can only improve the tilted endpoint exponent; the same constants cover a fixed compact neighborhood of the critical parameters after the usual slack adjustment.
\end{proof}

The expected-work and first-moment parts of the spherical primitive are therefore already polylogarithmic. The missing spherical part is \cref{asm:spherical-second-moment}: a lowest-common-ancestor second-moment calculation for good leaves using only a one-sided analytical barrier. No algorithmic two-sided tube is imposed.

\section{The certified score-and-ball process}

We now describe the Euclidean part as one branching process. A node of the process carries both a local Gaussian score state and a certified Euclidean ball. Gaussian score edges perform filtering inside the current ball; recentering edges are deterministic branches that move to a smaller certified ball and reset the local score state. Thus the geometric recursion is not a separate wrapper around the score tree.

\subsection{Two elementary geometric facts}

The first fact certifies the radius decrease obtained from a spherical cap.

\begin{lemma}[Cap recentering]\label{lem:cap}
   Let $P$ lie on the sphere of radius $R$ centered at $o$, and suppose
   \[
      \ip{(p-o)/R}{w}\ge\gamma
   \]
   for every $p$ in a cap $C\subseteq P$, where $\|w\|_2=1$. Then
   \[
      C\subseteq
      \Ball\!\left(o+\gamma Rw,\,R\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}\right).
   \]
   In particular, if $\gamma=\Theta_c(\eta)$, the certified child radius satisfies
   \[
      R_C\le R(1-\Theta_c(\eta^2)).
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   For $p\in C$,
   \begin{align*}
      \|p-(o+\gamma Rw)\|_2^2
                                           &=\|p-o\|_2^2+\gamma^2R^2-2\gamma R\ip{p-o}{w}\\
                                           &\le R^2+\gamma^2R^2-2\gamma^2R^2\\
                                           &=R^2(1-\gamma^2).
   \end{align*}
   The final estimate follows from $\sqrt{1-x}=1-\Theta(x)$ for sufficiently small positive $x$.
\end{proof}

The second fact shows that thin radial shells do not degrade the approximation factor.

\begin{lemma}[Angular gap on two shells]\label{lem:shell}
   Let data points have radius $S$ and let the query have radius $T$ around a common center. Put $\Delta=|S-T|$. If a data-query pair has Euclidean distance at most $r$, its squared angular chord distance is at most
   \[
      d_1^2=\frac{r^2-\Delta^2}{ST}.
   \]
   If its Euclidean distance is greater than $cr$, its squared angular chord distance is greater than
   \[
      d_2^2=\frac{c^2r^2-\Delta^2}{ST}.
   \]
   Whenever the near case is feasible,
   \[
      \frac{d_2^2}{d_1^2}\ge c^2.
   \]
   The same statements hold with $1+O_c(\eta)$ multiplicative slack for shells of relative width $O_c(\eta)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   For normalized directions $u=(p-o)/S$ and $v=(q-o)/T$,
   \[
      \|p-q\|_2^2=(S-T)^2+ST\|u-v\|_2^2.
   \]
   Solving for $\|u-v\|_2^2$ gives the first two formulas. Moreover,
   \[
      \frac{c^2r^2-\Delta^2}{r^2-\Delta^2}
      \ge c^2
   \]
   because $(c^2-1)\Delta^2\ge0$. Shell discretization perturbs $S$, $T$, and $\Delta$ by relative $O_c(\eta)$, which yields the final claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Posterior stopping}

Fix a geometric node containing $m$ points in a certified ball $\Ball(o,R)$. Let $\mu(u)$ be the probability that a uniformly random data point in the node reaches score prefix $u$, and let $\nu_q(u)$ be the corresponding probability for query $q$. For a deterministic child cluster $C$ of relative size $\alpha=|C|/m$, let $\mu_C(u)$ be the score-prefix law conditioned on the data point belonging to $C$. The posterior mass of $C$ at prefix $u$ is
\[
   Z_C(u)=\frac{\alpha\mu_C(u)}{\mu(u)}.
\]
Let $\mathcal A_C$ be the first-hit frontier on which $Z_C(u)\ge\tau$.

Put
\[
   \rho=\rho_c,
   \qquad
   s=1-\rho.
\]
The Bellman query mass assigned to prefix $u$ is
\[
   w_q(u)=\mu(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Posterior first-hit inequality]\label{lem:first-hit}
   For every prefix-free first-hit frontier $\mathcal A_C$,
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C} w_q(u)
      \le
      \left(\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\right)^s.
   \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
   On $\mathcal A_C$, the inequality $Z_C(u)\ge\tau$ gives
   \[
      \mu(u)\le \frac{\alpha}{\tau}\mu_C(u).
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho
      \le
      \left(\frac{\alpha}{\tau}\right)^s
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu_C(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho.
   \]
   Since $s+\rho=1$ and $\mathcal A_C$ is prefix-free, Hölder's inequality gives
   \[
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu_C(u)^s\nu_q(u)^\rho
      \le
      \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\mu_C(u)\right)^s
      \left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal A_C}\nu_q(u)\right)^\rho
      \le1.
   \]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Radius Bellman cancellation}

Use the recursive query potential
\[
   F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}.
\]
Suppose child $C$ has size $\alpha m$ and certified radius $R_C=\lambda R$. Activate it at posterior threshold
\[
   \tau=\lambda^2.
\]
Then the fractional cost of entering and recursively searching $C$ is exactly
\[
   \left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda^2}\right)^s
   F(\alpha m,\lambda R)
   =
   \alpha F(m,R).
\]
Thus disjoint activated children consume at most the parent potential, because $\sum_C\alpha_C\le1$. No cap geometry, balance condition, or bound on the number of children appears in this cancellation. Geometry enters only through the certified radii $R_C$.

The same idea controls space:
\[
   \sum_C |C|^{1+\rho}
   \le
   m^{1+\rho}
\]
for disjoint children. Polynomially many shells and bounded-diameter roots contribute only $\log^{O_c(1)}n$ factors when shell widths and distortions are inverse-polynomial in $\log n$.

\subsection{One cumulative score-and-ball tree}

The data structure is a deterministic certified ball hierarchy coupled to a cumulative Gaussian score tree.

At a state $u=(P_u,o_u,R_u)$, preprocessing proposes a disjoint partition of $P_u$ into certified children $C$ with centers $o_C$ and radii
\[
   R_C=\max_{p\in C}\|p-o_C\|_2.
\]
The partition may come from $k$-center, $k$-means, cap proposals, or any other routine; correctness uses only the certified radius. A child is geometrically useful when $R_C=\lambda_C R_u$ with $\lambda_C<1$ bounded away from one, and small or shallow residual sets are stored for resident scan.

Queries keep two populations on the same cumulative score tree.

\emph{Ordinary paths} use the continuation-mass decoder from \cref{lem:continuation-work}. They find isolated near points and residual leaves.

\emph{Detector cohorts} are used only to activate geometric children. A detector path votes for at most one child: the first child whose posterior reaches the radius threshold $\tau_C=\lambda_C^2$. Child $C$ is activated only after it receives
\[
   T_C=\log^{A_c}n\,|C|^\rho\left(\frac{R_C}{r}\right)^{2s}
\]
votes. The parent starts with
\[
   T_u=\log^{A_c}n\,|P_u|^\rho\left(\frac{R_u}{r}\right)^{2s}
\]
detector budget. Because each detector path casts at most one vote, activated children consume the existing parent budget rather than multiplying it.

When a query enters an activated child, the cumulative score prefix is retained. Recentered coordinates only change future score offsets; no new independent spherical decoder is started.

\begin{assumption}[Cohort-or-leaf]\label{asm:cohort-or-leaf}
   Let $C$ be the deterministic hierarchy child containing a true near neighbor $p$ of query $q$, and let $\tau_C=\lambda_C^2$ be its activation threshold. For the shared cumulative score tree, at least one of the following occurs with probability $\log^{-O_c(1)}n$:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item ordinary paths expose $p$ in a searchable residual leaf while $Z_C(u)<\tau_C$ along the route;
      \item detector paths generate enough first-hit votes to activate $C$.
   \end{enumerate}
   Equivalently, in the notation of the hand-off,
   \[
      \tau_C^{-\rho}\mathcal A_C^{\mathrm{hit}}
      +
      \mathcal L_C^{\mathrm{free}}
      \ge
      \log^{-O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Polylogarithmic score-and-ball process]\label{thm:polylog-process}
   Under \cref{asm:spherical-second-moment} and \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf}, the cumulative score-and-ball process has expected query time
   \[
      d\,n^{\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n
   \]
   and expected space
   \[
      dn+n^{1+\rho_c}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
   \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
   The continuation-mass score tree gives the ideal spherical query and storage exponents with only polylogarithmic overhead by \cref{lem:continuation-work} and \cref{cor:spherical-survival}. The shell lemma preserves the critical Euclidean angular gap up to inverse-polylogarithmic slack, which is absorbed into the displayed logarithmic factors.

   For every certified child, \cref{lem:first-hit} bounds the fractional query mass that can activate the child, and the choice $\tau_C=\lambda_C^2$ converts that activation cost into $\alpha_C F(m,R)$ by the Bellman cancellation. Summing over disjoint children is therefore bounded by the parent potential. The detector-vote rule is the integer version of this accounting: an activated child has already received its own recursive budget from parent detector votes. Space is controlled by disjointness and convexity of $x^{1+\rho}$.

   Along the near-neighbor route, \cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf} ensures inverse-polylogarithmic progress at each relevant scale: either the ordinary path finds the near point without activating the child, or detector cohorts activate the certified child containing it. The deterministic hierarchy has only $\log^{O_c(1)}n$ relevant scales after bounded-diameter localization and resident scans. Polylogarithmic amplification gives constant success probability, and truncation/rebuilding converts expected bounds to capped bounds as in the preliminaries.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the main theorem}

The conditional main theorem is exactly \cref{thm:polylog-process} with $\rho=\rho_c$. The assumptions named in \cref{thm:main} are precisely the two probabilistic inputs used in that theorem. Expected bounds are converted to capped bounds by the truncation paragraph in the preliminaries.

\section{The algorithm as one tree}

For clarity, we summarize the actual data structure as a single tree with two edge types.

\subsection{Preprocessing}

\begin{enumerate}
   \item Apply the usual bounded-diameter localization and build the associated polylogarithmic family of localized instances.
   \item Create a root state $(P,o,R)$ for each localized instance.
   \item At every nonterminal state, divide the resident points into radial shells of inverse-polylogarithmic relative width.
   \item Build a deterministic certified child partition for each shell. Certify every proposed child by explicitly computing
      \[
         R_C=\max_{p\in C}\|p-o_C\|_2.
      \]
   \item Store the activation threshold $\tau_C=(R_C/R_u)^2$ and vote budget
      \[
         T_C=\log^{A_c}n\,|C|^\rho\left(\frac{R_C}{r}\right)^{2(1-\rho)}.
      \]
   \item Build the cumulative Gaussian score tree with continuation-mass pruning. The same score prefixes support ordinary paths and detector cohorts.
   \item Stop once the node radius or node cardinality is small enough for a resident scan.
\end{enumerate}

Correctness does not depend on how a proposed child is found. A dense-cap routine, $k$-center, $k$-means, or a score-prefix detector may propose the subset and center; the proof uses only disjointness, posterior activation, and the certified radius.

\subsection{Query}

A query traverses the cumulative score tree. Ordinary paths use continuation-mass pruning and scan candidate buckets or resident blocks. Detector cohorts compute child posteriors along the same prefixes. A detector path votes for the first child whose posterior reaches $\tau_C$; the child is activated only when its vote budget is full. When a child activates, the query recenters the coordinates but retains the cumulative score prefix with the appropriate stored offset.

At all scan points, candidates are verified in the original metric. Therefore approximate clustering can affect performance but not correctness.

\subsection{Simplicity and implementation}

The filtering work remains level-synchronous. At one checkpoint, all active score paths can be advanced by a batched matrix multiplication, followed by continuation-mass pruning, posterior histogramming, detector-vote compaction, child activation, and resident scans. Recentering updates stored coordinate offsets; it does not start a fresh score tree.

The data structure stores only the tree metadata:
\[
   (\text{score seed},\text{cluster IDs},o_C,R_C,\tau_C,T_C,\text{resident block}).
\]
The same kernels support ordinary paths and detector cohorts; only their transition probabilities and counters differ.

\section{Why the compounded overhead disappears}

The restart analysis pays for the same loss once per geometric level. The cumulative process avoids that multiplication in two ways.

\paragraph{The score process is global.}
Continuation-mass pruning controls the total number of explored prefixes in one cumulative tree. Recentered children inherit the score prefix and only change the coordinate offset for future increments. Therefore there is no per-child independent spherical failure penalty.

\paragraph{Geometry is paid by Bellman cancellation.}
The child activation threshold $\tau_C=\lambda_C^2$ makes the radius decrease cancel the posterior stopping cost. The recursive potential of all activated children is at most the parent potential:
\[
   \sum_C
   \left(\frac{\alpha_C}{\lambda_C^2}\right)^s
   F(\alpha_Cm,\lambda_CR)
   \le
   F(m,R).
\]
This is the mechanism that removes dependence on the number of recenterings.

\paragraph{Integer materialization is isolated.}
The remaining issue is not an exponent calculation; it is the cohort-or-leaf probability. Ordinary paths must either find the near point before its geometric child becomes posterior-heavy, or detector cohorts must activate that child using the same path budget. This is the only unresolved place where the algorithm can still lose more than a polylogarithm.

\section{Proof obligations}

The draft now has two explicit probabilistic obligations.

\paragraph{One-sided spherical second moment.}
The expected work of continuation-mass pruning is already proved in \cref{lem:continuation-work}, and \cref{lem:good-first-moment} proves an inverse-polynomial first moment for good near leaves. What remains is to prove \cref{asm:spherical-second-moment}: the LCA second moment for those good leaves should be only polynomial in $k$. The intended proof uses a one-sided analytical upper barrier to control prolific early prefixes, while the algorithm itself enforces only continuation-mass pruning.

\paragraph{Cohort-or-leaf.}
\Cref{asm:cohort-or-leaf} should be tested against the hard cases: an isolated near point next to a massive far cluster, a near point on a cluster boundary, a tiny child that becomes heavy only late, and symmetric far shells. The proof should combine the posterior martingale, the one-sided continuation barrier, and concentration for detector votes.

\section{Conclusion}

The algorithmic target is a cumulative score-and-ball tree with query time
\[
   d\,n^{1/(2c^2-1)}\log^{O_c(1)}n
\]
and space
\[
   dn+n^{1+1/(2c^2-1)}\log^{O_c(1)}n.
\]
The exact pieces are already simple: continuation-mass pruning gives optimal expected spherical work, posterior first-hit gives the stopping inequality, and $\tau=\lambda^2$ gives exact radius Bellman cancellation. The central remaining task is the probabilistic cohort-or-leaf lemma that couples ordinary paths to detector cohorts without paying the exponent twice.

\section{More detail on the spherical exponent calculation}

For completeness, we verify the algebra leading to $\rho(a,b)$. Let $B$ be the branching factor and $\ell_B=\log B$. With
\[
   t^2=(1+a)\ell_B
\]
and
\[
   k=
   \frac{2(1+b)\log m}
   {(1+a)(1-b)\ell_B}
   +O(1),
\]
the expected number of accepting leaves for one point has logarithm
\begin{align*}
   k\ell_B-\frac{kt^2}{2}+O(\log k)
                                                                                                                  &=
                                                                                                                  \frac{k\ell_B(1-a)}{2}+O(\log k)\\
                                                                                                                                                                                                 &=
                                                                                                                                                                                                 \frac{(1-a)(1+b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}\log m+O(\log\log m).
\end{align*}
Thus it equals
\[
   m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O(1)}m.
\]

For a pair of correlation at most $b$, the expected number of common accepting leaves has logarithm at most
\begin{align*}
   k\ell_B-\frac{kt^2}{1+b}
                                                                                                                  &=
                                                                                                                  k\ell_B\left(1-\frac{1+a}{1+b}\right)\\
                                                                                                                                                                                                 &=-\frac{k\ell_B(a-b)}{1+b}\\
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           &=-\frac{2(a-b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}\log m+O(1).
\end{align*}
A direct calculation shows
\[
   1-\rho(a,b)
   =
   \frac{2(a-b)}{(1+a)(1-b)}.
\]
Hence one ordinary far point contributes at most
\[
   m^{-(1-\rho(a,b))}\log^{O(1)}m
\]
common leaves, and all $m$ ordinary far points contribute at most
\[
   m^{\rho(a,b)}\log^{O(1)}m.
\]

\section{Bellman cancellation algebra}

We record the cancellation with no surrounding notation. Let $s=1-\rho$, let a parent contain $m$ points in radius $R$, and let a child have relative size $\alpha$ and relative radius $\lambda$. With
\[
   F(m,R)=m^\rho\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}
\]
and activation threshold $\tau=\lambda^2$, the first-hit bound charges at most $(\alpha/\lambda^2)^s$ query mass to the child. The recursive charge is therefore
\begin{align*}
   \left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda^2}\right)^s
   F(\alpha m,\lambda R)
   &=
   \alpha^s\lambda^{-2s}
   \alpha^\rho m^\rho
   \lambda^{2s}\left(\frac Rr\right)^{2s}\\
   &=
   \alpha F(m,R).
\end{align*}
Thus the radius improvement pays exactly for the posterior threshold, and disjoint children sum to at most the parent potential.
